# CBT-informed, SEL, emotion regulation, social/communication skills and healthy thinking for ages 2-10, and how to build them into a family learning game

Context: an Israeli family with a girl aged 8.5 (grade 3, כיתה ג'), a boy aged 5.5 (גן חובה) and a toddler aged 2.5. The parent wants the kids to think clearly, communicate well, regulate emotions, persist, step out of their comfort zone and pick the right tools for a problem. Notes are current to Nov 2026. Items marked **[not verified this session]** come from well-established literature I know, but I did not re-fetch a source for them in this session. The report writer should treat them as lower-confidence or check them.

---

## 1. CBT for children: core concepts adapted for kids, evidence-based programs, and digital CBT

### Takeaway
CBT is the best-supported psychological treatment for childhood anxiety: about 1 extra child in 3 reaches remission compared with waitlist, and individual, group and parent formats work about equally well. Its kid-friendly building blocks (feelings-thoughts-actions, "detective thinking", coping self-talk, graded bravery steps, problem-solving steps) can be lifted into a game as *universal skill practice*. Games built specifically to deliver CBT (MindLight, RAGE-Control/Mightier) have small or non-superior trial results, so an app should claim "practice of skills" and not "treatment".

### Cited Findings
- **Cochrane 2020 (James, Reardon, Soler, James & Creswell, CD013162):** 41 studies, 1,806 participants with mild-to-moderate anxiety. Remission of any anxiety diagnosis vs waitlist: OR 7.85 (95% CI 5.31-11.60), NNT 3.0 (26 ITT studies, n=1,350). No difference between individual, group and family/parental formats. CBT was **not** more effective than non-CBT *active* controls (6 studies, n=426) or treatment-as-usual (2 studies, n=88). The CI reported for the NNT in snippets looks garbled ("1.75 to 3.03"), so check the full text before quoting it. — [Cochrane CD013162](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD013162.pub2/information/fa); earlier 2005 version: 56% remission CBT vs 28.2% control, NNT 3.0 — [Cochrane CD004690.pub2](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD004690.pub2)
- **Fun FRIENDS (ages 4-7), Pahl & Barrett 2010, N=263:** at post-intervention, intervention and waitlist groups improved *equally* on parent-reported anxiety. At the 12-month follow-up the Fun FRIENDS group showed gains in anxiety and social-emotional strength, and girls also improved in behavioural inhibition. A larger cluster trial (Anticich et al. 2013, N=488, 14 Brisbane Catholic schools, ages 4-7) exists, but I could not retrieve its outcomes. There is also an open (non-randomized) clinical trial in 5-7-year-olds with anxiety disorders (Barrett, Fisak & Cooper 2015, N=31). — [Foundations (UK) Guidebook: Fun FRIENDS](https://foundations.org.uk/toolkit/guidebook/fun-friends/); [informahealthcare, Pahl & Barrett 2010](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/1754730X.2010.9715683)
- **Resilience program meta-analysis:** a PMC-hosted systematic review and meta-analysis of resilience programs for children and adolescents exists (it includes FRIENDS-type programs). I did not extract its numbers. — [PMC8645691](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8645691/)
- **BRAVE-Online (internet CBT, ages 7-18; child version 7-12):** the developers' overview of 3 RCTs says it is superior to waitlist and has efficacy similar to clinic-based therapy. Spence et al. (JCPP 2020) was an open trial (n=175, ages 7-18 plus a parent, minimal therapist support): most completed it and showed strong anxiety reductions. Both sources are developer-led. — [USQ overview](https://research.usq.edu.au/item/q1x5v/online-therapy-for-youth-anxiety-works-an-overview-of-the-evidence-for-brave-online-and-predictors-of-therapy-outcome); [ACAMH digest](https://www.acamh.org/research-digest/brave-online-strong-reduction-in-anxiety-for-young-people/); [BeYou directory, BRAVE self-help](https://beyou.edu.au/resources/programs-directory/b/brave-self-help-program)
- **MindLight (neurofeedback game, Granic lab / Radboud):**
  - Schoneveld et al. 2016 RCT: 136 children aged 7-13 with elevated anxiety (screened from 750+). Anxiety fell in both MindLight and control-game groups, and the improvement did **not** differ between conditions. — [RUG research portal](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/a-neurofeedback-video-game-mindlight-to-prevent-anxiety-in-childr/)
  - Non-inferiority trial vs CBT: 174 children aged 7-12. Its design is documented, but I could not retrieve the outcome numbers. — [Springer 10.1007/s10862-018-9684-4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10862-018-9684-4); [PMC6223766](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6223766)
  - Autism RCT (Wijnhoven 2020, n=109, ages 8-16): no difference on child-rated anxiety, with some parent-rated benefit. — [PMC5801383](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5801383)
  - Wols et al. 2018 (n=43): changes in in-game behaviours that represent *exposure* predicted anxiety improvement 3 months later. — [BSL](https://thim.mijn.bsl.nl/in-game-play-behaviours-during-an-applied-video-game-for-anxiety/15838096)
- **RAGE-Control (the predecessor of Mightier):** a pilot double-blind RCT of 40 youth aged 10-17 with anger dyscontrol, comparing 10 sessions of ACT plus the biofeedback game against ACT plus a sham game. Self-reported anger fell similarly in both groups. Parents reported greater improvement in aggression and oppositional behaviour in the biofeedback group, and a larger heart-rate decrease went with more improvement. The authors call it a small proof of concept. Boston Children's owns the technology, and co-authors hold equity in Neuromotion (the maker of Mightier). — [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20211005/Video-game-with-biofeedback-trains-kids-to-stay-calm-during-stressful-or-frustrating-situations.aspx); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/930620); [Frontiers in Psychiatry 2021 Mightier paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2021.591906/pdf)
- **Breathe, Think, Do with Sesame (ages 2-5, free, English and Spanish):** marketed as "research-based" and built around a 3-step strategy (breathe, think of a plan, do it) with a monster character. I found **no** published outcome evaluation. Common Sense notes the belly-breathing and bubble-popping segments run long for young attention spans. — [Common Sense Education](https://www.commonsense.org/education/app/breathe-think-do-with-sesame); [Horn Book review](https://www.hbook.com/story/breathe-app-review); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id721853597)
- **Core kid-CBT techniques [not verified this session]:**
  - Coping Cat (Kendall) teaches the **FEAR** plan: Feeling frightened? / Expecting bad things? / Attitudes and Actions that help / Results and Rewards. It was one arm of the CAMS trial (Walkup et al. 2008, NEJM), where CBT, sertraline and the combination all beat placebo for anxiety in ages 7-17 and the combination did best. — [Walkup 2008, NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa0804633)
  - FRIENDS (Barrett) is often described as WHO-recognised.
  - SPARX (fantasy game CBT for adolescents with depressive symptoms) was non-inferior to usual care in an RCT. — [Merry et al. 2012, BMJ](https://www.bmj.com/content/344/bmj.e2598)

### Inferences
**Kid-adapted CBT building blocks, mapped to this family's three children:**

| Concept | Toddler 2.5 | Boy 5.5 (gan chova) | Girl 8.5 (grade 3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feelings-thoughts-actions triangle | Name the body feeling only ("hot tummy") | 2-node version: "feeling to action" with picture cards | Full triangle: "what did I *think* → how did I *feel* → what did I *do*" |
| Thinking traps (cognitive distortions) | Not appropriate | 2-3 traps as characters: "Never-Ever Nudnik" (overgeneralising), "Catastrophe Cloud" | 5-6 traps: all-or-nothing, fortune-telling, mind-reading, catastrophising, "it's all my fault", labelling |
| Detective thinking | Not appropriate | "Is that really true? Let's look for a clue" | Evidence for/against, plus "What would I tell a friend?" |
| Coping self-talk | One line from a parent ("I can take a breath") | Character-modelled sentences picked from a menu | Writes or records her own "brave sentence" |
| Problem-solving steps | Choose between 2 options | 3 steps (Sesame-like: breathe, plan, do) | 5 steps: Stop → name the problem → brainstorm 3 ideas → choose (consequences) → try and review |
| Graded exposure (bravery ladder) | Not applicable (parent-led only) | 3-step ladder, parent co-designed | 5-8-rung ladder with self-chosen rewards |

- The Cochrane finding that CBT beats waitlist but *not* active controls suggests the active ingredients are partly non-specific: attention, practice and structure. A universal game can reasonably target these as skill-building, not as treatment of a disorder.
- MindLight's exposure-behaviour finding (Wols 2018) is the strongest design lesson: **in-game approach behaviours (facing the scary thing step by step) are the mechanism to reward**. Calm-only mechanics are less supported.
- Biofeedback (Mightier) needs hardware and has developer-conflicted pilot evidence. A camera or microphone breath detector could imitate it, but it should not be marketed as therapeutic.
- Dialogue examples (Hebrew-first, with English):
  - Detective character "בלש המחשבות דובי": "רגע! זו מחשבה או עובדה? בואי נחפש רמזים." ("Wait! Is that a thought or a fact? Let's look for clues.")
  - Thinking-trap character "ענן האסון": "אם טעיתי בתרגיל אחד, אני גרועה בחשבון!" Child response options: "זו מלכודת 'הכל או כלום'. טעיתי בתרגיל אחד, ופתרתי נכון חמישה."
  - FEAR-style coping card for the 5.5-year-old: "הבטן מרגישה פרפרים → מה אני חושב שיקרה? → מה יעזור לי? (נשימת דרקון / לבקש עזרה) → כל הכבוד, ניסיתי!"

### Gaps
- Outcome numbers for the MindLight vs CBT non-inferiority trial. As I recall [not verified], it reported MindLight as non-inferior with both conditions reducing anxiety.
- The Anticich 2013 Fun FRIENDS cluster RCT outcomes.
- Evidence for Headspace for Kids and Moshi: I found no peer-reviewed outcome trials for children under 10 in this session, so treat both as unevaluated.
- No RCT found of CBT-based *universal* digital games for 4-7-year-olds.

## 2. SEL frameworks and programs with evidence (CASEL, meta-analyses, RULER, Second Step, PATHS, Zones, Tools of the Mind, Incredible Years, mindfulness)

### Takeaway
Universal SEL has robust meta-analytic support: about 11 percentile points on achievement in Durlak 2011, and broad gains in skills, behaviour, climate and achievement across 424 studies in Cipriano 2023. Effects are strongest when programs are SAFE (Sequenced, Active, Focused, Explicit). Branded programs vary a lot, though. PATHS and Tools of the Mind have null independent replications, Zones of Regulation is not established as evidence-based, and the large MYRIAD school-mindfulness trial found no benefit to students. Design the game around **SAFE principles and the CASEL 5**, not around any single brand.

### Cited Findings
- **Durlak et al. 2011 (Child Development):** 213 school-based universal SEL programs, 270,034 K-12 students, studies from 1955 to 2007, all with comparison groups and nearly half randomized. Achievement was **11 percentile points** higher than in controls. — [CASEL-hosted PDF](https://casel.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/meta-analysis-child-development-1.pdf); [OJP abstract](https://ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/impact-enhancing-students-social-and-emotional-learning-meta)
- **Taylor et al. 2017 follow-up:** students who had SEL programs scored on average 13 percentile points higher academically at long-term follow-up, but only 8 studies measured academics. — [CASEL 2017 summary](https://casel.org/2017-meta-analysis-summary/?view=true)
- **Cipriano et al. 2023 (Child Development 94(5):1181-1204):** 424 studies, 53 countries, 252 interventions, 575,361 K-12 students, 2008-2020. Participants improved on skills, attitudes, behaviours, school climate and safety, peer relationships, school functioning and academic achievement. Significant heterogeneity: **SAFE** programs taught by teachers showed the strongest effects. The press summary says gains persisted 6+ months. — [OUP/Child Development](https://academic.oup.com/chidev/article-abstract/94/5/1181/8255238); [MedicalXpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2023-07-social-emotional-significant-benefits-students.html)
- **RULER (Yale; Mood Meter, emotion vocabulary):** a cluster RCT (Rivers, Brackett et al. 2013, Prevention Science) in about 62 schools (another source says 64) with 5th and 6th graders. RULER classrooms had higher warmth and connectedness, more student autonomy and leadership, and more student-centred learning. An IDB summary notes no significant effect on *negative* classroom climate. — [Springer 10.1007/s11121-012-0305-2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11121-012-0305-2); [IDB evidence platform](https://plataformadeevidencias.iadb.org/en/casos-avaliados/ruler-approach); [Hagelskamp et al., RULER site](https://www.rulerapproach.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/pub328_Hagelskamp_etal_InPress.pdf)
- **PATHS:**
  - The large independent English trial (n=5,218, Years 3-5) found only tentative (p<0.10), very small improvements in social skills and peer support. — [Humphrey et al. 2018, NIHR Public Health Research](https://doaj.org/article/bb414edaa2364653851d9251f79a4149)
  - A review notes that positive trials were mostly developer-led and that independent trials in the USA, Switzerland and England were largely null. — [Turner et al., PMC7085485](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7085485)
  - WWC listing for Humphrey 2016, a cluster RCT with about 1,582 pupils. — [WWC](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/Study/87897)
- **Zones of Regulation:** independent reviews conclude it is **not established as evidence-based**.
  - Mason, Leaf & Gerhardt 2024 (J. Special Education) found only 2 experimental studies from 2011-2021. — [ASAT synopsis](https://asatonline.org/research-treatment/research-synopses/review-of-the-zones-of-regulation-program/)
  - A UniSA review found the evidence inconclusive and mostly at moderate-to-high risk of bias. — [UniSA](https://ojs.unisa.edu.au/index.php/tahs/article/view/1595)
  - The developer claims evidence; RCTs are said to be underway. — [Zones brief](https://zonesofregulation.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Evidence-of-Effectiveness-Brief.pdf)
- **Tools of the Mind (play-based executive function):**
  - Blair & Raver 2014: 759 kindergarteners in 29 Massachusetts schools, with gains in math, reading, vocabulary and executive function. — [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/11/141112144722.htm)
  - Nesbitt & Farran 2021, a Tennessee pre-K trial with 32 Tools vs 28 control classrooms, found **no** advantage on academics, executive function or self-regulation. — [SRCD Monograph Matters](https://monographmatters.srcd.org/?p=2227); [Stanford DREME](https://dreme.stanford.edu/?p=1727)
- **Mindfulness, MYRIAD (UK, 85 schools, ages 11-16):** the headline student result is that teacher-delivered school mindfulness did not improve students' mental health or wellbeing. Teacher burnout and climate effects were small and faded by 1 year. No serious adverse events were reported. — [ISRCTN86619085](https://www.isrctn.com/ISRCTN86619085); [Mindful.org commentary](https://www.mindful.org/?p=62492); [UT Southwestern/Elsevier record](https://utsouthwestern.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/effectiveness-of-universal-school-based-mindfulness-training-comp)
- **[not verified this session]**
  - CASEL 5 competencies: self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, responsible decision-making. — [CASEL fundamentals](https://casel.org/fundamentals-of-sel/)
  - Second Step (Committee for Children) and Incredible Years Dinosaur School (Webster-Stratton, ages 4-8, puppet-based) are widely used. Dinosaur School has several RCTs in clinic-referred conduct-problem samples. I did not re-verify the effect sizes this session. — [Second Step](https://www.secondstep.org); [Incredible Years](https://www.incredibleyears.com)

### Inferences
- **Translate SAFE into game rules.**
  - *Sequenced*: a fixed skill path, for example name feelings, then calm down, then think it through, then talk it out, then be brave.
  - *Active*: role-play and real-life missions, not just watching.
  - *Focused*: regular short sessions, 5-10 minutes, two or three times a week.
  - *Explicit*: each episode names its skill ("today's tool: the I-message").
- **RULER-style Mood Meter** is the best-supported vocabulary tool to borrow conceptually. Use a 2x2 grid of energy (high/low) by pleasantness, in colours. Toddler: 2 colours (happy/sad, faces). 5.5-year-old: 4 quadrants with 2 words each. 8.5-year-old: 4 quadrants with about 8-12 words each, granular ("מתוסכלת", "מאוכזבת", "נלהבת", "רגועה"). The quadrant idea is generic, but avoid copying RULER's trademarked branding.
- **Zones-style colour coding** is fine as a visual metaphor, but don't present it as evidence-based. Avoid implying that a "red" feeling is "bad": all feelings are OK, and actions are what get chosen.
- Teacher delivery is the strongest moderator in Cipriano. The app analogue is **parent co-delivery**: a 2-minute parent card per episode ("ask her tonight: what was your brave thing?").
- Don't rely on mindfulness meditation as a core pillar for this age group. Use breathing and body-awareness *micro-tools* framed as calming tools, not as an intervention promising wellbeing.

### Gaps
- Exact effect sizes in Cipriano 2023 (full text not accessed).
- Second Step independent RCT results (Low et al. 2015) were not retrieved.
- Evidence specific to ages 2-4 is much thinner than for K-12. Most of these meta-analyses start at kindergarten.

## 3. Emotion regulation development (2-10), emotion coaching, affect labeling, co-regulation, breathing, and frustration-safe design

### Takeaway
Emotion regulation shifts from mostly *co-regulation by caregivers* (toddlers) to growing self-regulation using language and strategies (5-10). Naming feelings ("name it to tame it") has adult neuroimaging support and correlational child support, not causal child trials. Slow breathing calms young children's physiology in the short term, but its effects on anxiety and mood are mixed. Build regulation tools in, and design so that frustration inside the game itself is scaffolded rather than punished.

### Cited Findings
- **Affect labeling:**
  - Lieberman et al. 2007 (Psychological Science, fMRI, adults): labeling the emotion in a negative image reduced amygdala and limbic response and increased right ventrolateral PFC activity. — [PubMed 17576282](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17576282/); [PDF](https://sites.lifesci.ucla.edu/psych-sanlab/wp-content/uploads/sites/31/2016/03/Lieberman_2007_PsychScience.pdf)
  - Child evidence is mostly correlational: preschoolers' language and labeling ability goes with better self-control and less time appearing angry. People also tend to *underestimate* labeling as a strategy, so it works as an "incidental" regulator. — [eScholarship review](https://escholarship.org/content/qt6n48b650/qt6n48b650.pdf?t=qaeiqw); [PMC3444304](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3444304)
- **Breathing:**
  - A Stanford field experiment randomized 342 children (mean age about 7) at museums, playgrounds and camps to a short animated breathing video, and found significant effects on stress *physiology*. This measured arousal, not anxiety. — [Greater Good, "How four deep breaths can help kids calm down"](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/article/item/how_four_deep_breaths_can_help_kids_calm_down)
  - A micro-randomized trial (n=171, ages 9-13, 15 days) found video-guided slow diaphragmatic breathing had **no immediate effect** on negative affect or relaxation vs controls. It helped relaxation when worry was higher than usual. — [PubMed 35704507](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35704507/)
- **Biofeedback-based regulation in a game (RAGE-Control):** greater heart-rate decreases tracked behaviour improvements. — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/930620)
- **Sibling emotion regulation (Kennedy & Kramer 2008, 95 families, siblings aged 4-8):** gains in children's emotion regulation were linked with better sibling relationship quality after the program. — [Kennedy & Kramer 2008 PDF](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.northeastern.edu/dist/d/3128/files/2023/02/Kennedy-Kramer-2008.pdf)
- **[not verified this session]**
  - Gottman's emotion coaching (Gottman, Katz & Hooven 1996) has five steps: notice the emotion, see it as a chance to connect, listen and validate, help name it, set limits while problem-solving. Parental "meta-emotion philosophy" predicted children's later regulation and peer outcomes. — [J. Family Psychology 10.1037/0893-3200.10.3.243](https://doi.org/10.1037/0893-3200.10.3.243)
  - "Name it to tame it" is a phrase from Siegel & Bryson's *The Whole-Brain Child*, a popular book and not a trial.

### Inferences
**Developmental tiers:**
- *Toddler (2.5):* regulation is largely borrowed from the adult. Design only **co-play** content: a parent-held "calm-down corner" with a breathing animal (blow out birthday candles, smell the flower). One-word emotion labels with faces (שמח/עצוב/כועס/מפחד). No solo timed challenges, failure states or loss of items.
- *5.5:* can use a picked strategy with prompting. Offer a "toolbox" of 4-5 strategies (dragon breath, squeeze-and-release, count to 5, ask for a hug or help, take a break). Choosing comes after naming.
- *8.5:* can reflect and plan ahead ("next time I feel X, I will Y"), granular vocabulary, and reappraisal (detective thinking) after calming down. Not during the peak.

**Breathing mini-game ("dragon fire," נשימת דרקון):**
- Inhale slowly (the dragon's belly fills, about 3-4 s), exhale long to make a steady, gentle flame that warms a frozen egg. A *steady* exhale is rewarded, not a forceful one.
- 3-5 breaths maximum. Common Sense found longer segments lose young kids.
- Optional on-device mic detection, with no audio stored.
- Frame it honestly as "a tool that helps your body calm down", not "makes worry go away".

**Frustration and tantrum-safe design rules:**
- No loss of progress on failure.
- Hints appear after 2 failed tries; "try a different tool" is offered after 3.
- An always-visible "pause / I need a break" button with no penalty.
- Rewards for *effort and strategy use* ("you tried a new way!"), not speed.
- No countdown timers for under-6s.
- Sessions end on a success or a calm moment ("land the plane").
- The character models its own frustration first ("אוף, זה קשה לי! אני אקח נשימת דרקון ואנסה שוב").

**Emotion-coaching parent cards** (Gottman-style, unverified source): "Notice → Name → Validate → Limit → Solve." Example: "את ממש מתוסכלת שהמגדל נפל. זה מתסכל! אסור לזרוק קוביות. מה אפשר לעשות במקום?"

### Gaps
- No rigorous trial of belly breathing for anxiety in preschoolers was found.
- No causal child study of affect labeling's neural effects was found.
- A 2022 school HRV-biofeedback study (n≈585, ages 7-12) reportedly found reduced anxiety and stress. I couldn't confirm its exact citation, and it used non-individual assignment.

## 4. Social skills and communication: perspective-taking, conflict resolution, assertiveness, listening, prosocial play, collaboration, presenting, sibling conflict

### Takeaway
Prosocial game content predicts later prosocial behaviour across correlational, longitudinal and experimental studies (Gentile 2009). Sibling-relationship programs (Kramer's More Fun With Sisters and Brothers) improve sibling warmth and emotion regulation in ages 4-8, though the evidence is mostly developer-led. A family game with three kids is a natural place for structured "team talk" conflict resolution and cooperative (not competitive) mechanics.

### Cited Findings
- **Gentile et al. 2009 (PSPB 35(6):752-763):** 3 studies. (1) Correlational, Singapore secondary students (about 727). (2) Longitudinal, Japanese children and adolescents (about 2,000, ages 10-16, 3-4 month follow-up): prosocial game play predicted later increases in prosocial behaviour. (3) An experiment with 161 US college students playing prosocial, neutral or violent games. Note that the samples are mostly older than this family's children. — [VU research portal abstract](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/the-effects-of-prosocial-video-games-on-prosocial-behaviors-inter/); [Iowa State news](https://news.iastate.edu/news/2009/apr/vgbenefits.shtml); [U-Michigan news](https://news.umich.edu/?p=7200)
- **More Fun With Sisters and Brothers (Kramer, Univ. of Illinois / Northeastern), siblings aged 4-8:**
  - The program teaches emotion regulation and social skills to the siblings together. Home observations before and after showed improved relationship quality, and parents intervened less. — [Kennedy & Kramer 2008](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.northeastern.edu/dist/d/3128/files/2023/02/Kennedy-Kramer-2008.pdf); [Northeastern news](https://bouve.northeastern.edu/news/getting-along-with-your-siblings-can-be-taught-northeastern-research-shows/)
  - Ravindran et al. 2015: parents in the program reported less intense negative emotion during their children's fights. — [ScienceDaily 2015](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2015/06/150625161514.htm); [U of I ACES](https://aces.illinois.edu/node/2204)
  - An online 4-lesson version (86 mothers) reported improvements in sibling relationship quality, child and parent emotion regulation, and co-parenting. Sources are mostly the research team's own. — [Northeastern sibling research group](https://siblingresearchgroup.sites.northeastern.edu/research); [Northeastern news 2024](https://news.northeastern.edu/2024/12/18/sibling-conflict-research)
- **Siblings Without Rivalry (Faber & Mazlish)** is a popular parenting book. It is not trial-tested as a program. **[not verified this session]**

### Inferences
**Sibling "team talk" (שיחת צוות) conflict-resolution protocol (sequence adapted from common SEL practice; Kramer-style emotion focus):**
1. Stop and calm: everyone takes a dragon breath.
2. Each sibling says the feeling with an I-message ("אני מרגישה ___ כש___ כי ___").
3. The other repeats back (active listening: "שמעתי שאת...").
4. Brainstorm 2-3 solutions together.
5. Pick a win-win, then high-five.
- The 8.5-year-old can lead as "team captain", which builds leadership, but rotate roles so it doesn't become bossing. The 5.5-year-old uses picture prompts. The toddler participates as the "helper" (hands over the talking stick).

**Cooperative mechanics:**
- Shared quests that need each child's distinct ability: the big sister reads clues, the brother finds shapes or counts, the toddler presses the big button or picks colours. This reduces competition among three siblings with very different skill levels.
- No sibling leaderboards. Team stars only.

**Perspective-taking:**
- "Whose shoes?" story dilemmas: the same event told from two characters' viewpoints. The child guesses what each one feels or knows. False-belief-style hidden-object stories suit the 5.5-year-old.
- "What would your friend think?" choice scenes for the 8.5-year-old.

**Assertive communication:**
- Three-voice game: "mouse voice" (passive), "monster voice" (aggressive), "lion voice" (assertive, calm and clear). The child picks the lion line for a scenario, for example someone cutting in line at the playground or a classmate taking an eraser.

**Public speaking (ages 5.5 and 8.5):**
- "Show & Tell studio": record a 30-60 s explanation of something they built in-game, play it to a parent.
- A graded ladder: talk to the character, then to a parent, then a video call with a grandparent, then a class presentation. This doubles as a bravery ladder.

**Real-life kindness missions (משימות חסד):**
- Weekly, parent-verified: help set the table, make a card for a grandparent, invite a lonely kid to play.
- Reward with a *shared* family garden or tree that grows, not individual currency, to avoid extrinsic over-justification.

**Dialogue example (team talk prompt by the mediator character "ינשוף השלום"):** "אני רואה ששניכם רוצים את אותו כדור. בואו כל אחד יגיד מה הוא מרגיש, ואחר כך נחפש רעיון שטוב לשניכם."

### Gaps
- No trial found of prosocial *games* for children under 8 specifically.
- No independent replication of More Fun With Sisters and Brothers was found.
- No evidence was retrieved on theory-of-mind training *apps*. The theory-of-mind training literature (e.g., story-based conversation training) was not searched this session.

## 5. Resilience and courage: Israeli programs, graded challenge and bravery ladders, growth mindset, self-compassion, optimism training

### Takeaway
Israel has a strong, locally researched base of school resilience programs: ERASE-Stress and related ones (Gelkopf & Berger, NATAL/ICTP-linked researchers), plus the Ministry of Education's core כישורי חיים (Life Skills) SEL program. Their language and framing can be echoed for cultural fit. Outside Israel, the evidence is modest. Penn Resiliency shows small effects (d≈0.11-0.21), and growth-mindset intervention effects are small and contested. Graded exposure (bravery ladders) is the best-supported mechanism for courage.

### Cited Findings
- **ERASE-Stress (Gelkopf & Berger 2009, JCPP 50:962-971):** a universal, teacher-delivered program of 12 × 90-minute sessions covering psychoeducation, skills and resilience strategies. 114 7th-8th graders in southern Israel were quasi-randomized to the program or waitlist, and it significantly reduced posttraumatic symptoms, depression, somatic complaints and functional problems at 3 months. Earlier work by the same group: an elementary-school terror-distress program (J. Traumatic Stress 2007) and a tsunami-distress school program (Psychother. Psychosom. 2009). — [BGU research portal](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/a-school-based-teacher-mediated-prevention-program-erase-stress-f/); [OJP abstract](https://ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/school-based-teacher-mediated-prevention-program-erase-stress)
- **School interventions for war-affected children:** there is a review of whether these are effective and harmless. Relevant given Israeli children's exposure since Oct 2023. — [Uni Bielefeld record](https://pub.uni-bielefeld.de/record/2681730)
- **Israel MoE שפ"י Life Skills (כישורי חיים):**
  - A core program developing social, emotional and values competence, described as a "basic condition for learning". — [MoE rationale, primary-school Life Skills](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/shefi/kishurey_haim_yesodi_new/Kishurey_Haim_Yesodi/Rachyonal.pdf)
  - It implements standard 3 of the school climate standards, "SEL". — [MoE Life Skills guide](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/shefi/gilrach/kishurei_haim_gilrach.pdf)
  - It covers intrapersonal, interpersonal and social skills, with an early-childhood version integrated across kindergarten activity. — [Kishurey Chaim 2022](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/shefi/kishurey_chayim/Kishurey_Chaim_Mathim_Bimyuchad2022.pdf); [MoE SEL framework](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/GAP/RigshitJevratitMinit.pdf)
- **Penn Resiliency Program:**
  - Brunwasser, Gillham & Kim 2009 meta-analysis (17 controlled evaluations, n=2,498): lower depressive symptoms vs controls at post-test and follow-up, effect sizes d≈0.11-0.21, lasting at least 12 months. Limited evidence that it beats *active* controls. — [Meta-analysis PDF](https://cns.org/Assets/72cf17ad-f311-4731-8fcf-2f5d8f6dfd04/637002585729470000/brunwasser-gillham-kim-2009-meta-analysis-of-prp-pdf); [Swarthmore](https://works.swarthmore.edu/fac-psychology/73)
  - A 2018 pooled analysis of 3 RCTs (n=1,145): baseline symptom severity moderated effects, but not reliably. — [PMC4667774](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4667774)
- **Growth mindset:**
  - Sisk et al. 2018 (Psych Science): the intervention meta-analysis (43 studies, n=57,155) found weak overall effects on achievement. — [Sisk 2018 PDF](https://hhs.purdue.edu/skill-learning-and-performance-lab/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2024/08/sisk-et-al-2018-to-what-extent-and-under-which-circumstances-are-growth-mind-sets-important-to-academic-achievement-two.pdf); [EdSurge](https://edsurge.com/news/2018-05-29-believe-and-you-can-achieve-researchers-find-limited-gains-from-growth-mindset-interventions)
  - Macnamara & Burgoyne 2023 (Psych Bulletin): apparent effects are likely inflated by inadequate study designs, with no significant moderators. — [Macnamara 2023 PDF](https://hhs.purdue.edu/skill-learning-and-performance-lab/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2024/08/2023-90931-004.pdf)
  - Contested by Burnette et al. 2023, which found effects in focal or at-risk groups. — [MDPI commentary](https://mdpi.com/2079-3200/11/6/104)
- **Exposure as mechanism:** in MindLight, in-game exposure behaviours predicted anxiety reduction. — [BSL](https://thim.mijn.bsl.nl/in-game-play-behaviours-during-an-applied-video-game-for-anxiety/15838096)
- **[not verified this session]**
  - Israeli resilience organizations: NATAL (Israel Trauma and Resiliency Center) and ICTP/Metiv (Israel Psychotrauma Center, Herzog) run school resilience trainings. The ICTP "Building Resilience" teacher-delivered program has been rolled out widely in Israeli schools, but I couldn't confirm a specific trial this session.
  - The "MASHABIM" coping program could not be located.
  - Self-compassion research in children under 10 is sparse. Most self-compassion intervention trials are in adolescents.

### Inferences
**Bravery ladder (סולם האומץ) mechanic:**
- The child (with a parent) picks one goal and breaks it into 3-8 rungs, from slightly uncomfortable to target. Example for the 8.5-year-old: "speak in class". Example for the 5.5-year-old: "order ice cream by myself".
- Each rung is a *real-world* mission, verified by the parent.
- The in-game avatar climbs a tower or mountain. The reward is for **trying**, not for "no fear". Fear ratings (a 0-3 thermometer) before and after show that "the fear got smaller".
- This is the core of exposure, framed as a universal comfort-zone stretch. A parent must stop it if fear is extreme (see guardrails).

**"Mistake celebration" ritual (חגיגת טעויות):**
- When a puzzle fails, the character says "!טעות מעולה! מה למדנו?" and the child earns a "learning gem".
- This targets the *behavioural* elements (persistence, strategy-switching) that are more defensible than mindset messaging. Praise *process and strategy* ("ניסית דרך חדשה") rather than "you're smart"; process praise is a common recommendation, and this note does not establish its evidence base.
- Don't oversell growth mindset to the parent as a proven achievement booster.

**"Right tool for the problem" toolbox (ארגז הכלים):**
- This matches the parent's goal. Each skill learned becomes a collectable tool card: Dragon Breath, Detective Glass, I-Message Megaphone, Brave Ladder, Brainstorm Cloud, Ask-for-Help Bell.
- Scenarios require *choosing* the fitting tool, which builds metacognition and flexible strategy selection.
- For the 8.5-year-old, add "which tool worked best? why?" reflection.

**Cultural fit:**
- Use the Hebrew terminology of the MoE Life Skills program (מודעות עצמית, ויסות רגשי, קבלת החלטות אחראית, תקשורת בין-אישית) so the parent and school language align.
- Include gentle, *non-graphic* "when sirens / loud noises happen" coping content only as optional, parent-activated material, not default gameplay. Many Israeli kids have experienced shelters since 2023; ERASE-Stress-type psychoeducation is a precedent, but trauma content needs professional review.

**Optimism / explanatory style (Penn Resiliency) for the 8.5-year-old:** "Is it forever or just now? Is it about everything or just this?" (permanence and pervasiveness). Present it as one of the detective tools.

### Gaps
- No Israeli program evaluated specifically for ages 2-6 was found.
- NATAL/ICTP school program trial data was not retrieved.
- The current 2025-2026 version of the MoE Life Skills curriculum for grade 3 and gan was not confirmed.
- No self-compassion RCT for under-10s was found.

## 6. Guardrails: not therapy, red flags, co-play, professional review, data minimisation

### Takeaway
Since CBT and SEL ingredients overlap with clinical treatment, the app must be explicit that it is universal skill practice and not a diagnosis or treatment. It should route concerning signs to parents and professionals, use co-play for younger children, have a child psychologist review content, and collect as little emotional data as possible. Israel's Privacy Protection Law Amendment 13 (in force 14 Aug 2025) classifies "assessments of personality traits" and medical data as highly sensitive.

### Cited Findings
- **Israel Privacy Protection Law, Amendment 13:**
  - In force 14 August 2025.
  - "Highly sensitive" information includes medical and mental-health data, biometric and genetic data, and assessments of personality traits.
  - Databases with highly sensitive data on more than 100,000 people must be reported to the regulator.
  - There is no dedicated child-consent age, so the general rules apply to minors, per a secondary EdTech compliance summary.
  - Sources: [Pearl Cohen](https://www.pearlcohen.com/the-knesset-enacts-a-comprehensive-amendment-to-the-privacy-protection-law/); [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/israel-marks-a-new-era-in-privacy-law-amendment-13-ushers-in-sweeping-reform); [Goldfarb](https://www.goldfarb.com/amendment-13-to-the-israeli-privacy-protection-law/); [EduVerse summary on minors](https://edlaw.eduverse.com/docs/jurisdiction/nat/il/protection-of-privacy-law)
- **Evidence for honest claims:**
  - CBT is not superior to active controls (Cochrane 2020). — [Cochrane](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD013162.pub2/information/fa)
  - Games did not beat control games (MindLight 2016). — [RUG](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/a-neurofeedback-video-game-mindlight-to-prevent-anxiety-in-childr/)
  - Universal school mindfulness had null student effects (MYRIAD). — [Mindful.org](https://www.mindful.org/?p=62492)
  - Together these make "therapeutic" claims indefensible.
- **Commercial conflicts of interest are common:** Mightier/RAGE-Control equity, developer-led PATHS trials, Zones' self-published evidence. — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/930620); [PMC7085485](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7085485); [ASAT](https://asatonline.org/research-treatment/research-synopses/review-of-the-zones-of-regulation-program/)
- **Graded challenge must be paced:** exposure-related in-game behaviour is a therapeutic mechanism (Wols 2018), so pushing a highly anxious child is a clinical act. — [BSL](https://thim.mijn.bsl.nl/in-game-play-behaviours-during-an-applied-video-game-for-anxiety/15838096)

### Inferences
**Disclaimers and framing:**
- In the parent area, in Hebrew: "האפליקציה מתרגלת כישורים רגשיים-חברתיים לכל הילדים. היא אינה טיפול, אבחון או תחליף לפסיכולוג."
- No in-app scores labelled "anxiety level" or "mood score".

**Red-flag guide for parents** (static content, ideally written with an Israeli child psychologist). Seek help if, for weeks, the child shows:
- persistent worry or avoidance that limits school, sleep or friends
- frequent intense tantrums beyond what is typical for the age (for the 8.5-year-old especially)
- persistent sadness, loss of interest, or talk of self-harm (urgent)
- regression after a traumatic event, such as sirens or loss
- separation fears that block gan or school

Point to the school counsellor (יועצת), קופת חולים child mental health services, and ERAN/NATAL hotlines. Verify current numbers before shipping.

**The app does not "detect" disorders.** If a child repeatedly picks "scared" or "sad", at most show a gentle parent-side nudge ("שמנו לב שהרבה פעמים נבחר 'עצוב' השבוע — אולי זה זמן טוב לשיחה"). Make it optional and on by parent choice.

**Data minimisation:**
- Store feelings check-ins **locally on the device** or not at all. Default is ephemeral: the check-in drives that session's dialogue only.
- No free-text emotional journaling sent to servers.
- No audio or camera storage for breathing detection; process on-device.
- No third-party analytics on emotional screens.
- No AI chat that elicits disclosures from children without strict guardrails and parent visibility.
- This keeps the app out of "highly sensitive" processing under Amendment 13, and it is good practice regardless.

**Co-play:**
- Toddler: always with a parent.
- 5.5-year-old: co-play for new skills, then solo for practice.
- 8.5-year-old: solo play with a weekly 5-minute parent debrief card.
- Bravery missions always need a parent to co-sign.

**Content review:** a child psychologist (ideally CBT-trained, Israeli) reviews scripts for thinking traps, trauma-adjacent content and bravery ladders. Also do cultural and religious sensitivity review, and avoid shame-based messages ("big boys don't cry").

**No manipulative engagement loops:** no streak punishment, no loot boxes. "All feelings are OK" messaging stays consistent.

### Gaps
- Exact current Israeli rules on children's consent for apps were not confirmed from a primary source; Privacy Protection Authority guidance should be checked.
- Hotline numbers were not verified.
- There's no published consensus standard for "red flags" in consumer SEL apps. The list above is an inference from general clinical practice and should be confirmed by a clinician.

## 7. Concrete game mechanics and character dialogues (synthesis)

### Takeaway
The highest-value mechanics combine the best-evidenced ingredients:
- emotion vocabulary (RULER-style)
- explicit, sequenced skill episodes (SAFE)
- active role-play and real-world missions
- graded approach behaviour (exposure/bravery)
- cooperative sibling play (Kramer, prosocial content)

Calming mechanics such as breathing are useful short-term tools but should not be oversold.

### Cited Findings
- SAFE features predict stronger SEL effects. — [Cipriano 2023](https://academic.oup.com/chidev/article-abstract/94/5/1181/8255238)
- Prosocial game content predicts prosocial behaviour. — [Gentile 2009](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/the-effects-of-prosocial-video-games-on-prosocial-behaviors-inter/)
- Exposure-type in-game behaviour predicts anxiety improvement. — [Wols 2018](https://thim.mijn.bsl.nl/in-game-play-behaviours-during-an-applied-video-game-for-anxiety/15838096)
- Brief slow breathing shifts children's stress physiology. — [Greater Good](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/article/item/how_four_deep_breaths_can_help_kids_calm_down); with null mood effects in daily life — [PubMed 35704507](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35704507/)
- Sibling programs improve warmth and regulation in ages 4-8. — [Kennedy & Kramer 2008](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.northeastern.edu/dist/d/3128/files/2023/02/Kennedy-Kramer-2008.pdf)
- Sesame's 3-step "breathe, think, do" structure for ages 2-5 is a usable template, though unevaluated. — [Horn Book](https://www.hbook.com/story/breathe-app-review)

### Inferences
**Mechanic catalogue** (name, ages, skill/evidence anchor, sketch):

1. **Feelings check-in with a companion (מד הרגשות)**
   - Ages and anchor: all ages. RULER mood-meter concept, affect labeling.
   - Session start: the companion (e.g., a fox, "שועלי") asks "?איך הגוף שלך מרגיש עכשיו". Toddler taps a face. 5.5 picks a colour quadrant, then a word. 8.5 picks a granular word plus "how strong" (1-3).
   - The companion *validates* ("זה בסדר להרגיש מתוסכלת") and offers a fitting tool.
   - The data is not stored by default.
2. **Emotion vocabulary collection (אוסף מילות רגש)**
   - Ages and anchor: 5.5 and 8.5. Vocabulary granularity.
   - New feeling words are unlocked as illustrated cards with a body cue plus a situation ("מאוכזב: כשמשהו שחיכית לו לא קרה").
   - The 8.5-year-old can sort words into "cousins" (כועס → מתוסכל → זועם).
3. **Thought Detective (בלש המחשבות)**
   - Ages and anchor: 8.5 (simplified for 5.5). CBT cognitive restructuring, Penn Resiliency.
   - Characters voice thoughts. The child spots the thinking trap from a set of trap "masks", collects clues (fact vs. feeling), and chooses a balanced thought.
   - Example: "אף אחד לא רוצה לשחק איתי אף פעם" → trap: "תמיד/אף פעם" → clue: "אתמול נועה שיחקה איתי" → balanced: "היום קשה לי, אבל יש ילדים שאוהבים לשחק איתי".
4. **Brave Quest ladder (מסע האומץ)**
   - Ages and anchor: 5.5 and 8.5, parent co-designed. Graded exposure.
   - See section 5. Real-world rungs, fear thermometer before and after, and celebration of *trying*.
5. **Dragon Breath (נשימת דרקון)**
   - Ages and anchor: all ages (toddler with a parent). Short-term physiological calming.
   - 3-5 slow breaths warm an egg that hatches a baby dragon over sessions.
   - Variants: "smell the flower, blow the candle" (toddler); "box breathing 4-4-4-4" for 8.5, optional.
6. **Problem-solving workshop (בית המלאכה לפתרון בעיות)**
   - Ages and anchor: 5.5 (3 steps) and 8.5 (5 steps). Coping Cat / FRIENDS problem-solving.
   - A character has a problem (lost a ball over the fence; a friend won't share). The child brainstorms ideas, *including silly ones*, predicts consequences ("ואז מה יקרה?"), picks one, and watches the outcome. Bad choices lead to gentle natural consequences and a retry.
7. **Choose-your-path story dilemmas (סיפורי בחירה)**
   - Ages and anchor: 5.5 and 8.5. Responsible decision-making, perspective-taking.
   - Branching stories of 3-5 choices. After each choice, show how *each* character feels.
   - Themes: telling the truth after breaking something, including a new kid, losing a game gracefully, a sibling who wrecked your Lego tower.
8. **Sibling Team Talk (שיחת צוות)**
   - Ages and anchor: family mode. Kramer program, I-messages, active listening.
   - Animated scenarios first, then "use it for real" with a talking-stick button on the parent's phone that guides the 5-step protocol in section 4.
9. **Cooperative family quests (משימת צוות משפחתית)**
   - Ages and anchor: all three kids. Prosocial and collaborative play.
   - Asymmetric roles fitted to each child's ability. Success needs everyone. Team rewards only.
10. **Mistake celebration (חגיגת טעויות)**
    - Ages and anchor: all ages. Persistence, process praise.
    - Failed attempts give "learning gems". The companion shares its own mistakes. Weekly "best mistake" story with the family.
11. **Kindness missions (משימות חסד)**
    - Ages and anchor: all ages. Prosocial behaviour.
    - A real-world weekly mission, parent-confirmed, that grows a shared family tree or garden.
12. **Toolbox selection (ארגז הכלים)**
    - Ages and anchor: 5.5 and 8.5. Metacognition, "right tool for the problem".
    - In scenarios the child picks which tool to use (breathe, detective, I-message, ask for help, brainstorm, brave step). Feedback explains why it fits.
    - Reflects the parent's explicit goal.
13. **Presentation stage (במת ההצגה)**
    - Ages and anchor: 5.5 and 8.5. Communication and courage.
    - Record and play back a short "teach the companion" explanation. The audience grows over time as a bravery ladder.

**Character dialogue snippets (Hebrew, with English intent):**
- Companion modelling frustration: "אוף! הגשר שלי נפל שוב. אני מרגיש מתוסכל. רגע... נשימת דרקון... עכשיו אני אנסה דרך אחרת." (models label, calm, strategy shift)
- Validation before solution: "נשמע שזה ממש הכעיס אותך. מותר לכעוס. בוא נחשוב מה אפשר לעשות עם הכעס."
- Bravery: "אומץ זה לא להיות בלי פחד. אומץ זה לעשות צעד קטן גם כשקצת מפחד."
- Detective: "מחשבות הן לא תמיד עובדות. בוא נבדוק אותן כמו בלשים."
- Team talk: "כל אחד יקבל תור לדבר, והשני מקשיב עד הסוף."
- Session close: "מה היה הכלי שהכי עזר לך היום? ספר/י לאמא או לאבא!" (prompts parent co-regulation)

**Age-banded session design:**
- Toddler: 3-5 minutes, all co-play, no reading, no failure.
- 5.5: 5-8 minutes, voice-led with picture choices, mostly 3-step tools.
- 8.5: 8-12 minutes, reading allowed, reflective prompts, leadership roles in family mode.

**Measurement (lightweight, parent-side only):** an optional monthly 3-question parent reflection (e.g., "my child names feelings more / uses a calming tool / tries new things"). Not a clinical scale and not stored as an emotional profile.

### Gaps
- No direct trial evidence exists for most of these specific mechanics. They are inferred from program components and should be pilot-tested with the family and reviewed by a clinician.
- Whether in-app sibling conflict scenarios transfer to real sibling behaviour is untested. Kramer's evidence is for an in-person or online parent and child program, not a game.
