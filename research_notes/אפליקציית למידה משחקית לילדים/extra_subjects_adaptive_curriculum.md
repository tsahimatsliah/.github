## A1. Israeli Ministry of Education curriculum: science & technology, moledet/chevra/ezrachut, gan areas. What does a 3rd grader study?

### Takeaway
Grade 3 "Moledet, Chevra ve'Ezrachut" is about the child's own town/settlement and its nearby surroundings: nature, society, the town's history within Eretz Israel history, and settlement in Israel. It is taught in "expanding circles" (family, then neighborhood, then town, then country) across grades 2-4. Science & technology is a compulsory spiral subject in every grade 1-6. I could not get the official grade 1-3 science topic list, but grade-band documents group materials and their properties, water and air in the grades 3-4 tier.

### Cited Findings
- Moledet, chevra ve'ezrachut, grade 3: the Ministry subject page lists four grade-3 topics: (1) אדם וטבע ביישוב ובסביבה הקרובה (people and nature in the town and nearby area); (2) אדם וחברה ביישוב ובסביבה ויחסי הגומלין ביניהם (people and society in the town and how they interact); (3) היישוב שלי בראי תולדות ארץ ישראל (my town through the history of Eretz Israel); (4) היישובים במדינה שבדרך (settlements of the pre-state "state in the making" period) — [pop.education.gov.il, פותחים שנה מולדת](https://pop.education.gov.il/tchumey_daat/moledet_hevra_ezrahut/yesodi/noseem_nilmadim/pothim-shana-moledet/)
- The guiding principle of the grades 2-4 moledet program is learning in expanding circles (מעגלים מתרחבים): family, then neighborhood, then town, then more distant environments — [pop.education.gov.il, רצף התכנים והרציונל](https://pop.education.gov.il/tchumey_daat/geography_adam_sviva/yesodi/curriculum/contents-rational/); [Ministry annual planning / expected achievements page](https://pop.education.gov.il/tchumey_daat/moledet_hevra_ezrahut/yesodi/homeland-pedagogy/annual-plan/)
- A 2022 grades 2-4 homeland syllabus exists. I could not extract its grade-3 detail — [HomelandSyllabus2022.pdf](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Mazkirut_Pedagogit%5CGeographya/HomelandSyllabus2022.pdf). A grade-3 geography "chozer" (circular) for 2020 also exists — [HozerGeo32020.pdf](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Mazkirut_Pedagogit%5CGeographya/HozerGeo32020.pdf)

- Science & technology (מדע וטכנולוגיה): Ministry elementary documents describe it as compulsory in every grade 1-6, built as a spiral sequence, with lessons in a dedicated science room (summarized from search snippets of Ministry elementary science pages, e.g. [engineer.pdf, elementary science/technology](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Mazkirut_Pedagogit/MadaTechnologya/yesodi/engineer.pdf). ### Inferences
- For the 8.5-year-old, the "world knowledge" content should be local and Israeli first: the family's own town (map of the neighborhood, local nature, who works in town, the town's founding story), then settlement history and Israeli geography. Generic "countries and flags" content does not match what grade 3 teaches, though it can serve as a hook (see A3).
- Expanding circles is a ready-made spine for the social-studies track across all three kids: the 2.5-year-old does "me and my family", the 5.5-year-old does "my gan and neighborhood, holidays", the 8.5-year-old does "my town and Eretz Israel".
- For science, materials and properties, water, and air are safe grade-3 targets. Exact grade 1-3 units (for example living things, plants, the body, energy, technology/design) still need to be confirmed against the official document.

## A2. Evidence for extra subjects: science/inquiry, world knowledge, coding, arts, music, SEL, financial literacy, chess, logic, executive function

### Takeaway
"Far transfer" claims are weak. Chess (EEF RCT) and music (Sala & Gobet meta-analyses) show roughly zero effect on academic or cognitive outcomes once study quality is controlled. The best-supported "extra" for academic gains is **background/world knowledge**, because it directly drives reading comprehension. Next are metacognition/self-regulation (EEF +7 to +8 months) and SEL (moderate impact, extensive evidence). Arts participation gives about +3 months. Early coding reliably improves **sequencing**, a near-transfer skill.

### Cited Findings
**World / background knowledge (strongest case for reading)**
- Recht & Leslie (1988), *Journal of Educational Psychology*, "Effect of Prior Knowledge on Good and Poor Readers' Memory of Text": 64 junior-high students were grouped by reading ability × baseball knowledge, then re-enacted a passage about a game with figures. Baseball knowledge, not reading ability, drove comprehension. Low-ability readers with high knowledge did about as well as high-ability readers — [Wikipedia: Baseball Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_Study); [Core Knowledge Foundation](https://www.coreknowledge.org/baseball-experiment-two-wisconsin-researchers-discovered-comprehension-gap-knowledge-gap/)
- A defense of the study's relevance (domain knowledge is what readers lean on): [Natalie Wexler, "Clearing up misconceptions about the Baseball Study"](https://nataliewexler.substack.com/p/clearing-up-misconceptions-about-a44). A critique says it is over-cited to justify specific teacher-led pedagogies: [Human Restoration Project, Medium](https://medium.com/human-restoration-project/that-baseball-study-cant-tell-you-how-to-teach-9ee2d6cedb08)

**Chess**
- EEF "Chess in Primary Schools" RCT: 100 schools across 11 English local authorities, 4,009 Year 5 pupils, 2013/14, about 30 hours of chess instruction. Result: no significant difference in maths attainment — [ISRCTN33648117](https://www.isrctn.com/ISRCTN33648117); [Chess.com, "School Chess Fails PR Test"](https://www.chess.com/article/view/school-chess-fails-pr-test)
- Caveats raised: 30 hours is a small dose, and teaching consistency is questionable. 39% of pupils kept playing after the trial (an enjoyment/engagement signal) — [Chess.com](https://www.chess.com/article/view/school-chess-fails-pr-test); [ChessExpress blog](https://chessexpress.blogspot.com/2016/07/chess-doesnt-make-you-smarter-it-seems.html)

**Music**
- Sala & Gobet 2017 meta-analysis (*Educational Research Review*): small overall effect d≈0.16 (intelligence d≈0.35, memory d≈0.34). Effects shrink as study quality rises — [Liverpool repository](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3005023/)
- Sala & Gobet 2020 multilevel meta-analysis (*Memory & Cognition*): 54 studies, 254 effect sizes. With design quality controlled, the overall effect is about zero (g≈0), consistent across outcome type, age and training duration — [PMC7683441](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7683441); [MinnPost coverage](https://www.minnpost.com/second-opinion/2020/08/music-lessons-may-not-help-children-do-better-in-academic-subjects-after-all-study-suggests/)
- Gobet: music may still benefit social skills or self-esteem (a non-cognitive value) — [MinnPost](https://www.minnpost.com/second-opinion/2020/08/music-lessons-may-not-help-children-do-better-in-academic-subjects-after-all-study-suggests/)
- Sala & Gobet's broader argument: far transfer is rare across chess, music and working-memory training, and effect size falls as design quality rises (for example with active controls) — ["Does Far Transfer Exist?", Liverpool repository](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3030311/)

**Coding / computational thinking (Marina Bers, Tufts DevTech)**
- An 8-week KIBO robotics/coding curriculum raised PreK-grade 2 sequencing scores by 20-35% (citing Kazakoff, Sullivan & Bers 2013, *Early Childhood Education Journal*). The source is vendor material from KinderLab, a company Bers co-founded — [KinderLab, "Making the Case for KIBO"](https://kinderlabrobotics.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Making-the-Case-for-KIBO.pdf)
- ScratchJr study (IDC 2015): N=57 K-2 students over 7 weeks making animated stories, collages and games. All students mastered foundational coding concepts, and performance differed by grade — [DevTech IDC 2015 paper](https://sites.bc.edu/devtech/wp-content/uploads/sites/181/2018/02/sjr-demo-idc2015.pdf)
- An early poster reports more sequencing and block-comprehension errors among boys in K-1 (preliminary) — [NSF ScratchJr poster 2014](https://sites.bc.edu/devtech/wp-content/uploads/sites/181/2018/02/NSF-SJr-Poster-2014.pdf)

**Arts, metacognition, SEL (EEF Teaching & Learning Toolkit)**
- Arts participation: +3 months of additional progress in other academic areas, very low cost, moderate evidence — [EEF Arts participation](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/arts-participation/technical-appendix)
- Metacognition & self-regulation: the EEF's 2025 update says the estimate rose from +7 to +8 months — [EEF toolkit update](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/updates-ten-strands-toolkit). An earlier EEF blog reported it falling from +8 to +7 — [EEF blog](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/latest-updates-to-the-toolkit/). **These conflict; the live toolkit page is authoritative.**
- Social & emotional learning: "moderate impact for moderate cost, based on extensive evidence" (older mirror; no months figure retrieved) — [Education Scotland EEF toolkit mirror](https://nrs.preservation.mirrorweb.com/public/20191211012915mp_/https://education.gov.scot/improvement/eef-toolkit/)

### Inferences
- Rank "extra subjects" by **what they directly teach**, not by claimed transfer:
  1. World knowledge (science, nature, the body, Israel/geography, history stories). This directly raises reading comprehension (Recht & Leslie), so it doubles as Hebrew/English reading support. Build it as knowledge-rich read-aloud/read-along stories plus quizzes.
  2. Self-regulation/metacognition habits built into the game itself ("predict, try, check"; "how sure are you?"; planning a puzzle before acting). This is the highest-leverage EEF strand.
  3. SEL: emotion naming, calming strategies, perspective-taking stories. These are especially suited to the 2.5- and 5.5-year-olds.
  4. Coding/sequencing: ScratchJr-style block sequencing for the 5.5-year-old, more complex loops and events for the 8.5-year-old. Expect sequencing gains, not general IQ gains.
  5. Art and music: include for joy, expression and self-esteem, and say so honestly. Do not market them as "makes kids smarter".
  6. Chess and logic puzzles: include as an interest-driven fun track (39% kept playing in the EEF trial), not as a math intervention.
- Financial literacy, executive-function games, Gopnik-style early science inquiry and CASEL competencies would fit the SEL/self-regulation and science slots, but their evidence was not checked here (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- Gopnik ("scientist in the crib", causal learning in toddlers) and "science talk" research: not retrieved within budget.
- CASEL's five competencies framework and EEF's SEL months figure: not retrieved.
- Financial literacy for ages 5-10 (for example OECD/CFPB "money habits formed by age 7"): not retrieved. Treat it as unverified.
- Executive-function training games (Diamond; Melby-Lervåg & Hulme working-memory meta-analyses): only Sala & Gobet's general "far transfer is rare" point was found.
- Code.org and Bee-Bot evidence: not retrieved. The KIBO figures come from vendor material, so verify them in Kazakoff et al. 2013 before citing.

## A3. Which extras have the strongest evidence vs. which are "interest hooks"?

### Takeaway
Strongest evidence: background knowledge (for reading), metacognition/self-regulation, SEL, and near-transfer skills like sequencing from coding. Weak or no transfer evidence: chess, music, generic "brain training". Interest topics (space, dinosaurs, animals, the human body, countries/flags) are best used as the **content carriers** for knowledge-building and reading. In this context that makes them high-value, not just filler.

### Cited Findings
- Domain knowledge can outweigh general reading skill for comprehension, so knowledge of any domain a child cares about (baseball in the study) is a lever — [Wikipedia: Baseball Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_Study); [Wexler](https://nataliewexler.substack.com/p/clearing-up-misconceptions-about-a44)
- Chess, music and working-memory training show null or near-null far transfer under rigorous designs — [Sala & Gobet, Liverpool repository](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3030311/); [PMC7683441](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7683441)
- Engagement can persist even without measured academic transfer (39% of EEF chess pupils kept playing) — [Chess.com](https://www.chess.com/article/view/school-chess-fails-pr-test)

### Inferences (proposed scope: design recommendation, not sourced)
| Track | Age 2.5 | Age 5.5 (gan chova) | Age 8.5 (grade 3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Science/nature ("מדע") | Animals, sounds, colors, body parts, weather words; cause-effect toys (push/drop/float) | Living vs non-living, plants growing, seasons in Israel, senses, simple float/sink and magnet experiments, predict-then-check | Materials & properties, water, air (grade 3-4 tier); human body systems basics; habitats; simple design/technology challenges |
| Moledet / world | Me, family, home, gan | Neighborhood, community helpers, Israeli holidays cycle, map of my gan/room | My town (nature, society, history); Eretz Israel settlement history; reading simple maps; regions of Israel; then the world (continents, flags as a hook) |
| SEL / self-regulation | Naming feelings with faces, turn-taking, calm-down breathing | Feelings with causes, friendship scenarios, "stop-think-do" | Perspective-taking stories, growth-mindset reflection, "how sure am I?" confidence ratings |
| Logic / coding | Sorting, matching, simple patterns | Patterns, sequencing (ScratchJr-style 3-6 blocks), directional "robot" moves | Loops, events, debugging; logic grids, chess tactics as optional fun |
| Art / music | Free drawing, rhythm tapping, songs | Shapes-to-pictures, rhythm/echo games, Hebrew children's songs | Composition with simple sequencer, art from other cultures tied to world topics |
| Interest hooks | Animals | Dinosaurs, space, vehicles | Space, dinosaurs, the human body, countries/flags, sport |

- Make interest topics "knowledge packs" (vocabulary plus facts plus a short read-along text plus quiz items), tagged to reading-level skills. Each pack then advances both world knowledge and literacy.

### Gaps
- No direct study found comparing the engagement value of specific topics (dinosaurs vs space and so on) for these ages.

## B1. How leading adaptive systems model skills (Khan, ALEKS, Math Academy, Duolingo, Math Garden/Rekentuin, IXL, DreamBox, Zearn)

### Takeaway
Three useful patterns. (1) **Discrete mastery ladders** with test-gated promotion and demotion (Khan). (2) **Prerequisite/knowledge-structure models** with a short diagnostic that infers a "knowledge state" and an "outer fringe" of ready-to-learn topics (ALEKS; Math Academy's knowledge graph). (3) **Continuous ratings** updated per answer without pre-calibration (Math Garden's Elo with response time) plus **memory-decay models** for review (Duolingo half-life regression; Math Academy's FIRe, which gives implicit credit down the prerequisite graph).

### Cited Findings
**Khan Academy**
- Levels: Not started → Attempted → Familiar → Proficient → Mastered. Familiar = 70-85% on an exercise, or all questions on a skill correct in a quiz/test/challenge. Proficient requires Familiar first, then all questions correct. Mastered requires Proficient first, then all questions correct on a **Unit Test or Course Challenge**. Points: 50 / 80 / 100 per skill — [Khan help: Course and Unit Mastery](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/115002552631) (2018-era wording, may have changed)
- Course mastery % counts only Proficient and Mastered skills. A unit test is a course challenge scoped to one unit — [Khan help](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/115002552631)
- Demotion (Sept 2018): Mastered with 70-99% on an exercise drops to Proficient; below 70% drops to Familiar — [Khan community: Mastery Mechanics Changes Sept 2018](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/de/community/posts/360023249611/comments/360003019811)

**ALEKS (Knowledge Space Theory)**
- Built on knowledge space theory. A knowledge state is the set of topics a student plausibly knows, and the knowledge space is the family of feasible states, constrained by prerequisites — [ALEKS EDM 2022 industry paper](https://educationaldatamining.org/EDM2022/proceedings/2022.EDM-industry-track.109/index.html); [Falmagne/Doignon et al., "Knowledge Spaces and Learning Spaces", arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.06757)
- The initial assessment is about 15-29 questions and sorts topics into likely known, likely unknown and uncertain using probabilities plus prerequisite relations. Students then see the topics they are "ready to learn" (the fringe), which updates as they master more — [EDM 2022](https://educationaldatamining.org/EDM2022/proceedings/2022.EDM-industry-track.109/2022.EDM-industry-track.109.pdf); [EDM 2024 placement assessment evaluation](https://educationaldatamining.org/EDM2024/proceedings/2024.EDM-industry.61/index.html); [EdTech Digest interview](https://edtechdigest.blog/2013/04/10/smart-aleks)
- A newer ALEKS initial assessment uses a neural network that outputs probabilities consistent with the knowledge space — [EDM 2022](https://educationaldatamining.org/EDM2022/proceedings/2022.EDM-industry-track.109/index.html). ALEKS has also modeled forgetting — [Matayoshi et al., EDM 2018](https://jmatayoshi.github.io/publications/EDM2018_forgetting.pdf); [McGraw Hill KST overview](https://www.mheducation.com/content/dam/mhe/research/prek-12/essa-tier-4/aleks-practical-perspective-knowledge-space-theory.pdf)

**Math Academy (knowledge graph + FIRe)**
- Curriculum: thousands of interlinked topics from 4th grade through university math, connected in one knowledge graph — [Math Academy, How our AI works](https://mathacademy.com/how-our-ai-works)
- Fractional Implicit Repetition (Justin Skycak): a successful review gives implicit (fractional) credit to the topic's prerequisites and their prerequisites "trickling down" the graph. The scheduler picks reviews whose implicit credit covers other due reviews, which minimizes the total number of reviews — [Skycak, "Optimized, Individualized Spaced Repetition in Hierarchical Knowledge Structures"](https://gwern.net/doc/www/www.justinmath.com/9af2ce40376e836660c237a0e971d1896a163eee.html); [The Math Academy Way (PDF)](https://justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf); [Frank Hecker's review series](https://frankhecker.com/2025/02/14/math-academy-part-7/)
- No independent empirical evaluation of FIRe was found. The efficiency claims rest on Math Academy's own materials — (search finding; see sources above)

**Duolingo**
- Half-life regression (Settles & Meeder, ACL 2016): predicted recall p = 2^(−Δ/h), with half-life h = 2^(Θ·x) learned from features of the practice history. It cut recall-prediction error by more than 45% vs baselines including Leitner, and raised daily engagement by 12% in an operational test. Code and data: github.com/duolingo/halflife-regression — [ACL Anthology P16-1174](https://preview.aclanthology.org/landing_page/P16-1174); [Duolingo blog, "How we learn how you learn"](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-we-learn-how-you-learn); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/how-duolingo-is-using-ai-to-humanize-virtual-language-lessons)
- Duolingo first used Leitner-style doubling and halving of half-lives (2012) — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-we-learn-how-you-learn)

**Math Garden / Rekentuin (Klinkenberg, Straatemeier & van der Maas 2011, *Computers & Education*)**
- An Elo-based model updates **both child ability and item difficulty after every trial**, using an explicit scoring rule that combines accuracy and response time (a speed-accuracy "high speed, high stakes" style rule). No item pre-calibration is needed: about 40 games and 50,000+ items start from arbitrary ratings. Ability estimates correlate highly with paper-and-pencil tests — [UvA DARE record](https://dare.uva.nl/id/67ab8977-5e36-4f9f-962d-be294f2b8c09); [Klinkenberg profile/papers](https://independent.academia.edu/SharonKlinkenberg)
- Later multidimensional Elo (M-ERS) using speed plus accuracy outperforms accuracy-only — [Frontiers in Psychology 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00620)

### Inferences
- For a small family app, **Math Garden's Elo** is the most practical core estimator. It needs no calibration data, self-calibrates item difficulty as the three kids play, and fits short game items. Response time matters especially for math facts.
- Borrow **Khan's discrete ladder** for UI and parent reporting (Familiar/Proficient/Mastered, with mastery requiring success on a later, mixed check, not just the practice session).
- Borrow **ALEKS/Math Academy's prerequisite graph** to decide what is "ready to learn" (all prerequisites ≥ Proficient) and to run a short adaptive placement.
- Borrow **FIRe's implicit credit** idea in simplified form. When a child succeeds on an advanced skill (for example 2-digit addition with regrouping), push forward the review due dates of its prerequisites (1-digit facts) by a fractional amount, so review time isn't wasted.
- Use **half-life regression or FSRS** only for memorizable items (letters, sight words, English vocab, number facts, flags/capitals). Use procedural skills via graph-based review.

### Gaps
- IXL SmartScore and Real-Time Diagnostic mechanics, Duolingo Birdbrain (the newer Elo/IRT-like model), DreamBox and Zearn: not retrieved within the tool budget.
- FSRS (Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler) and BKT parameters: not retrieved. Both are well documented publicly (FSRS on the open-spaced-repetition GitHub; BKT from Corbett & Anderson 1995), but they were not verified here.

## B2. Placement, school-calendar alignment, session composition, gaps, acceleration, frustration

### Takeaway
Use a short adaptive placement that walks the prerequisite graph (ALEKS-style, about 15-30 items for older kids, far fewer and game-embedded for young kids), then let continuous Elo updates correct it. Treat a parent's "this month in class" input as a **priority boost** on graph nodes, not a hard override. Keep the prerequisite-readiness rule so the app fills gaps under the class topic first. The sources here cover the components. The composition rules are design inferences.

### Cited Findings
- ALEKS placement: about 15-29 questions, yielding known/unknown/uncertain topics and a ready-to-learn fringe — [EDM 2022](https://educationaldatamining.org/EDM2022/proceedings/2022.EDM-industry-track.109/2022.EDM-industry-track.109.pdf); [EDM 2024 placement evaluation](https://educationaldatamining.org/EDM2024/proceedings/2024.EDM-industry.61/index.html)
- Elo-based practice avoids pretesting and starts from arbitrary ratings, so placement can be "soft" and continuous — [Klinkenberg et al. 2011](https://dare.uva.nl/id/67ab8977-5e36-4f9f-962d-be294f2b8c09)
- Math Garden matches items to ability level so children practice at an appropriate difficulty — [UvA DARE](https://dare.uva.nl/id/67ab8977-5e36-4f9f-962d-be294f2b8c09); [Frontiers 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00620)
- Mastery should be confirmed by a later, cumulative check (Khan: Mastered only via a Unit Test or Course Challenge), with demotion on later weak performance — [Khan help](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/115002552631); [Khan 2018 changes](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/de/community/posts/360023249611/comments/360003019811)
- Math Academy-style review selection compresses review load by choosing reviews that implicitly cover other due topics — [Skycak FIRe](https://gwern.net/doc/www/www.justinmath.com/9af2ce40376e836660c237a0e971d1896a163eee.html)

### Inferences (design proposals)
- **Placement by age**:
  - 2.5: no test. Start at age-default nodes, and let Elo move them through play. A parent checklist ("knows colors? counts to 5?") seeds priors.
  - 5.5: a 5-8 minute game-embedded probe per domain (counting, letter recognition, phonemic awareness), 6-10 items, stopping early after 2 consecutive misses.
  - 8.5: an ALEKS-style probe that starts at the grade-3 midpoint node and binary-searches up and down the graph, about 15-25 items per subject, spread over 2-3 sessions to avoid fatigue.
- **School-calendar alignment**: store a yearly scaffold per kid (Ministry grade topics mapped to graph nodes, ordered by typical month). The parent sets "this month in class: X" (or picks from a list). The planner adds a priority boost to X and its ready-to-learn prerequisites. If prerequisites are below Proficient, it schedules those first ("pre-teach / gap-fill"). After class teaches X, the boost switches to retrieval practice of X.
- **Daily session composition** (10-15 min for 5.5 and 8.5; 5-8 min for 2.5): about 20% warm-up review (due items, easy wins), 40-50% new/current material at ~70-85% expected success (Elo target), 20% interleaved mixed review across subjects, and 10-20% fun/interest track (knowledge pack, art, coding, chess). End on a success.
- **Interleaving across subjects**: alternate blocks (math → world knowledge story → Hebrew reading of the same story's vocabulary). Knowledge packs naturally interleave literacy and content.
- **Gaps**: when an item fails and its prerequisite estimate is uncertain, drop one node down the graph (ALEKS fringe logic). Mark a gap, fill it, then return.
- **Acceleration**: allow "challenge-out". If a child passes a cumulative check on nodes ahead of grade, mark them Proficient and jump the fringe forward (Khan course challenge analogue). There is no grade cap; the graph decides.
- **Frustration handling**: if accuracy is below ~60% over the last 5-8 items, or response times spike, or the child exits early, lower the target difficulty, insert a known-easy item, and switch modality (story/game). Never demote mastery on a single bad session. Require a pattern across sessions (Khan demotes only on exercise-score thresholds).

### Gaps

## B3. Data model sketch and simple algorithms feasible for a small app (Elo, BKT, Leitner/FSRS)

### Takeaway
A compact model of Skill nodes (prerequisite DAG, subject, grade/month tags), Items (difficulty rating, item type, media), Attempts (correct, RT, hints), and per-child SkillState (Elo rating, mastery level, half-life/next-due, last seen) is enough. Elo with response time (Math Garden) plus a Khan-style ladder plus Leitner/HLR review plus FIRe-like implicit credit is implementable on-device in Swift with SQLite/SwiftData.

### Cited Findings
- Elo updates of ability and item difficulty per trial, incorporating RT via a scoring rule, with no pretesting — [Klinkenberg et al. 2011](https://dare.uva.nl/id/67ab8977-5e36-4f9f-962d-be294f2b8c09)
- HLR: recall probability decays as 2^(−Δ/h), h = 2^(Θ·x), roughly halving Leitner's error — [ACL P16-1174](https://preview.aclanthology.org/landing_page/P16-1174); [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-we-learn-how-you-learn)
- Mastery ladder thresholds (70-85% → Familiar; perfect → Proficient; perfect on a cumulative test → Mastered; demotion thresholds) — [Khan help](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/115002552631); [Khan 2018](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/de/community/posts/360023249611/comments/360003019811)
- Implicit trickle-down credit through prerequisites — [Skycak FIRe](https://gwern.net/doc/www/www.justinmath.com/9af2ce40376e836660c237a0e971d1896a163eee.html)
- Knowledge state = set of mastered topics, with the fringe determined by prerequisites — [arXiv 1511.06757](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.06757)

### Inferences (design sketch; not sourced)
```
Skill { id, subject (math|hebrew|english|science|moledet|sel|logic|art|music),
        title_he, title_en, prereqs: [SkillId], encompasses: [{SkillId, weight 0..1}],
        ageMin, ageMax, gradeTag (gan-trom|gan-chova|1|2|3|4), monthHint (Sep..Jun),
        type (fact|procedure|concept|story-knowledge) }
Item  { id, skillId, kind (tap|drag|speak|trace|build), difficultyElo (init 0),
        nAttempts, media, lang, audioPrompt (for pre-readers) }
Attempt { childId, itemId, ts, correct, responseMs, hintsUsed, sessionId }
ChildSkillState { childId, skillId, abilityElo, level (none|attempted|familiar|proficient|mastered),
        halfLifeDays, nextDue, lastSeen, streak, implicitCredit }
ChildProfile { id, age, grade, classThisMonth: [SkillId], interests: [tag], sessionMinutes }
YearPlan { childId, month -> [SkillId target], generated from gradeTag/monthHint + parent input }
```
- **Elo update with response time** (Math Garden style): score S = (2·correct − 1)·(1 − rt/deadline) gives a value in −1..1. Expected E from (ability − difficulty). ability += K_child·(S − E); difficulty −= K_item·(S − E). Use a higher K for new children and items, decaying with count.
- **Item selection**: pick an item with difficulty ≈ ability − offset, so expected success ≈ 75% (lower offset for frustrated or young kids).
- **Level promotion**: map Elo margin plus recent accuracy onto the Khan ladder. Require a delayed, mixed "checkpoint" (≥1 day later) for Mastered.
- **Review**: Leitner boxes (1, 2, 4, 8, 16 days) to start. Upgrade to HLR/FSRS once there is enough data. For a success on skill S, add `weight × credit` to each encompassed prerequisite's half-life (FIRe-lite).
- **Planner** (nightly or per session): candidates = due reviews ∪ fringe (prereqs ≥ proficient) ∪ class-boost nodes ∪ interest pack. Score = priority weights (due-ness, class boost, year-plan month, interest), then fill the session template from B2.
- **BKT** is an alternative to Elo per skill (P(known), P(learn), P(slip), P(guess)). Elo is simpler and self-calibrates item difficulty, which suits a tiny user base of 3 children.
- **Cold start with 3 users**: item difficulty won't calibrate from 3 kids alone. Seed difficulty from author estimates (grade/age tags), and let all three kids' attempts share item ratings.
