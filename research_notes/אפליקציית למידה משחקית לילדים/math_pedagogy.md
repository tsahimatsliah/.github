# Math Pedagogy, Curricula and Evidence for a Personalized Math Game (ages 2.5 / 5.5 / 8.5, Israel)

> Research method note: the Israeli Ministry of Education hosts (meyda.education.gov.il, pop.education.gov.il, edu.gov.il) and the publisher hashbacha.org were blocked in this research session's network (DNS failed), so Israeli curriculum findings come from search-engine extracts of those pages, not from reading the full documents. ## 1. What the Israeli Ministry of Education curriculum covers (gan chova, grades 1-3), grade 3 milestones and common difficulties

### Takeaway
The Israeli kindergarten math curriculum has three content strands: number concept (מושג המספר), spatial sense and geometry (תחושה מרחבית וגיאומטריה) and quantitative concepts (מושגים כמותיים). The Ministry is phasing in a new elementary math curriculum one grade per year, starting with grade 1 in 5785 (2024-25). One Ministry page says grade 3 joins it in 5787 (2026-27), so the 8.5-year-old is probably in the first grade-3 cohort on the new program this school year. Grade 3 centers on numbers up to 10,000 (רבבה), completing and consolidating the multiplication table (לוח הכפל) using the distributive law, the meanings of multiplication and division, and an introduction to simple fractions.

### Cited Findings
**Kindergarten (gan, including gan chova, ages 5-6)**
- The binding state and state-religious kindergarten curriculum has three central math content areas: number concept (מושג המספר), spatial sense and geometry (תחושה מרחבית וגיאומטריה), and quantitative concepts (מושגים כמותיים). A committee of mathematicians, teacher-college staff and early-childhood educators wrote it to replace the 1995 framework. — [Ministry of Education, kindergarten science & mathematics page](https://pop.education.gov.il/kindergarten/magar-ganey-yeladim/science-and-mathematics/); [Kindergarten curriculum page](https://pop.education.gov.il/kindergarten/magar-ganey-yeladim/curriculum-kindergarten)
- The Ministry publishes supporting materials organized on the same three strands: a "reducing gaps in mathematics" booklet with suggested gan activities and home recommendations, and a resource on developing arithmetic thinking in early childhood. — [Mathematics reducing gaps booklet](https://pop.education.gov.il/kindergarten/magar-ganey-yeladim/mathematics-reducing-gaps-booklet); [Cultivating arithmetic thinking in early childhood](https://pop.education.gov.il/kindergarten/magar-ganey-yeladim/cultivating-arithmetic-thinking-early-childhood/)

In 5787 (2026-27), grade 3 joins the new curriculum. — [Ministry of Education, "The new curriculum"](https://pop.education.gov.il/tchumey_daat/matmatika/yesodi/oraat-math/new-curriculum/)

- Ministry digital broadcast lessons for grade 3 include "exploring the multiplication table" (חקר לוח הכפל), an exploratory, pattern-finding framing rather than pure memorization. — [Ministry VOD lesson, grade 3](https://pop.education.gov.il/sherutey-tiksuv-bachinuch/vod-broadcasts/arabic-21-4/math-arabic-3rd-grade-21-4-10/)
- The publisher Hashbacha's 5786 grade 3 plan assumes at least 4 weekly math hours, including at least 2 weekly hours of geometry and arithmetic, and covers the meaning of multiplication, the commutative property and table-based problem solving. — [Hashbacha grade 3 plan 2025](https://hashbacha.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%A1%D7%AA-%D7%AA%D7%95%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%93%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%9C%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%99-%D7%94%D7%A9%D7%91%D7%97%D7%94-%D7%9B%D7%99%D7%AA%D7%94-%D7%92.pdf); [Hashbacha 5786 plans index](https://hashbacha.org/new_prisot/)
- Commercial Israeli practice workbooks frame the multiplication table 1-10 plus division as grade 3-5 material, which suggests that consolidating the table continues past grade 3 for many children. — [Achiya Yeda: "לוח הכפל בכיף" workbook](https://achiyayeda.org/material/218657/)
- The University of Haifa "Young Mathematician" (המתמטיקאי הצעיר) program offers enrichment for strong students. — [ymath.haifa.ac.il activity](https://ymath.haifa.ac.il/images/stories/part4/activities_new_site/upgraded_openings/upgraded37.pdf); [israelmath.com Young Mathematician 5785](https://israelmath.com/youngmath_courses-5785/)

### Inferences
- **Which curriculum the 8.5-year-old is on:** if the published rollout held, the 3rd-grader is in the first grade-3 cohort on the new curriculum this year (5787), which stresses fluency together with understanding and math talk. The game should be built around both, not drill alone.
- **Number range conflict:** the old Ministry grade 3 document says the range is up to 10,000, while one secondary site says addition and subtraction to 1,000. A plausible reconciliation: number sense, place value and reading or writing numbers go up to 10,000, while column addition and subtraction are drilled mainly to 1,000. The game should support place value to 10,000 and multi-digit addition and subtraction at least to 1,000. This needs checking against the new grade 3 document.
- **Likely grade 3 milestones** (based on the sources above plus general knowledge of the Israeli sequence; not verified line by line): multiplication and division facts within the 10x10 table; the commutative and distributive laws used as fact strategies (for example 7x8 = 7x5 + 7x3); multiplying by 10 and 100; place value to 10,000; multi-step word problems; simple fractions (half, quarter, unit fractions); geometry (polygons, right angles, perimeter); and measurement (length, time, money).
- **Likely common difficulties** (inferred from the multiplication-table focus and the multi-grade workbooks): the "hard facts" (6s, 7s, 8s, x9 patterns), division as the inverse of multiplication, and choosing between multiplication and division in word problems.
- **The 5.5-year-old (gan chova):** the three strands map directly onto game areas: (a) number concept (counting, cardinality, subitizing, comparing numbers, composing and decomposing to 10); (b) spatial sense and geometry (shapes, positions, mental rotation, building); (c) quantitative concepts (comparing length, weight and capacity, measurement language, ordering, patterns).

### Gaps
- I could not read the official new-curriculum documents for grades 1-3 or the gan curriculum in full (government domains were blocked), so I could not confirm exact grade-by-grade objectives: when the times table must be mastered (end of grade 3 vs. grade 4), the fraction scope in grade 3, division with remainder, or the 1,000 vs. 10,000 calculation range.
- No RAMA/Meitzav data on grade 3 common errors was found in this session. Meitzav math is traditionally given in grade 5, not grade 3 (background knowledge, unverified here).
- No Hebrew-specific research on number-word effects was found, for example the gendered number words (שלוש/שלושה) or teen-number structure (שלוש-עשרה), which could affect counting acquisition.

## 2. Core principles and techniques of major math pedagogies

### Takeaway
Across methods, the shared core is: concrete → pictorial → abstract (CPA) progression; part-whole thinking (number bonds, bar models); mastery of small steps before moving on (Singapore, Kumon, Beast Academy); rich problem-centered discussion (the Japanese "structured problem solving" approach with hatsumon and neriage, and Number Talks); and learning trajectories that sequence tasks along children's natural developmental path (Building Blocks). The strongest causal evidence is for Building Blocks and learning-trajectory instruction. Singapore-method effectiveness evidence is mostly promotional or correlational. Montessori results are mixed.

### Cited Findings
**Singapore Math (CPA, number bonds, bar model)**
- The CPA sequence is credited to Jerome Bruner and is essential to maths teaching in Singapore. — [Maths No Problem, "What is Singapore Maths"](https://mathsnoproblem.com/en/approach/what-is-singapore-maths); [Owl Tutors, CPA](https://owltutors.co.uk/cpa-maths-the-building-blocks-of-mathematical-understanding/)
- Number bonds and bar models represent "part-part-whole" relationships. Bar modelling is a visualization strategy that helps children solve word problems systematically. — [Bhanzu, Singapore Math CPA & bar models](https://bhanzu.com/parents/what-is-singapore-math); [Structural Learning, Singapore Maths guide](https://structural-learning.com/post/singapore-math)
- Causal evidence for CPA, bar models and number bonds specifically is thin. The often-cited support is national TIMSS performance (Singapore ranked first in recent TIMSS cycles) and publisher claims for Math in Focus, which are correlational or promotional. — [Maths No Problem](https://mathsnoproblem.com/en/approach/what-is-singapore-maths); [EdReports publisher background (Math in Focus)](https://cdn.edreports.org/series/ag9zfmVkcmVwb3J0cy13ZWJyHgsSCVB1Ymxpc2hlchgGDAsSBlNlcmllcxjh-NU0DA/publisher-background_K-8.pdf); [EdNC on Singapore math](https://www.ednc.org/singapore-math/)

**EEF (UK) guidance, ages 3-7: closest to an evidence-synthesis "method"**
- The EEF "Improving Mathematics in the Early Years and Key Stage 1" guidance (January 2020) has five recommendations: (1) develop practitioners' understanding of how children learn maths (learning trajectories); (2) dedicate time to maths every day; (3) use manipulatives and representations; (4) build on what children already know; (5) give high-quality targeted support to children who struggle. — [EEF news release on early maths guidance](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/storybooks-and-board-games-can-boost-early-maths-skills-new-eef-guidance/); [EEF full guidance report (DERA mirror)](https://dera.ioe.ac.uk//35027/1/EEF_Maths_EY_KS1_Guidance_Report.pdf); [EEF summary of recommendations](https://bso.bradford.gov.uk/userfiles/file/!Learning%20Support/EEF_Maths_EY_KS1_Summary_of_Recommendations.pdf)
- The EEF announced the guidance under the headline that storybooks and board games can boost early maths skills. — [EEF news release](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/storybooks-and-board-games-can-boost-early-maths-skills-new-eef-guidance/)
- On manipulatives, the guidance says children must understand the link between each manipulative and the mathematical idea it represents, manipulatives should prompt discussion, and fingers are an important manipulative to encourage. — [EEF guidance report](https://greatermanchester-ca.gov.uk/media/4105/eef_maths_ey_ks1_guidance_report.pdf); [EEF: manipulatives from familiarity to fluency](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/manipulatives-familiarity-to-fluency)
- A practitioner reading of the guidance (not EEF wording): manipulatives build understanding only when adults point attention to structure ("What stays the same? What changes? What do you notice?"). The companion KS2-3 guidance treats manipulatives as temporary scaffolds to remove once the child is independent. — [Research School, St Matthew's: "More than counting bears"](https://researchschool.org.uk/stmatthews/news/more-than-counting-bears); [Unity Research School commentary](https://researchschool.org.uk/unity/news/my-take-on-the-eefs-improving-mathematics-in-early-years-and-ks1)

**Building Blocks (Clements & Sarama): learning trajectories**
- Learning trajectories are "descriptions of children's thinking and learning… and a related, conjectured route through a set of instructional tasks." — [Building Blocks/TRIAD program descriptions](https://successfulstemeducation.org/sites/default/files/The%20Building%20Blocks%20and%20TRIAD%20Early%20Mathematics%20Projects%20-%20Pogram%20Descriptions.pdf)
- In a 42-school cluster RCT in low-resource communities, Building Blocks preschoolers learned more math than controls (g = 0.72). The quality of the classroom math environment partly mediated the effect. — [JRME 2011, NCTM](https://nctm.org/Publications/Journal-for-Research-in-Mathematics-Education/2011/Vol42/Issue2/Mathematics-Learned-by-Young-Children-in-an-Intervention-Based-on-Learning-Trajectories_-A-Large-Scale-Cluster-Randomized-Trial); [WWC study review](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/Study/77947)
- An earlier 36-classroom study found effect sizes of 0.47 vs. a comparison curriculum and 1.07 vs. control. — [McGraw-Hill hosted 2008 evaluation](https://www.mheducation.com/unitas/school/explore/sites/building-blocks/2008-evaluation-effects-research-based-pk-math.pdf) (publisher-hosted copy of the peer-reviewed AERJ paper)
- The effect fades: two years later, g = 0.28 without follow-through vs. g = 0.51 with follow-through in K-1 (the TRIAD model). — [PMC article on TRIAD long-term effects](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6541454); [TRIAD long-term impacts (ERIC)](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_eric_primary_ED567218)
- The Building Blocks scale-up evaluation is continuing (Duke Center for Child and Family Policy, 2021). — [Duke news release](https://childandfamilypolicy.duke.edu/news-release/evaluating-the-scale-up-of-the-building-blocks-preschool-mathematics-curriculum)

**Japanese approach: structured problem solving, hatsumon, neriage, lesson study**
- Hatsumon means "posing a problem", the key question that launches a lesson. — [MAA review of "Mathematical Thinking: How to Develop it in the Classroom"](https://www.maa.org/press/maa-reviews/mathematical-thinking-how-to-develop-it-in-the-classroom)
- In Stigler and Hiebert's "structured problem solving", whole-class discussion of students' different solutions is the heart of the lesson. The Japanese call it neriage, "polishing" ideas. — [Lesson Research, Characteristics of Japanese Mathematics Lessons](https://lessonresearch.net/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Characteristics-of-Japanese-Mathematics-Lessons-2.pdf); [Takahashi, CRICED Tsukuba presentation](https://www.criced.tsukuba.ac.jp/math/apec/apec2006/pdf/takahashi_ppt_1.pdf)
- Neriage is central to structured problem solving and is a significant factor in developing higher-order mathematical skills. This comes from case-study research, not large trials. — [Hokkaido University of Education repository](https://hokkyodai.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/2000741)

**Montessori math**
- Basargekar & Lillard (2021): studies have not consistently found Montessori programs more effective in math than conventional ones. A Montessori advantage is more likely with high-fidelity programs and longer immersion. — [UVA Early Development Lab, "Math achievement outcomes associated with Montessori education"](https://uva.theopenscholar.com/early-development-lab/publications/math-achievement-outcomes-associated-montessori-education); [Early Child Development and Care article](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/03004430.2020.1860955)
- Laski, Vasilyeva & Schiffman found that Montessori children used different addition strategies in grades 2-3 than non-Montessori children (the decimal and place-value materials such as golden beads). — [Journal of Montessori Research (KU)](https://journals.ku.edu/jmr/issue/download/539/4)

**Kumon and Beast Academy**
- Kumon: repetition of key skills (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division) until mastery, self-paced daily worksheets (about 20 minutes a day), advancing only after consistent accuracy. — [Kumon Method (official)](https://kumon.ac.jp/k-gakuen/kokusai-e/kumonShiKi.html); [National Geographic Kids: what is Kumon](https://mumble.nationalgeographic.com/what-is-kumon); [Thinkster comparison](https://www.hellothinkster.com/blog/kumon-vs-mathnasium-vs-thinkster-which-math-program-actually-works)
- Beast Academy (Art of Problem Solving): a mastery curriculum, one concept at a time and in depth, in a comic-book format. It has less computation drill and more puzzles and non-routine problems, aimed at advanced learners. Critics note it has less spaced review. — [Rainbow Resource, Beast Academy](https://www.rainbowresource.com/category/408/Beast-Academy-by-Art-of-Problem-Solving-Gr.-2-5.html); [Smarter Learning Guide: Beast Academy vs Singapore](https://smarterlearningguide.com/beast-academy-vs-singapore-math)
- I found no controlled studies of Kumon or Beast Academy outcomes. — [Smarter Learning Guide](https://smarterlearningguide.com/beast-academy-vs-singapore-math); [Kumon alternatives blog (MonsterMath)](https://www.monstermath.app/blog/kumon-alternatives-7-better-options)

**Number Talks / Jo Boaler (youcubed)**
- Boaler's central claim: the best way to develop fluency is to develop number sense and to work with numbers in different ways, with facts learned through understanding and use in varied situations. She supports practice and repetition but not blind memorization. — [Stanford GSE, "Learning math without fear"](https://ed.stanford.edu/news/learning-math-without-fear); [youcubed, "Fluency Without Fear"](https://www.youcubed.org/?p=963)

### Inferences
- **For game design, the most actionable method stack is:** (a) Building Blocks or EEF-style learning trajectories as the "level map" for adaptivity; (b) CPA as the representation progression inside each skill (virtual objects → ten-frames, dots and bars → numerals and equations); (c) Singapore number bonds and bar models as the main visual tools for addition, subtraction, multiplication, division and word problems; (d) Japanese hatsumon and neriage style "one rich problem, many solutions, compare them" for the 8.5-year-old; (e) Kumon-style small, mastery-gated increments for fact practice, with spacing and interleaving added (see section 4).
- The fade-out of Building Blocks effects without follow-through argues for continuity: the same trajectory framework should carry from the toddler through the 3rd-grader. That favors one app shared by the siblings with one progression model.
- **From general knowledge (not verified in this session):** Estonia and Finland, top European PISA performers, are often credited with teacher autonomy, late formal schooling (age 7) and heavy play-based early years rather than a branded math method. I found no specific Finnish or Estonian math-method sources, so this is listed as a gap.

### Gaps
- No primary sources were retrieved on Finnish or Estonian math pedagogy.
- No peer-reviewed RCT was found isolating the effect of bar-model or CPA instruction. A US WWC practice guide on CRA (concrete-representational-abstract) for struggling students likely exists but was not retrieved.
- Singapore MOE primary syllabus (P1-P3) details were not retrieved. A grade-by-grade Singapore comparison would help benchmark the Israeli sequence.
- Japanese lesson study effect sizes (for example, US lesson-study RCTs by Lewis & Perry) were not retrieved.

## 3. Evidence-based learning trajectories for early numeracy (toddlers 2-3; ages 5-6)

### Takeaway
Toddlers move through predictable "knower levels". Around 2-2.5 years most are "one-knowers" who can reliably give exactly one object. Verbal cardinality (understanding that "three" names the total) begins around 2.5 years, and children then become two-, three- and four-knowers before grasping the cardinal principle. Subitizing (recognizing small sets of 1, 2, 3 without counting) is the earliest route into number words. For ages 5-6, the highest-leverage evidence-based activity is linear number board games, which build an accurate mental number line (Siegler & Ramani). Composing and decomposing to 10, spatial reasoning, and daily maths with manipulatives (EEF) complete the picture.

### Cited Findings
**Toddlers (2-3): counting, cardinality, subitizing**
- On the Give-N task, most 2-year-olds give one object when asked for one but no consistent amount for larger numbers ("one-knowers"). Verbal cardinality generally begins around 2.5 years. — [NSF PAR: Measuring emerging number knowledge in toddlers](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10278121-measuring-emerging-number-knowledge-toddlers); [NSF PAR paper](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10401756)
- Small-number recognition by subitizing (seeing the total without counting and linking it to a number word) is typically the child's first way of naming small quantities: "one" first, then "two", then "three". Some researchers argue "two" may come with or before "one". — [Springer, Educational Studies in Mathematics (2022)](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/doi/10.1007/s10649-022-10153-5)
- Cardinal-principle knowledge develops gradually. Children show partial cardinal knowledge before becoming cardinal-principle knowers. The stable-order and one-to-one (word-object) correspondence principles, two of Gelman & Gallistel's counting principles, likely emerge earlier. — [Developmental Science 2022 (desc.13219)](https://zendy.io/title/10.1111/desc.13219); [Orbi ULiege](https://orbi.uliege.be/handle/2268/262198)
- The standard Give-N task may substantially underestimate children's counting-based cardinal knowledge. — [Springer, Educational Studies in Mathematics (2022)](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/doi/10.1007/s10649-022-10153-5)
- Children's acquisition of numerical knowledge in the early years has also been studied in PMC literature on number-word learning. — [PMC3110717](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3110717)

**Ages 4-6: number line, magnitude, board games**
- Siegler & Ramani (2008): four 15-minute sessions of a simple linear numbered board game (squares 1-10) eliminated income-related differences in numerical estimation among preschoolers. The same game with colors in place of numbers had no effect. — [Siegler & Ramani 2008, Developmental Science](https://onpurposely.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/sieg-ram08.pdf); [Developmental Science 11(5):655-661](https://bibliotheek.ehb.be:2102/10.1111/j.1467-7687.2008.00714.x)
- Siegler & Ramani (2009): a linear board produced more learning of numerical magnitude (number-line estimation and magnitude comparison) than a circular board. The proposed reason is that a linear layout matches the mental number line. WWC rated the RCT as meeting standards without reservations. — [WWC study review 73725](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/study/73725)
- A review of Ramani & Siegler's work reports that effects persisted 9 weeks after play, with effect sizes of about 0.55 to 0.80. This comes from a secondary source and was not checked against the primary tables. — [Laski & Siegler 2013 (CMU)](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/psychology/cs/research-teaching/docs/2013LaskiSieglerNumberBoardGames.pdf); [EdUHK review PDF](https://www.lib.eduhk.hk/pure-data/pub/201710711.pdf)
- Laski & Siegler (2013) found that how children move matters: counting on from the current number ("6, 7, 8"), rather than counting "1, 2, 3" for each move, improves learning from the board game. — [Laski & Siegler 2013, CMU](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/psychology/cs/research-teaching/docs/2013LaskiSieglerNumberBoardGames.pdf) (title confirmed; detail on the counting-on condition is from background knowledge of this paper and should be checked)
- A 2025 meta-analysis in *Review of Educational Research* covered linear number board games: 18 studies from preschool to grade 2 with 123 effect sizes, examining main effects and moderators. — [Meta-analytic review listing](https://metatoc.com/papers/171624-investigating-main-effects-and-moderators-of-linear-number-board-games-a-meta-analytic-review)
- An IES-funded project, "Improving children's pure numerical estimation", extends this number-line work. — [IES award](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/awards/improving-childrens-pure-numerical-estimation)

### Inferences
**Trajectory for the 2.5-year-old** (sequence synthesized from the knower-level and subitizing findings plus the EEF and Building Blocks framing):
1. Perceptual subitizing of 1-3 ("show me two ducks").
2. Give-N with 1, 2, 3 (the game asks the child to "give the bunny exactly 2 carrots").
3. Stable-order counting to 5 and then 10 (songs and chants).
4. One-to-one tapping (each tap lights up one object).
5. Cardinality ("how many?" answered with the last number said; the game confirms by circling the whole set).
6. Comparing small sets (more/less) and sizes (big/small, long/short).
7. Shape names and matching (circle, square, triangle), simple AB patterns.

Expect the cardinal principle to arrive sometime in ages 3-4. The game should not push numerals beyond 5 before cardinality is solid.

**Trajectory for the 5.5-year-old** (gan chova), mapped to the three Israeli gan strands:
- Number concept:
  - Counting to 20-30+ and counting on.
  - Conceptual subitizing of 5-10 (dice, fingers and ten-frame patterns: "5 and 2 more").
  - Numeral recognition to 20.
  - Number line 0-10 and then 0-20 (board-game style).
  - Composing and decomposing numbers to 10 (number bonds, "how many are hiding?").
  - Early addition and subtraction stories.
- Spatial sense and geometry: shape properties, composing shapes (tangram-like puzzles), position words, mental rotation.
- Quantitative concepts: compare length, weight and capacity; order by size; repeating and growing patterns.

**Trajectory for the 8.5-year-old:** see section 1. Number-line estimation should extend to 0-1000 and 0-10,000, and to fractions on a number line, because whole-number and fraction magnitude knowledge both build on the linear representation.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the full Clements & Sarama learning-trajectory tables with age ranges per level (for example subitizing levels and counting levels).
- I did not retrieve primary data on typical ages for the cardinal principle (commonly cited as about 3.5-4.5; unverified here).
- I did not retrieve primary research on spatial-reasoning interventions (for example mental rotation training effects on math) in this session.

## 4. Math fact fluency (addition and multiplication facts): strategy-based vs. rote, timed tests, spaced practice; math anxiety

### Takeaway
Fluency is "efficient, appropriate and flexible" use of facts, not speed alone (Baroody). Practitioner and research consensus favors strategy-based fact instruction (derived-fact strategies such as doubles, make-10 and the distributive split) followed by low-stakes, untimed or self-paced retrieval practice. Practice should be spaced and interleaved. The interleaving RCT evidence is strong (d = 0.83 in grade 7, Rohrer et al. 2020). High-pressure timed tests are linked to anxiety and working-memory disruption, though the evidence for that link is partly advocacy-driven. Parents' math anxiety transmits to children through homework help. Low-pressure, story-based math at home (Bedtime Math) eliminated that gap.

### Cited Findings
**Fluency definition and strategy-based instruction**
- Baroody (2006) defines basic fact fluency as "the efficient, appropriate, and flexible application of single-digit" calculation skills. Speed is only one component. — [Plainville district addition strategies doc quoting Baroody](https://www.plainville.k12.ma.us/cms/lib/MA01000200/Centricity/Domain/253/PR%20Addition_Strategies_FINAL_8-22-14.pdf)
- NCTM *Teaching Children Mathematics* (2014) says timed tests offer little insight into how flexibly students use strategies, and suggests timed assessments and drill may not be necessary for fact mastery. — [NCTM TCM 2014, Assessing basic fact fluency](https://www.nctm.org/Publications/teaching-children-mathematics/2014/Vol20/Issue8/tcm2014-04-488a_pdf/); [PSU-hosted copy](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.psu.edu/dist/3/1552/files/2018/07/assessing-basic-fact-fluency-TCM-yxaog2.pdf)
- An NCTM regional presentation (2015) cites research that timed testing led to less progress toward knowing facts from memory. This is secondhand, with no sample or effect size available. — [NCTM 2015 regional addition-facts handout](https://nctm.confex.com/nctm/2015MN/webprogram/Handout/Session34818/Addition%20Facts%20NCTM%20Regional%202015%20PDF%20of%20Powerpoint.pdf)
- Summaries claim strategy-based fact instruction produces greater long-term retention than rote memorization. These are secondary summaries without primary citations. — [Math Fact Lab research summary](https://mathfactlab.helpscoutdocs.com/article/286-what-the-research-says-strategy-based-math-fact-instruction-versus-traditional-rote-memorization)
- Counterpoint: avoiding timed tests does not mean facts are unimportant. Fluency remains essential, and some describe best practice as "highly debated". — [Edutopia, guiding students to learn math facts](https://www.edutopia.org/article/guiding-students-learn-math-facts); [core.ac.uk action research paper](https://core.ac.uk/download/217206724.pdf)

**Timed tests, anxiety and working memory**
- Boaler: for about a third of students, the start of timed testing marks the beginning of math anxiety, and math anxiety has been recorded in children as young as 5. — [Stanford GSE, "Learning math without fear"](https://ed.stanford.edu/news/learning-math-without-fear); [youcubed, Fluency Without Fear](https://www.youcubed.org/?p=963)
- Boaler, citing Beilock: stress impairs working memory, where math facts are held, so stressed students cannot reach facts they actually know. — [Education Week opinion, "Timed tests and the development of math anxiety"](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/opinion-timed-tests-and-the-development-of-math-anxiety/2012/07)
- Counterpoint: the Hechinger Report agrees timed tests are inappropriate because of the stress, but supports untimed daily fact practice. It notes Boaler's influence may have outrun the strength of her evidence. Boaler herself concedes that not everyone is harmed by timed quizzes. — [Hechinger Report](https://hechingerreport.org/?p=19322); [Washington Monthly, "Should we stop making kids memorize times tables?"](https://washingtonmonthly.com/2015/02/09/should-we-stop-making-kids-memorize-times-tables/)

**Spaced and interleaved practice**
- Rohrer, Dedrick, Hartwig & Cheung (2020), *Journal of Educational Psychology* 112:40-52, ran a cluster RCT in 54 grade 7 classes. On an unannounced delayed test, the interleaved-practice group scored 61% vs. 38% for blocked practice (d = 0.83). Both groups saw the same problems; only the order differed. WWC: meets standards without reservations. — [WWC Study 80702](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/Study/80702); [Rohrer et al. 2019/2020 PDF](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2019-rohrer.pdf); [Structural Learning research briefing](https://www.structural-learning.com/research-briefings/mix-it-up)
- Emeny, Hartwig & Rohrer (2021): spaced mathematics practice improves test scores and reduces overconfidence (*Applied Cognitive Psychology* 35:1082-1089). Effect size not retrieved. — [Rohrer replication project page](https://www.neilheffernan.net/projects/funded-projects/the-matlen-rohrer-replication-project)
- IES-funded replications of interleaved practice are under way, including a WestEd-led replication and a replication on digital platforms (ASSISTments). — [IES efficacy study award](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/awards/efficacy-study-interleaved-mathematics-practice); [IES systematic replication award](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/awards/systematic-replication-study-interleaved-mathematics-practice)

**Math anxiety and parents**
- Children of highly math-anxious parents learned less math across the school year, and were more likely to develop math anxiety, but only when those parents helped often with homework. Children who got no such help did as well as peers. — [KQED MindShift](https://www.kqed.org/mindshift/52749/how-to-make-sure-your-math-anxiety-doesnt-make-your-kids-hate-math); [Education Week, "Breaking the link between parents' math anxiety and students' progress"](https://edweek.org/leadership/breaking-the-link-between-parents-math-anxiety-and-students-progress/2018/12)
- Families who used the Bedtime Math app (math stories plus questions) at least once a week erased this disadvantage. A three-year follow-up (grades 1-3) found children of anxious parents caught up with peers. Usage was low (often less than weekly). The proposed mechanism was parents' higher expectations and valuing of math, not a cure for parental anxiety. — [KQED MindShift](https://www.kqed.org/mindshift/52749/how-to-make-sure-your-math-anxiety-doesnt-make-your-kids-hate-math); [Chicago Maroon](https://chicagomaroon.com/?p=20968); [MI Sci Writers](https://misciwriters.com/2015/12/08/terrified-by-math-dont-pass-it-on/)
- Sources disagree on venue. The original study ("Math at home adds up to achievement in school") was in *Science* (October 2015); the three-year follow-up appeared in *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* (2018). — [Chicago Maroon](https://chicagomaroon.com/?p=20968); [MPR News/NPR](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2018/12/16/npr-make-sure-your-math-anxiety-doesnt-make-your-kids-hate-math)

### Inferences
**Fact-fluency design** for the 8.5-year-old's times tables and the 5.5-year-old's facts to 10:
1. Teach each fact family through a strategy first, using CPA visuals:
   - x2 as doubles; x4 as double-double; x5 from the clock or half of x10; x9 as x10 minus one group.
   - 7x8 via the distributive split 7x5 + 7x3. This matches the Israeli emphasis on חוק הפילוג.
   - Commutativity halves the facts to learn.
2. Then practice retrieval with adaptive spacing: Leitner or spaced-repetition per fact, interleaving old and new families.
3. Measure fluency privately (response time per fact) without visible countdown timers.
4. Optionally offer a "beat your own time" mode that the child chooses, never a forced timer.

**Anxiety-safe design:**
- No public leaderboards across siblings; personal progress only.
- Wrong answers trigger an immediate hint or visual model rather than a penalty.
- A "parent co-play" mode modeled on Bedtime Math (short story problems the parent reads aloud) supports home math talk without homework pressure.

**Sibling dynamics:** the 8.5-year-old could act as a "teacher" helping the 5.5-year-old (learning by teaching). This is a design idea, not evidence-based in these sources.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve Baroody's or Henry & Brown's (2008) primary studies, or quantitative results comparing strategy-based vs. rote multiplication instruction in grades 2-4.
- I did not retrieve primary Beilock/Ramirez studies on math anxiety in grades 1-2 (Ramirez et al. 2013 is commonly cited; not verified here).
- I found no study specifically testing spaced practice for single-digit fact retrieval in young children. The interleaving evidence is from grade 7.

## 5. Digital math tools with evidence: what worked, effect sizes, mechanics

### Takeaway
The best independently validated digital tool is ST Math. Its effect size is about 0.13-0.17 in the independent SRI review of the cross-state study, compared with 0.36-0.47 in MIND-reported subgroups of consistent or fully implementing schools. Zearn has an ESSA "Strong" rating but a small average effect (+0.09). Board-game-style number-line games have RCT support (0.55-0.80 reported). The Number Race had only a small open trial with near-transfer gains (subitizing, comparison). Reflex and Prodigy evidence is mostly vendor or correlational. Khan Academy Kids' RCT (effect size 0.72) was on pre-literacy, not math. I found no rigorous DragonBox outcome study. Effect sizes shrink sharply when moving from vendor to independent studies and from fully implementing to all users, so dosage and consistency matter.

### Cited Findings
**ST Math (MIND Research Institute): visual, language-light spatial-temporal puzzles with the JiJi penguin character**
- National WestEd study (grades 3-5, 474 schools, 16 states): effect size about 0.36 on statewide math ranking for consistent implementers (one MIND infographic says 0.35). Across all 80,000 ST Math students regardless of implementation, the effect was 0.17. — [MIND: WestEd national study](https://2024.mindresearch.org/blog/national-study-significant-math-results); [MIND infographic](https://www.mindeducation.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Infographic_WestEd_Study.pdf)
- California 2014 study (grades 2-5): effect size 0.47 (Education Week reported 0.42 on 2010-11 scores). "Full implementation" meant at least 85% of students logged in and at least 50% of grade-level content completed. — [STMath WestEd infographic](https://stmath.com/hubfs/STMath/PDF/WestEd-Study-Infographic-web.pdf); [Education Week 2014](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/ed-tech-math-program-boosts-elementary-student-achievement-study-says/2014/11); [MIND: WestEd study on CA students](https://2024.mindresearch.org/news/wested-study-edtechs-impact-on-ca-students)
- Los Angeles Unified (2013), high-need schools: effect size 0.41. — [MIND: WestEd LA study](https://2024.mindresearch.org/news/wested-lastudy)
- SRI International review of the cross-state study: +0.13 on scale scores and +0.17 on percent proficient, which is moderate evidence (ESSA Tier 2) for grades 3-5. The review was commissioned by MIND. — [SRI review](https://sri.com/?p=12003)
- The What Works Clearinghouse treats 0.25 or more as "substantively important". — [MIND: national study](https://blog.mindresearch.org/blog/national-study-significant-math-results)

**Zearn Math**
- Evidence for ESSA (Johns Hopkins): "Strong" (Tier 1), 3 studies, 22,575 students, average effect size +0.09. The RCT found +0.11 on NWEA MAP (STAAR result non-significant). A Louisiana supplemental-use study found +0.20 on LEAP. Zearn's internal reports claim larger effects (0.27). — [Evidence for ESSA, Zearn](https://www.evidenceforessa.org/?p=1310); [Zearn: Evidence for ESSA press release](https://about.zearn.org/press-releases/evidence-for-essa); [Zearn effectiveness page](https://about.zearn.org/insights/zearn-math-effectiveness); [Journal of Research on Educational Effectiveness 2023](https://www.informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/19345747.2023.2174919)

**Prodigy Math (RPG fantasy game with math questions to cast spells)**
- Self-reported ESSA Tier 3 based on correlational usage-outcome findings. Its 2025 Florida and Pennsylvania reports are year-over-year comparisons against state averages, not controlled designs. I found no independent effect size. — [Prodigy blog "Prodigy works"](https://www.prodigygame.com/blog/prodigy-works); [Prodigy Pennsylvania efficacy white paper](https://marketing.prodigygame.com/Pennsylvania-Efficacy-Report-White-Paper.pdf); [Prodigy Florida white paper](https://marketing.prodigygame.com/Florida-Efficacy-Report-White-Paper.pdf)

**Khan Academy Kids**
- RCT (Arnold et al. 2021, *Journal of Children and Media*): 49 low-income 4-5-year-olds, 10 weeks, about 13-20 minutes a day. TOPEL pre-literacy scores rose from the 34th to the 47th percentile (effect size 0.72). The outcome was pre-literacy, not math, and the sample was small; the study was funded by the Overdeck Foundation. — [Khan Academy blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/khan-academy-kids-improves-pre-literacy-skills-in-preschoolers-research-confirms/); [Journal of Children and Media listing](https://www.x-mol.com/paper/1349122282629132288); [Khan Kids help center](https://khankids.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042832631-Research-from-University-of-Massachusetts-Amherst-shows-Khan-Academy-Kids-boosts-pre-literacy-skills)

**The Number Race (Wilson, Dehaene et al., 2006): adaptive number-comparison race game for dyscalculia**
- Design principles: an adaptive algorithm keeps the child near 75% success (a multidimensional difficulty space of numerical distance, speed deadline and conceptual complexity). It links symbolic and non-symbolic quantity (dots ↔ digits ↔ spoken words) and moves along a linear board to reinforce the number line. — [Wilson et al. 2006, design principles (Behavioral and Brain Functions)](https://behavioralandbrainfunctions.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1744-9081-2-19); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16734905/) (the 75% target and difficulty dimensions are from background knowledge of this paper; the paper title and design-principles focus are confirmed)
- Open trial, 9 children with math difficulties, 5 weeks: improvements in number comparison, subitizing speed and subtraction. There was no change in the counting range, and the authors called for larger controlled studies. — [Wilson et al. 2006, open trial (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1523349/)
- A later review notes limited transfer to counting or arithmetic. — [Frontiers in Psychology 2013](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00489/full)

**Reflex Math (adaptive fact-fluency games)**
- Vendor claims: 35,000+ students in 150+ schools. Second graders two or more grade levels behind gained 11 percentile points vs. 5 for non-users. — [ExploreLearning Reflex](https://www.explorelearning.com/our-products/reflex); [Reflex insights](https://reflex.explorelearning.com/resources/insights/achieve-math-fact-fluency-efficiently)
- An independent grade 4 action-research study found Reflex improved fact fluency and math attitudes, but not standardized STAR Math scores. — [Touro Scholar, Millward](https://touroscholar.touro.edu/tucgsoe/162/); [Walden dissertation](https://scholarworks.waldenu.edu/dissertations/7626)

**DragonBox**
- I found no randomized outcome study of DragonBox (Elements, Numbers, Big Numbers) with children. The only result was a 2018 dissertation on preservice teachers (no significant effect on teaching confidence). — [Georgia State ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.gsu.edu/items/ddedb5ab-ff6c-4806-b284-c822bee6e10b)

### Inferences
**Mechanics shared by the tools with the best evidence:**
1. Visual, language-light representations, which suit a Hebrew-speaking pre-reader and toddler (ST Math's JiJi puzzles show the result of the child's answer as an animation instead of saying "wrong").
2. Adaptive difficulty targeting a high but not perfect success rate (The Number Race).
3. A linear number-line layout (board games, The Number Race).
4. Consistent dosage: ST Math effects depended on 85% logins and 50% content completion, and Building Blocks effects faded without follow-through. The game should engineer daily habits (short 10-20 minute sessions, streaks that forgive missed days).

**Extrinsic-reward games** like Prodigy, where math is a gate to unrelated fantasy combat, have the weakest causal evidence. Mechanics where the math is the game action (moving along a number line, building with bars) are more consistent with the ST Math and board-game findings ("intrinsic integration"; I found no direct source for the comparison in this session).

**Expected effect sizes:** a realistic expectation for a well-designed supplemental game is small-to-moderate (about 0.1-0.4 on broad measures), with larger effects on the specific trained skills (for example number-line estimation or fact fluency).

### Gaps
- Not retrieved: Math Shelf (Agostino/Sarama-type tablet preschool intervention) efficacy data, Khan Academy Kids math-specific outcomes, DragonBox Numbers or Big Numbers studies, and the Number Catcher (Dehaene/Wilson follow-up) evaluation.
- Not retrieved: a systematic meta-analysis of digital game-based math learning for ages 3-8 (for example by Hillmayr et al. 2020 or Cheung & Slavin) that would give pooled effect sizes.
- Not retrieved: evidence on Hebrew-language math apps used in Israel (for example Ministry-recommended times-table apps, or local platforms such as Matific, which originated in Israel).

## 6. Concrete game mechanic ideas derived from these methods

### Takeaway
The most evidence-aligned mechanics are:
- Linear number-line movement with counting on (Siegler & Ramani; Laski & Siegler).
- Adaptive magnitude comparison linking dots, digits and number words (The Number Race).
- Virtual manipulatives with explicit links to symbols (EEF recommendation 3; CPA).
- Part-whole puzzles: number bonds and bar models (Singapore).
- Spaced, interleaved, untimed fact retrieval with strategy hints (Rohrer; Baroody; Boaler).
- Visual "see the consequence" feedback (ST Math).
- One-problem-many-solutions discussion prompts (Japanese neriage; Number Talks).
- Low-pressure parent co-play stories (Bedtime Math).

### Cited Findings
- **Linear board race:** the number on each square must be said aloud while moving. Colors in place of numbers gave no benefit. A linear layout beat a circular one. — [Siegler & Ramani 2008](https://onpurposely.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/sieg-ram08.pdf); [WWC 73725](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/study/73725)
- **Manipulatives:** must be explicitly linked to the math idea, used to drive talk, and include fingers. They are a scaffold to fade. — [EEF guidance report](https://dera.ioe.ac.uk//35027/1/EEF_Maths_EY_KS1_Guidance_Report.pdf); [Research School St Matthew's](https://researchschool.org.uk/stmatthews/news/more-than-counting-bears)
- **Board games and storybooks** are endorsed by the EEF as early maths boosters. — [EEF news](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/storybooks-and-board-games-can-boost-early-maths-skills-new-eef-guidance/)
- **Interleaving:** mixing problem types instead of blocking them roughly doubles delayed retention in the RCT (61% vs. 38%). — [WWC 80702](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/Study/80702)
- **Adaptive number comparison and subitizing training** improves comparison and subitizing speed. — [Number Race open trial](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1523349/)
- **Math talk at home** through short story problems with a parent closes anxiety-related gaps. — [KQED MindShift](https://www.kqed.org/mindshift/52749/how-to-make-sure-your-math-anxiety-doesnt-make-your-kids-hate-math)
- **Exploring the multiplication table for patterns** is used in Israeli Ministry grade 3 lessons. — [Ministry VOD lesson](https://pop.education.gov.il/sherutey-tiksuv-bachinuch/vod-broadcasts/arabic-21-4/math-arabic-3rd-grade-21-4-10/)

### Inferences
These are design proposals derived from the findings above, organized by child.

**Toddler (2.5): "Animal Friends" (no reading, voice-led in Hebrew, big touch targets)**
- **"Feed exactly N"** (Give-N): an animal asks for "שתיים" carrots and the child drags carrots; the animal counts aloud as each one arrives. Start at 1-2, then 3. Builds cardinality.
- **"Peek-a-boo dots"** (subitizing): 1-3 dots or animals flash briefly, then the child taps the matching group or says the number word. No counting is possible, so it trains instant recognition.
- **"Tap to count"** (one-to-one): each tap lights up one object and voices the next number; at the end a lasso circles the set while the voice says "שלושה! יש שלושה" (the cardinal word).
- **Big/small and long/short sorting**; shape matching (put the shape into the hole); AB color patterns ("what comes next?").
- **Songs and chants** for stable-order counting to 10.
- **Feedback:** no fail states; wrong choices get a gentle re-demonstration.

**Kindergartner (5.5, gan chova): "Number Path Adventures" (voice plus numerals)**
- **Linear number-line board race** 1-10, then 1-20, then 1-100. A spinner or die (subitized dot faces) sets the move. The child must count on from the current square while moving the token. This is the core Siegler & Ramani mechanic.
- **Number-line estimation:** "where does 7 live?" on a 0-10, then 0-20 line, with the true position revealed by a hopping character. Error distance gives magnitude feedback.
- **Ten-frame "Fill the bus":** 10 seats; "how many more to fill?" builds make-10 and number bonds of 10.
- **Number bond "hiding animals":** 7 bunnies, some go into a hut, 3 are outside; how many are hiding? This is part-whole decomposition and missing addends.
- **Comparison duel** (adapted from The Number Race): pick the bigger of two quantities, shown as dot sets vs. digits vs. spoken words. An adaptive distance makes 8 vs. 9 harder than 2 vs. 9.
- **Spatial:** tangram or pattern-block silhouette filling, rotate-to-fit puzzles, position-word games ("put the cat under the table").
- **Measurement:** compare lengths with non-standard units (how many paper clips long?), balance scales.
- **Patterns:** repeating, then growing.
- **Fingers:** the game prompts "show me 6 on your fingers" using the device camera, or self-report (EEF: fingers are a manipulative).

**3rd-grader (8.5): "Math Quest" (strategy plus fluency plus problem solving)**
- **Times-table "strategy forge":**
  - Each fact family is unlocked by a visual strategy lesson: array or area model, splitting with the distributive law (חוק הפילוג), commutative flip, x10 then subtract for x9.
  - Then spaced-repetition retrieval: the system tracks latency per fact, shows no visible timer, and interleaves old and new families.
  - The 10x10 multiplication grid (לוח הכפל) is a "map" the child colors in as facts become fluent. This visualizes progress and also lets the child "explore" patterns (diagonal symmetry, x9 digit sums).
- **Division as missing factor:** "12 cookies in 3 boxes" bar-model puzzles, then fact triangles that link x and ÷.
- **Bar-model word-problem builder:** the child drags bars to represent a story ("Dana has 3 times as many stickers as Yoni…") before computing. The CPA pictorial step is mandatory before the equation is entered.
- **Place-value bank** (Montessori golden-bead style, digital): ones, tens, hundreds and thousands tokens; trading 10 for 1 in exchange animations; numbers to 10,000 on a zoomable number line.
- **Fractions:** cut a pizza or chocolate bar into equal parts; place 1/2, 1/4 and 3/4 on a number line, building fraction magnitude on the same line metaphor.
- **"Number Talk of the day"** (Boaler, Japanese neriage): one dot image or computation (for example 18x5) that the child solves 2-3 different ways; the game shows other ways "another kid" solved it and asks which they like. This is the hatsumon-plus-neriage loop in miniature.
- **Geometry:** perimeter garden fencing, right-angle hunts, polygon sorting.
- **Measurement:** analog clock setting, shekel/agorot money shop.

**Cross-cutting systems**
- **Learning-trajectory skill graph** (Building Blocks, EEF recommendation 1): every activity is tagged to a trajectory level. Mastery gating follows Kumon-style small steps, and spaced review follows Rohrer-style interleaving.
- **Adaptive difficulty** targeting about 70-85% success (Number Race-inspired).
- **Session design:** 10-15 minutes a day (Kumon, Khan Kids and ST Math dosage findings) with habit streaks; consistency drives effect size.
- **Anxiety-safe defaults:**
  - No cross-sibling leaderboards.
  - Speed is measured silently.
  - Optional "challenge my own record" mode.
  - Wrong answers lead to a visual model or hint (ST Math-style animated consequences).
- **Parent dashboard plus "bedtime math story" co-play** for each child's level, read aloud in Hebrew (Bedtime Math).
- **Sibling co-op:** the older child can "teach" or help the younger one in shared board-race mode. The race mechanic works at different number ranges for each child on the same board.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence was found comparing intrinsic integration (math as the core game action) vs. extrinsic rewards (math gating unrelated play) in young children within this session's sources. This is commonly argued (for example Habgood & Ainsworth 2011) but not verified here.
- No evidence was retrieved on optimal session length or frequency for ages 2-3 on touchscreens. Screen-time guidance for under-3s, such as AAP recommendations for co-viewing, is relevant to the toddler mode but was outside this search.
- No studies were found on voice or speech-recognition input for Hebrew-speaking preschoolers in math apps.
