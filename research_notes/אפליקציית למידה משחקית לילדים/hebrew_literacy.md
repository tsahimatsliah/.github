## 1. What does the Israeli Ministry of Education curriculum specify for gan and grades 1-3? What are the milestones?

### Takeaway
In 2025 the Ministry published a new unified language-and-literacy curriculum covering gan through grades 1-2, built on three strands: communicative competence, reading/writing acquisition, and understanding/producing texts of different genres. The hard milestones are:
- pointed (menukad) decoding acquired within about half of grade 1, and by the end of grade 1 at the latest;
- fluency keeps developing through grades 2-3;
- deliberate work on unpointed text (ktiv male) starts from mid-grade 3.

### Cited Findings
- **New continuum curriculum (2025):** a new curriculum for teaching language and literacy along the gan-to-grades 1-2 continuum was published in 2025. It sets goals and suggested activities in three areas: communicative competence (כשירות תקשורתית), acquisition of reading and writing (רכישת הקריאה והכתיבה), and understanding and producing texts of various genres (הבנה והפקה של טקסטים מסוגות שונות). — [Ministry curriculum page (search snippet)](https://pop.education.gov.il/tchumey_daat/ivrit_chinhch_leshony/yesodi/pedagogy-hebrew-linguistic-education/curriculum/); [Program PDF](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Pop/0files/ivrit_hinuch_leshoni/Yesodi/tohnit-limudim-safa.pdf)
- **Who wrote it:** the committee was chaired by Prof. Michal Shany (מיכל שני), appointed by the head of the Pedagogical Secretariat. It was decided to write one joint program so the developmental continuum is visible; a 2022 document presented this idea. The program explicitly covers children whose home language is not Hebrew and children with special needs. — [Ministry curriculum page (search snippet)](https://pop.education.gov.il/tchumey_daat/ivrit_chinhch_leshony/yesodi/pedagogy-hebrew-linguistic-education/curriculum/); [Retzef report](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Mazkirut_Pedagogit/HebrewPrimary/mafmardesk/retzef-report.pdf)
- **Gan's role:** the preschool curriculum names "developing a foundation toward acquiring reading and writing" (פיתוח תשתית לקראת רכישת יכולת קריאה וכתיבה) as a central goal of gan. It does this while keeping experiential learning and "the spirit of the gan" (רוח הגן). — [Kindergarten "tashtit kria" page (search snippet)](https://pop.education.gov.il/kindergarten/magar-ganey-yeladim/tashtit-kria)
- **Gap-closing kit for gan:** a Ministry booklet, "צמצום פערים בשפה ואוריינות" (closing gaps in language and literacy), gives the gannenet (kindergarten teacher) activities for the gan space and recommends activities for home. — [Ministry gan page](https://pop.education.gov.il/kindergarten/magar-ganey-yeladim/1closing-gaps)
- **Grade-1 target date:** the 2025-26 (תשפ"ו) chief-inspector letter for grade-1 Hebrew says acquisition of reading and writing in the pointed system should finish within about half a year, and by the end of grade 1 at the latest. Spelling develops in parallel with reading. — [Iggeret ha-mafmar, grade 1, 2025-26](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Pop/0files/ivrit_hinuch_leshoni/Yesodi/horaa.pdf)
- **What grade-1 reading rests on:** reading at this stage is phonological decoding, meaning converting letter + vowel-sign combinations (צירופים) into sound. Alphabetic knowledge is completed toward the end of the year, and fast, efficient decoding of these combinations becomes the base for comprehension. — [Iggeret ha-mafmar, grade 1](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Pop/0files/ivrit_hinuch_leshoni/Yesodi/horaa.pdf)
- **Older binding end-of-grade-1 achievements:** an older director-general circular lists fluent, fast decoding of isolated words, and producing the vowel sounds of all nikud signs. — [Ministry circular, "required achievements at end of grade 1" (older)](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/special/pedagogy/hl3.doc)
- **End-of-grade-1 fluency test:** the Ministry fluency check uses a pointed text ("השבלול", about 77 words). It scores accuracy (number of errors), rate (reading time in seconds) and words per minute. — [Grade-1 reading & writing test, teacher guide](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Yesodi/ivrit/madrich.pdf); [Test instructions](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Yesodi/ivrit/hanchayot.pdf)
- **Fluency keeps growing:** the Ministry's fluency program says reading ability develops across elementary school, does not end in grade 1, and fluency keeps improving after grade 3. — [Ministry reading-fluency program](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Mazkirut_Pedagogit/HebrewPrimary/program/reading-fluency.pdf)
- **Grades 2-3 program "צומחים מחדש" (2025-26):** it targets all grade 2-3 students. "Starting mid-grade 3 we will develop reading of unpointed full spelling" (החל מאמצע כיתה ג נפתח את יכולות הקריאה של הכתיב המלא שאינו מנוקד). It includes a teacher's guide, student texts in two versions (pointed and unpointed), and cloze (fill-the-word) activities to build fluency and language knowledge. — [Tzomchim Mechadash, grades 2-3, 2025-26](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Pop/0files/ivrit_hinuch_leshoni/Yesodi/growing-again-2-3g.pdf)
- **Ministry graded assessment texts:** the texts come in pointed and unpointed versions. Milestone markers include a pointed text for mid-grade 3 and an unpointed text for end of grade 3. Teachers are advised to compare a child's performance on the pointed and unpointed versions of the same text. — [Ministry assessment texts](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Mazkirut_Pedagogit/HebrewPrimary/assessment/attache2-texts.pdf)
- **Research-based view of grade 1:** decoding is necessary but not sufficient, so comprehension and language should be developed alongside it from grade 1. This is a lecturer's position, not official policy. — [MOFET portal article](https://portal.macam.ac.il/article/%D7%94%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%A7%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%90%D7%94-%D7%91%D7%9B%D7%99%D7%AA%D7%94-%D7%90-%D7%9C%D7%90%D7%9F/)

### Inferences
- **Age-to-milestone mapping for the game:**
  - 2.5-year-old: oral language, vocabulary and "tashtit" (foundations) only.
  - 5.5-year-old (gan chova): phonological awareness (syllables, then the initial CV, then phonemes), letter-sound knowledge, emergent/invented writing of the name and simple words. Formal decoding is not required, but early decoding is fine if the child is ready.
  - 8.5-year-old (grade 3): pointed fluency (words per minute, accuracy) consolidating; unpointed reading should be the main new target in the second half of the year; plus comprehension of different genres and short text writing.
- **Content tagging:** the Ministry pairs pointed and unpointed versions of the same text, so the game can carry a "nikud toggle"/fade mechanic on identical content. The pointed-vs-unpointed accuracy gap then works as a built-in diagnostic, as the Ministry itself suggests.
- **Fluency as a metric:** words-correct-per-minute on a short pointed text is the Ministry's own metric, so a speech-based read-aloud check could mirror it.

### Gaps
- I could not read the full 2025 curriculum PDF, so I have no per-age goal list for gan (e.g., which PA tasks at age 4 vs 5) and no grade-1/2 spelling or handwriting standards. The domains were blocked.
- The grade-3 "Ivrit: lashon, sifrut vetarbut" curriculum (grammar, gender/number, literature) was not retrieved. The new curriculum covers gan to grade 2, and grade 3 may still be under the older program; this is unconfirmed.
- No official recommended letter-introduction order was found.

## 2. How does the science of reading apply to Hebrew (pointed vs unpointed, CV units, morphology, maternal writing mediation)?

### Takeaway
Hebrew learners pass through two quite different orthographies:
- **Pointed Hebrew** is highly transparent. Children reach about 80% decoding accuracy by the end of grade 1. The key sub-lexical unit is the consonant + vowel (CV) combination, not the phoneme or the rime.
- **Unpointed Hebrew** is deep and full of homographs. Skilled reading of it relies on morphology (root + pattern) and context.

Both phonological awareness and morphological awareness predict Hebrew reading. Morphological deficits in kindergarten specifically predict dyslexia that emerges late (around grade 4). Parents' mediation of children's early writing is one of the best-evidenced Israeli home interventions for alphabetic skills.

### Cited Findings
**Orthography and the English-centric critique**
- Share (2008), "On the Anglocentricities of current reading research and practice" (Psychological Bulletin 134(4):584-615), argues that English's irregular spelling-sound mapping makes it an outlier. It says the field's agenda has been narrowed by focusing on English. — [Scinapse abstract](https://www.scinapse.io/papers/2040035146); [Haifa lecture page](https://edu.haifa.ac.il/?p=9288)
- Share later extends this critique to "alphabetism" (treating alphabets as the default script). — [Share 2020, IJRLD](https://journals.ub.uni-koeln.de/index.php/IJRLD/article/download/1874/1910)
- Pointed Hebrew is highly transparent, and children reach about 80% reading accuracy by the end of grade 1. Unpointed Hebrew is a deep orthography. — [Shany & Katzir, Reading and Writing (citing Share)](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11145-011-9314-y.pdf)

**Pointed-to-unpointed transition**
- Share & Bar-On's **Triplex model** has three stages:
  1. early phonological decoding of pointed text;
  2. lexico-morpho-orthographic processing emerging around grade 2;
  3. in the upper elementary grades, a supralexical contextual level that resolves the pervasive homography of unpointed Hebrew.

  The transition from pointed to unpointed text is the central challenge. — [Triplex model, TAU CRIS](https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/learning-to-read-a-semitic-abjad-the-triplex-model-of-hebrew-read/); [Haifa CRIS](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/learning-to-read-a-semitic-abjad-the-triplex-model-of-hebrew-read/)
- **Context use:** reliance on context changes during reading acquisition. Monitoring and reanalysis (catching a wrong homograph reading) lag far behind context effects and appear only with age and experience. — [Bar-On et al., context effects on homograph resolution](https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/context-effects-on-heterophonic-homography-resolution-in-learning/)
- **Timing:** pointed text is used for instruction mainly in grade 1, and children generally master both pointed and unpointed systems by the end of grade 3. — [Shany & Katzir](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/doi/10.1007/s11145-011-9314-y) (via search summary)
- **The shift is unique to Hebrew and Arabic:** it happens gradually during grades 2-3, and unpointed text is considerably ambiguous because many words become hard to identify without vowels. — [Yesod "Korim BaAlef" teacher guide, scientific advisor Dr. Amalia Bar-On](https://www.yesod.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/madal_U3952.pdf)
- **Phonology-morphology trade-off (2024, Reading and Writing):** accuracy on pointed pseudowords decreased with age, while accuracy on unpointed, morphologically based pseudowords increased. This is evidence of a developmental shift from phonology to morphology. — [OUCI record](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4VYLPGm7)

**CV units, letter names and phonological awareness**
- Hebrew CV "body" units are more accessible to young children than isolated phonemes. In English, rimes are the more accessible unit. — [Syllable splitting in literate and preliterate Hebrew speakers](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7705565_Syllable_splitting_in_literate_and_preliterate_Hebrew_speakers)
- Kindergartners tend to isolate the initial CV sub-syllable rather than the initial phoneme. — same source
- **Core-CV predictor (2025 RRQ review, Shechter et al.):** kindergarten awareness of the phonemes *inside* the core CV unit predicted grade-2 reading. The more easily extracted CV units and the extra-core consonants did not. The same review says the evidence repeatedly affirms the importance of both phonological and morphological awareness. — [Learning to Read and Developmental Dyslexia in Hebrew, RRQ 2025](https://ila.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/rrq.599) (search summary; full text blocked)
- **Letter sounds vs names:** in a study of 123 Israeli preschoolers, Hebrew letter sounds were learned more easily than letter names. Training on names tended to produce multi-phoneme responses ("buh" instead of /b/), linked to the multi-syllable structure of Hebrew letter names. — [Levin, Shatil-Carmon & Asif-Rave 2006](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022096505001232)
- **Letter-name structure:** in a study of 149 kindergartners, letter-name knowledge predicted letter-sound knowledge. Letters whose names follow a CV pattern were easier to map to sounds, and this was not explained by PA. About two-thirds of Hebrew letter names are monosyllabic (mostly CVC) and about one-third are disyllabic. — [Levin et al., letter names/sounds across letters and children](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/226834982_Letter_names_letter_sounds_and_phonological_awareness_An_examination_of_kindergarten_children_across_letters_and_of_letters_across_children); [Role of Hebrew letter names (multiphonemic acrophonic names)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022096507001130)
- **Letter names as spelling cues:** kindergartners use letter names as cues for letter reporting and spelling, and longer congruent name cues give better performance. — [Letter names: effect on letter saying, spelling and word recognition in Hebrew](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/231846266_Letter_names_Effect_on_letter_saying_spelling_and_word_recognition_in_Hebrew)
- **Direction of transfer (Share 2005):** learning letter sounds first later helped children learn the same letters' names, but not the reverse. — (cited in search summary of [letter-name literature](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022096507001130))
- **Letter naming and PA:** in 41 Hebrew-speaking kindergartners, letter naming correlated strongly with PA at the end of kindergarten. — [Predictive Examination of PA among Hebrew-speaking kindergartners, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6724554/)

**Morphology**
- Ravid's work on Hebrew spelling identifies phonological and morphological factors, and grade-school spelling errors point to a consistent morphological representation in Hebrew orthography. — [Gillis & Ravid](https://www.doi.org/10.1023/A:1011192806656); [Cambridge reference](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/66979301EED7A8AA1AF173390678B17A)
- **Written morphological analogies (grades 2-6, Ravid & Schiff):** most errors involved the root rather than the pattern (mishkal/binyan), and pseudowords produced more pattern errors. — [Roots and patterns in Hebrew language development](https://education.biu.ac.il/en/node/1331)
- Morphological effects on reading were stronger in grade 2 than grade 5. Correlations with morphological awareness appeared only for grade-2 readers of bi-morphemic words. — [Frontiers in Psychology 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.02369/pdf)
- A study of 100 grade-2 and grade-3 readers examined how inflectional, derivational and construct-state (smichut) morphology relate to comprehension, beyond vocabulary. — [Cambridge core reader](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/C8A36E349CC4D004294DF1D78893A46A/core-reader)
- **Humor-based morphology program:** Ravid & Geiger ran a metalinguistic morphology program using linguistic humor with grade-4 students, compared against nonverbal humor. — [SAGE abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/ref/10.1177/0142723708097483)

**Kindergarten predictors and dyslexia**
- A five-year cohort of 515 Hebrew-speaking children in northern Israel was followed from kindergarten to grades 1 and 4. Kindergarten cognitive-linguistic measures predicted risk for both early- and late-emerging dyslexia. Kindergarten morphological awareness deficits uniquely predicted late-emerging (grade-4) dyslexia. — [Kindergarten screening for early and late-emerging dyslexia, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13014184/); [Kindergarten predictors of dyslexia pathways, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13124792/)
- Kindergarten rapid automatized naming (RAN) predicted word-reading fluency. — [Early Predictors of Hebrew Reading Development, Scientific Studies of Reading 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10888438.2025.2497234)

**Parents mediating early writing**
- **Correlational (Aram & Levin 2001):** 41 low-SES children aged 5;5-6;0 and their mothers. The quality of mothers' writing mediation predicted word writing, word recognition and phonological awareness after controlling for sociocultural factors. — [Aram & Levin, TAU CRIS](https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/the-role-of-maternal-mediation-of-writing-to-kindergartners-in-pr/)
- **Longitudinal:** maternal writing mediation in kindergarten predicted school literacy beyond SES and kindergarten literacy. — [Aram & Levin longitudinal](https://dx.doi.org/10.1007/s11145-010-9254-y) (via search summary)
- **Randomized intervention (Aram & Levin / Levin & Aram 2012, Reading and Writing):** 119 mothers and 5 fathers were coached in one of three areas: interactive storybook reading, joint writing, or visuomotor skills. They had a workshop, then 7 weeks of three structured dyadic sessions per week plus weekly home visits.
  - The writing-mediation group improved children's alphabetic skills.
  - Effects were domain-specific: reading coaching did not affect writing, and writing coaching affected only writing.

  — [Mother-child joint writing and storybook reading intervention](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4Y3njd09); [IUCC CRIS](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/mother-child-joint-writing-and-storybook-reading-and-their-effect/)
- **Home spelling programs (Elimelech, Aram & Levin):** programs teaching mothers to support Hebrew spelling significantly improved children's spelling in both a low-SES and a middle-SES sample. — [Mothers teaching their children the Hebrew writing system](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/mothers-teaching-their-children-the-hebrew-writing-system-the-eff/)

### Inferences
- **For the 5.5-year-old, the PA ladder should be Hebrew-specific:**
  1. whole-word syllable clapping;
  2. initial CV ("מה ההברה הראשונה ב־מָה־זָה?", "what's the first syllable in ma-ze?");
  3. *splitting the CV into consonant + vowel*. This step is what predicts grade-2 reading (Shechter RRQ 2025), so the game should not stop at CV identification;
  4. phoneme deletion/substitution.

  English-style rhyme/rime units are less natural for Hebrew.
- **Teach letter sounds rather than names as the primary label.** Sounds are easier, and sound learning transfers to names but not the reverse. Use the letter name as a secondary label, and favor letters whose name begins with their sound (most Hebrew names are acrophonic).
- **Model the decoding unit as a letter + nikud "tzeruf" block (e.g., בָּ, בִּ, בּוּ).** This matches Ministry and research framing.
- **Plan explicitly for the grade-3 child's pointed-to-unpointed transition.** The research-backed levers are:
  - root (shoresh) awareness;
  - pattern/binyan awareness;
  - homograph resolution using context, including deliberately training the "notice-and-reanalyze" step, which develops late;
  - plenty of unpointed reading of familiar words.
- **Morphology in gan (inference from the dyslexia-prediction data):** kindergarten morphological awareness predicts late dyslexia, so oral morphology games could start for the 5.5-year-old, e.g., "גַּן → גַּנָּן → גִּנָּה" (gan → gardener → garden), and singular/plural, gender inflection.
- **Joint writing makes a strong co-play mechanic for the 5.5-year-old.** The child writes a word and the parent/app mediates segmentation ("what sound do you hear first? which letter makes it?") instead of dictating. The intervention evidence is specific to writing, so a writing mode is needed in addition to reading/story modes.
- **Background, not verified this session:** the general science-of-reading frameworks (National Reading Panel's five pillars, the Simple View of Reading = decoding × language comprehension, Scarborough's rope) are standard. They map onto Hebrew as described above, with morphology carrying unusual weight in the word-recognition strand.

### Gaps
- Full text of the Triplex model and the 2025 RRQ review was not accessible, so there are no effect sizes or exact age markers.
- I found no Katzir or Breznitz fluency-intervention details this session. Breznitz's "acceleration phenomenon" (reading faster improves comprehension) is relevant, but no source was retrieved.
- I found no Hebrew-specific application of Scarborough's rope or the Simple View in this search.

## 3. Common Hebrew reading-teaching methods; letter order; confusable letters and final letters

### Takeaway
Israeli grade-1 instruction is officially phonological/synthetic: letter + vowel combinations build up to words. The Ministry steered schools back toward phonics in the early 2000s after the Shapira committee. Commercial programs differ mainly in letter order and pacing.

I found no peer-reviewed comparison of named Israeli programs. Visual letter confusions are the dominant letter-naming error in Israeli preschoolers, more than phonological confusions are.

### Cited Findings
- **Approaches:** Hebrew reading approaches fall into three families: synthetic/phonetic, analytic/global, and eclectic. — [MOFET portal, approaches to teaching reading](https://portal.macam.ac.il/article/%D7%92%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%95%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%98%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%9C%D7%94%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%A7%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%90%D7%94/)
- **Synthetic phonics programs:** phonetic/phonological methods aim at accurate and fluent decoding. Phonetic-synthetic programs such as "קריאה ראשונה" (Shva publishers) build from the small units (consonants, letters, vowels, sub-syllables) up to the whole. — [Yesod "Tzlil, Mila, Sipur" teacher guide](https://www.yesod.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/tg_new_odkria_U0910.pdf); [Kriya Rishona, Shva](https://bonusbooks-shop.co.il/product/%D7%A7%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%90%D7%94-%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%94-%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%95%D7%90/)
- **Program examples in use:**
  - "קוראים ב־א'" (Yesod, Ministry-approved, scientific advisor Dr. Amalia Bar-On);
  - "בסוד האותיות", ordered by alphabet order and vowel order;
  - "אוצר מילים", a phonetic-phonological method;
  - "על אפיקי מים" (mainly religious-sector).

  — [Yesod Korim BaAlef](https://www.yesod.co.il/product/%D7%A7%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%91%D7%90%D7%9C%D7%A3-%D7%9E%D7%9E%D7%9C%D7%9B%D7%AA%D7%99/); [Afikey Mayim method](https://www.afikey-books.co.il/%D7%A2%D7%9C-%D7%94%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%98%D7%94/); [Achiya "Lomdim Likro" PDF](https://achiyayeda.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/lomdim-likro-1.pdf); [Reches "Hitzlachti Likro"](https://www.reches.co.il/files/products/390/876b14acff6e4110a3e33039202ff346.pdf)
- **Policy history (2002):** the Ministry instructed schools to incorporate phonetic components after the committee chaired by Rina Shapira described reading achievement as "grim" (עגום). The source is dated. — [Haaretz, 2002](https://www.haaretz.co.il/misc/2002-09-03/ty-article/0000017f-e286-d9aa-afff-fbde6ffa0000)
- **Letter-naming errors (Treiman, Levin & Kessler 2007):** 645 Israeli children were tested before formal instruction. Their errors mostly involved visually similar letters or letters adjacent in alphabetical order. There were fewer phonologically based confusions than in English, because Hebrew letter names sound distinct. — [Learning of letter names follows similar principles across languages](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/learning-of-letter-names-follows-similar-principles-across-langua/)
- **Practitioner observation:** visual confusions (e.g., ב/כ) resurface once instruction moves on to vowels and letter names are no longer reinforced. A timed recital of the alef-bet is suggested to expose weak letters. This is practitioner opinion, not research. — [Dei'ah veDibur](https://chareidi.org/archives5764/achrei/hread.htm)

### Inferences
- **Practical confusable sets to drill with minimal-pair discrimination games**, based on letter shapes (these are inference/background, not from a source retrieved here):
  - ב/כ/פ (and בּ/ב, כּ/כ, פּ/פ dagesh pairs);
  - ד/ר/ך;
  - ה/ח/ת;
  - ו/ז/ן/י;
  - ס/ם;
  - ט/ע/צ;
  - ג/נ.
- **Alphabet-adjacent confusions are also real.** Practicing in fixed alef-bet order may create sequence confusions, so the game should randomize order once letters are known.
- **Phonologically identical pairs:** ט/ת, כ/ח, ק/כּ, א/ע, ס/שׂ, ו/ב. These cannot be resolved by sound and only matter for spelling. They are a grade-2-3 spelling target tied to morphology (the root fixes the letter), not a gan target.
- **Final letters (ך ם ן ף ץ):** introduce them as "the same letter in its end-of-word shape" after the base letter is known (background, no source retrieved).

### Gaps
- No Ministry-recommended letter order was found. No efficacy comparison of named programs was found. The programs named in the assignment ("Mesila", "Dalet Amot", "Sfarim baGan", "Ivrit Bet", "Pa'amonim") could not be verified or found. ("Sifriyat Pijama", the Israeli PJ Library gan book-gifting program, may be what "Sfarim baGan" refers to; this is unverified.)
- I found no letter-by-letter Hebrew confusability data and no stroke-order research.

## 4. Early language for toddlers (2-3): dialogic reading, vocabulary, songs/rhymes, phonological play

### Takeaway
For ages 2-3, the evidence-based core is oral vocabulary and conversation built through frequent, interactive shared reading. How the reading is done matters as much as how often. Israeli research supports this through Aram & Levin's shared-reading work and the Hebrew MB-CDI norms.

Hebrew-specific, rhyme-focused toddler intervention studies were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Israeli children exposed to frequent storybook reading consistently surpass peers in vocabulary (Aram & Levin 2002). The vocabulary benefit depends on reading style, and home interventions training parents in interactive reading improved children's vocabulary. — [Springer chapter on Hebrew parent-child literacy interactions](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-030-99891-2_16)
- **Technion resource:** dialogic story reading means reading together, sharing, asking questions and developing discourse. It is described as enriching toddlers' vocabulary and preparing them for discourse and reading. — [Technion Neuroimaging Center](https://neuroimaging-center.technion.ac.il/the-place-of-knowledge-and-research-insights-during-war-time/)
- **Hebrew MB-CDI norms:** 1,555 toddlers aged 1;0-2;0. Lexicon size is influenced by parental education, income, religiosity, birth order and child-care arrangement. This provides Hebrew vocabulary growth curves usable for word-list selection. — [Hebrew Web CDI, J. Child Language](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/80A8AFEEB4ED71E3682560A77FBD7941/S0305000921000179a.pdf/the-hebrew-web-communicative-development-inventory-mb-cdi-lexical-development-growth-curves.pdf)
- **Alphabet books (Bergman Deitcher, Aram & Itzkovich 2021):** parent-child shared reading of Hebrew alphabet books contributed to early literacy beyond motivation. — [Taylor & Francis reference](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/ref/10.1080/02568543.2019.1577774)
- **Aram's affiliation:** Dorit Aram heads the Early Childhood Research Lab at Tel Aviv University and writes guidance on shared book reading in preschools. — [ILA, Shared book reading in preschools](https://www.literacyworldwide.org/get-resources/instructional-practices/shared-book-reading-in-preschools)
- **Phonological development:** an overview of early receptive and productive phonological development in Israeli Hebrew infants and toddlers exists (Keren-Portnoy). — [De Gruyter chapter](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1075/tilar.19.03ker/html)
- **Rhyme intervention (English evidence):** a rhyme + initial-sound intervention delivered through shared book reading improved PA (Ziolkowski & Goldstein). — [LuCiD handout](https://www.lucid.ac.uk/media/2019/handout-sr-impact-on-phonological-awareness-use-with-children-with-language-delay.pdf)

### Inferences
- **The 2.5-year-old's mode should be dialogic, not instructional.** Use picture-book scenes where the app (or a sibling or parent) asks wh-questions, prompts labeling, expands the child's utterance, and repeats books, i.e., the PEER/CROWD pattern of classic dialogic reading (background knowledge).
- **Word lists** should come from the Hebrew CDI norms, i.e., high-frequency early nouns, verbs and adjectives.
- **The 2.5-year-old's own name and alphabet books** (Bergman Deitcher et al.) are a natural first contact with print: "the letter of my name" / "האות של השם שלי".
- **Hebrew nursery songs and rhymes** suit syllable play and rhythm. Rhyme can be used for enjoyment, but the main PA unit later should be syllables and initial CV (see §2).
- **Speech features for the toddler** should be lenient and reward attempts. Toddler pronunciation is immature (Keren-Portnoy's work covers Hebrew norms).

### Gaps
- No Hebrew RCT on toddler dialogic reading or toddler phonological play was found.
- No Hebrew-specific source on songs/rhymes efficacy was found.

## 5. Digital Hebrew literacy tools and evidence

### Takeaway
The strongest Hebrew digital evidence is the Bar-Ilan e-book research program of Korat, Shamir and colleagues. Well-designed e-books improved vocabulary, phonological awareness, word reading and story comprehension in Israeli kindergartners, including low-SES and at-risk children, typically after about 3 sessions. The exception was letter naming.

Peer-paired use beat solo use. Most commercial Hebrew e-books and apps were found to have weak literacy-supporting features, and no 2023-2024 efficacy trial of a named Hebrew app was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Review (Korat, Shamir & Segal-Drori 2014, Early Child Development and Care 184(7):998-1016):** e-book reading supported all language and literacy skills measured except letter naming, with most gains after about three sessions. — [BIU listing](https://education.biu.ac.il/en/node/6042); [IUCC CRIS](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/e-books-as-a-support-for-young-childrens-language-and-literacy-th/)
- **Peer vs solo use (Shamir, Korat & Barbi, Computers & Education 51(1):354-367):** 110 kindergartners in a low-SES township. Children using an e-book in same-age peer pairs (tutor/tutee) gained more in PA, emergent reading and story comprehension than children using it individually. — [Search summary / BIU](https://education.biu.ac.il/en/node/433)
- **E-book vs adult reader (Korat & Shamir 2007, J. Computer Assisted Learning 23:248-259):** both the e-book and the adult-read print book outperformed controls over 3 sessions, with effects examined by social class. — [Electronic books versus adult readers](https://scholarhub.vn/publication/Electronic-books-versus-adult-readers-effects-on-children-s-emergent-literacy-as-a-function-of-social-class/50e99b57-4bdc-4473-853c-148585e5ad1e)
- **At-risk kindergartners (Shamir & Korat 2015, Computers in the Schools 32(2):105-121):** both at-risk and typical children improved vocabulary and PA after e-book activity. — [IUCC CRIS educational e-books](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/educational-electronic-books-for-supporting-emergent-literacy-of-/)
- **CD-ROM storybooks, low-SES:** the effect of CD-ROM storybook reading depended on learning context. — [BIU](https://education.biu.ac.il/en/node/814)
- **Repeated e-book reading:** 78 low-SES kindergartners who read an e-book learned more new words than controls. — [ERIC EJ1145384](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1145384.pdf)
- **Commercial content quality (Korat & Shamir 2004):** a content analysis of 43 Hebrew e-books found limited multimedia features supporting literacy. — (search summary of Korat/Shamir corpus, [BIU](https://education.biu.ac.il/en/node/433))
- **Commercial apps:** Jewish Interactive "Ji Tap" Hebrew apps are marketing-only, with no outcome data. — [Ji blog](https://info.jewishinteractive.org/blog/ji-tap-hebrew)
- **Educational TV:** the show "Bli Sodot" was designed to teach reading and was popular, but no outcome evaluation was found. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bli_Sodot)

### Inferences
- **E-book design features that should carry over to the game**, based on what Korat & Shamir tested (dictionary/word-explanation hotspots, highlighted text synchronized with narration, embedded PA/vocabulary activities):
  - narration with word-by-word highlighting;
  - tap-for-meaning on hard words;
  - built-in questions;
  - repeated readings of the same story (gains appear by session 3).
- **Avoid decorative hotspots** (animations unrelated to the text), which the content analysis flagged as common and unhelpful (inference from the critique).
- **Letter naming did not improve with e-books.** Letter-sound learning needs explicit practice games, not stories.
- **Use the peer-pair advantage:** a sibling co-play mode, e.g., the 8.5-year-old as "tutor" reading or helping the 5.5-year-old, is directly supported by the tutor/tutee e-book finding.

### Gaps
- No efficacy evidence was found for Ofek, Galim, Pedagogy.co.il, TinyTap Hebrew content, Kids Zone/Hop! or similar. Their mechanics were not researched due to the tool budget and blocked domains.
- No Hebrew speech-recognition reading-tutor evaluation was found.

## 6. Grade-3 specifics: comprehension, fluency, writing, grammar, root dictionary

### Takeaway
Grade 3 is the pivot year:
- consolidating pointed fluency (measured as accuracy and rate);
- beginning systematic unpointed (ktiv male) reading from mid-year using paired pointed/unpointed texts and cloze tasks;
- growing reliance on morphology (roots, patterns, inflection) for reading, spelling and comprehension.

### Cited Findings
- **Mid-year unpointed start and program design:** unpointed full-spelling reading starts from mid-grade 3, using dual-version texts and cloze activities. — [Tzomchim Mechadash](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Pop/0files/ivrit_hinuch_leshoni/Yesodi/growing-again-2-3g.pdf)
- **Assessment texts:** mid-grade-3 texts are pointed and end-of-grade-3 texts are unpointed. — [Ministry assessment texts](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Mazkirut_Pedagogit/HebrewPrimary/assessment/attache2-texts.pdf)
- **Fluency assessment beyond grade 3:** the Ministry has a fluency assessment protocol for grades 3-6 (special-education version). — [Reading fluency evaluation, grades 3-6](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/special/pedagogy/safa/reading_fluency_evaluation.pdf)
- **Mastery timing:** children generally master both pointed and unpointed systems by the end of grade 3. — [Shany & Katzir](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/doi/10.1007/s11145-011-9314-y)
- **Morphology and comprehension:** inflectional, derivational and construct (smichut) morphology relate to comprehension in grades 2-3 beyond vocabulary. — [Cambridge core reader](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/C8A36E349CC4D004294DF1D78893A46A/core-reader)
- **Root vs pattern errors:** in grades 2-6, root errors dominate on real words and pattern errors on pseudowords. — [Ravid & Schiff](https://education.biu.ac.il/en/node/1331)
- **Spelling follows morphology:** Hebrew spelling errors reflect morphological representation. — [Gillis & Ravid](https://www.doi.org/10.1023/A:1011192806656)
- **Homograph monitoring is late:** context use comes before monitoring/reanalysis of homographs. — [Bar-On et al.](https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/context-effects-on-heterophonic-homography-resolution-in-learning/)
- **Ready-made practice material:** a "מניקוד ללא ניקוד" (from pointed to unpointed) worksheet resource exists, practitioner material. — [Revital Gonen](https://revitalgonen.co.il/worksheet/%D7%9E%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%A7%D7%95%D7%93-%D7%9C%D7%9C%D7%90-%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%A7%D7%95%D7%93/)

### Inferences
- **Core grade-3 game loops:**
  1. timed re-reading of short pointed passages (repeated reading improves wpm), tracked against the Ministry-style metric;
  2. "nikud fade": the same text shown with progressively less nikud;
  3. cloze in unpointed text, where context selects the word;
  4. homograph detectives, e.g., ספר = סֵפֶר / סָפַר / סַפָּר (book / counted / barber), with a "re-read and fix" step;
  5. root families (ש־ל־ח: שָׁלַח, שְׁלִיחַ, מִשְׁלוֹחַ — sent, messenger, delivery);
  6. spelling of same-sound letters justified by the root (e.g., why כתב is with כ and not ח: because of כְּתִיבָה, writing).
- **Grammar basics** (gender/number agreement, tense, possessive/smichut) map naturally to sentence-building drag-and-drop with agreement checking. This is background inference; no grade-3 grammar standards were retrieved.
- **Writing short texts:** genre-based prompts (story, invitation, description), consistent with the curriculum strand "producing texts of various genres."

### Gaps
- No official grade-3 standard for writing length/genres, grammar topics, or a target wpm was retrieved.
- No Hebrew root-dictionary tool evaluation was found.
- No Hebrew comprehension-strategy (summarizing, questioning) intervention studies were retrieved this session.

## 7. Concrete game-mechanic ideas grounded in the above

### Takeaway
The best-supported mechanics are:
- CV-block (letter + nikud) building and splitting for gan/grade 1;
- sound-first letter games with confusable-letter discrimination;
- parent- or sibling-mediated joint writing;
- narrated e-books with word highlighting, embedded vocabulary and repeat readings;
- nikud-fade, cloze and root-family games for the grade-3 transition.

These are design inferences drawn from the findings cited in §§1-6.

### Cited Findings
- **CV as the natural unit**, with phoneme-within-CV awareness predicting later reading. — [Syllable splitting](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7705565_Syllable_splitting_in_literate_and_preliterate_Hebrew_speakers); [RRQ 2025](https://ila.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/rrq.599)
- **Sounds before names**, since sound learning transfers to names. — [Levin et al. 2006](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022096505001232)
- **Joint writing mediation improves alphabetic skills.** — [Aram & Levin intervention](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4Y3njd09)
- **E-books with peer pairing beat solo use**, with gains by session 3. — [Korat/Shamir review](https://education.biu.ac.il/en/node/6042)
- **Paired pointed/unpointed texts and cloze are the Ministry's own grade 2-3 tools.** — [Tzomchim Mechadash](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Pop/0files/ivrit_hinuch_leshoni/Yesodi/growing-again-2-3g.pdf)
- **Words-per-minute + accuracy on short pointed texts is the Ministry fluency metric.** — [Grade-1 test guide](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Yesodi/ivrit/madrich.pdf)

### Inferences (mechanic list by child)

**Age 2.5 (oral language)**
- **"Talking picture book":** dialogic prompts ("מה זה?", "what's this?"; "מה הוא עושה?", "what is he doing?"). The app expands the child's answer ("כן! הכלב רץ מהר", "yes! the dog runs fast"). Repeat favorite books and use the child's name and family photos as characters.
- **Word hunts in a scene:** tap the named object, with vocabulary drawn from the Hebrew CDI norms.
- **Song/rhythm clapping:** syllable clapping of family names ("נֹ־עָה", No-a).
- **First print:** "my letter", the first letter of the child's name shown everywhere.

**Age 5.5 (gan chova)**
- **Syllable train:** drag pictures to wagons by number of syllables.
- **First-CV catch:** "which picture starts with מָ?". Then the split step: "מָ = מ + אָ".
- **Sound-first letter cards**, ordered by usefulness and acrophonic transparency. Each new letter is introduced with its sound and a picture, with the name secondary.
- **Confusable-pair "spot the difference" mini-games:** ב/כ, ד/ר, ה/ח/ת, ו/ז/ן, ס/ם, introduced only after each letter in the pair is known separately.
- **Tzeruf builder:** combine a letter tile + nikud tile → hear the syllable → build a real word (מָ + מָה, ma + ma = "Mama"). Early decoding with kamatz/patach first is common practice (background).
- **Invented-spelling writing studio:** the child writes a word with letter tiles or tracing. The app or parent mediates segmentation ("איזה צליל שומעים בהתחלה?", "what sound do we hear first?") instead of correcting outright, following the Aram & Levin mediation model. A parent co-play mode includes prompts for the parent.
- **Letter tracing with stroke order:** right-to-left directionality. No research source was retrieved on stroke order, so use the Ministry/textbook print-letter conventions.
- **Oral morphology play:** singular → plural (ילד→ילדים, boy → boys), masculine → feminine (גנן→גננת, gardener m. → f.), word families from a root.

**Age 8.5 (grade 3)**
- **Fluency sprints:** speech-recognition read-aloud of short pointed passages, with accuracy + wpm tracked over repeated readings. This needs a Hebrew ASR that handles child speech (no evaluation was found).
- **Nikud fade:** the same personalized story is re-read with nikud gradually removed. Nikud stays only on ambiguous words, then is fully unpointed.
- **Homograph detective:** choose the right reading of ספר/חלב/דבר-type words (book/counted/barber; milk/fat; thing/spoke) from sentence context, then a "check yourself" reanalysis step.
- **Root family trees:** collect words sharing a shoresh. Spelling quests use the root to decide between same-sound letters (ט/ת, כ/ח, א/ע, ק/כּ, ס/שׂ).
- **Unpointed cloze stories** (Ministry technique).
- **Agreement builder:** drag adjective/verb forms to agree in gender and number.
- **Short genre writing** (story, letter, description), with root-based spell hints.

**Cross-age**
- **Sibling tutor mode:** the grade-3 child reads or coaches the gan child (peer-pair evidence).
- **Personalized decodable stories:** use only the letters and nikud each child has mastered, starring the family.

### Gaps
- No evidence was retrieved on the efficacy of speech-recognition read-aloud feedback for Hebrew-speaking children.
- No evidence was retrieved on digital letter tracing or stroke order for Hebrew.
- No evidence was retrieved on adaptive/personalized decodable-text generation in Hebrew.
