## (1) Social / co-op play: what makes 99 Nights in the Forest, runners, party games, Hazelight co-op, Roblox, and classroom quiz games work, and what research says about cooperative vs competitive play for siblings

### Takeaway
The hits that work for kids share a few things. They have one clear shared goal to protect, like the campfire in 99 Nights, plus a short day/night tension cycle and role variety through classes. They also mix simple "answer to earn" loops with a playful meta-game, as in Blooket, Gimkit and Kahoot-style modes. Research supports cooperative goal structures over competitive ones for achievement: Johnson & Johnson report effect sizes of roughly 0.4 to 0.7. Competition can motivate some low achievers, but it backfires when it exposes ability gaps. For a family with a 2.5-year-old, a 5.5-year-old and an 8.5-year-old, the evidence points toward a shared-goal team mode with asymmetric roles and per-child difficulty, with competition only against oneself (ghost races, personal bests).

### Cited Findings

**99 Nights in the Forest (Roblox, 2025)**
- Core loop: gather resources by day, defend the campsite after dark, and survive 99 nights that get harder each time. Players work together against creatures hidden in the forest. — [ExitLag guide](https://www.exitlag.com/blog/99-nights-in-the-forest-roblox/)
- The campfire is the center of the game. Managing fuel is described as "the core loop," and most of the day goes to gathering fuel so the fire lasts the night. One guide says that if the fire goes out at any point, the run ends immediately. — [ddingpy survival guide](https://ddingpy.github.io/mynote/posts/99-nights-in-the-forest-roblox-survival-guide); [Sportskeeda beginner's guide](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/99-nights-forest-a-beginner-s-guide?key4=flipboardroblox)
- Night threats include the Deer monster, described as invincible: it stalks at night and the flashlight is the counter. Other threats are cultists, the Owl and the Ram. — [PC Gamer tips (search snippet)](https://pcgamer.com/games/roblox/99-nights-in-the-forest-tips/); [Endsights tips](https://endsights.com/99-nights-tips)
- Classes and roles: the count differs by source. One says 16 classes, another says 20 or more, each with its own perks. Some classes speed up crafting, others improve healing or trapping, and some focus on ranged damage. Classes are bought in a rotating **Daily Class Shop** in the lobby. One source says the price is in diamonds earned from chest hunting and achievements; another mentions 350 Robux. — [TechWiser all classes](https://techwiser.com/all-classes-in-99-nights-in-the-forest-roblox/); [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/99-nights-forest-a-beginner-s-guide?key4=flipboardroblox). *These sources conflict on class count and currency.*

**Hazelight (Josef Fares): designing for forced cooperation**
- Hazelight builds its games around collaboration as the central design theme. The studio starts with two players in mind, and communication between players is meant to be essential to progress. — [Wikipedia: It Takes Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_Takes_Two_(video_game))
- Fares: "when you design a single-player game and then you add co-op to it, you have to adapt it." He says the game cannot be played alone, and that this is "almost part of the story." — [The Ringer interview](https://www.theringer.com/2021/4/22/22396763/josef-fares-it-takes-two-interview-co-op-gaming)
- It Takes Two has no single-player mode, which the industry saw as risky. Fares says "the story and the gameplay should marry together." The game won several Game of the Year awards. — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/josef-fares-youll-love-it-takes-two-or-you-can-break-his-arms-and-legs/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_Takes_Two_(video_game))

**Classroom quiz games (Blooket, Gimkit)**
- Blooket's official help center describes **Gold Quest** this way: "Build your riches in this chaotic mode by answering questions to earn gold and take it from other players." It describes **Tower Defense** this way: "Answer questions, build towers, and design your defense to protect yourself from waves of Evil Blooks." Tower Defense is rated strategy- and accuracy-based and "complex." — [Blooket Game Mode Previews (official)](https://help.blooket.com/hc/en-us/articles/31616660563479)
- Other Blooket modes, from secondary sources: **Battle Royale** (last player standing), **Factory** (build and upgrade a factory), and **Café**, where students fill orders. One third-party source calls Café cooperative. Blooket's mode list rotates by season. — [Blooket Help](https://help.blooket.com/hc/en-us/articles/31616660563479); [Plisio review](https://plisio.net/education/blooket) *(secondary)*
- Gimkit (third-party sources only):
  - **Classic**: answer questions to earn cash and buy upgrades. The game ends at a time limit or when a money goal is reached.
  - **Trust No One**: Among Us-style social deduction, launched in 2021.
  - **Fishtopia**: answer questions to earn bait, then fish.
  - **Don't Look Down**: a 2D platformer where answers give energy for climbing six summits.
  - Gimkit has about two dozen modes. The free tier gets two modes at a time, rotating weekly.
  - Sources: [Designbeep Gimkit guide 2026](https://designbeep.com/2026/06/09/gimkit/); [Plisio Gimkit review 2026](https://plisio.net/education/what-is-gimkit); [GimJam guide](https://sites.google.com/view/gimjam/gimkit-guide/gamemodes)

**Cooperative vs competitive learning research**
- Johnson & Johnson's 1993 meta-analysis of college studies: cooperative learning gave higher individual achievement than competitive learning, ES = 0.54. — [Cascadia summary of Johnson & Johnson](https://faculty.cascadia.edu/mpanitz/tpanitz_Cooperative_Education/cccchtml/weknowcl.html)
- A later series of college meta-analyses found g = .42 for cooperative vs competitive and g = .36 for cooperative vs individualistic. — [U. Minnesota Conservancy](https://conservancy.umn.edu/items/5e0a2b70-7901-443a-ac5e-2f787065e056)
- The 1981 meta-analysis of 122 studies found cooperation beat competitive and individualistic efforts on achievement. Secondary sources cite a follow-up of 111 studies (2002) with ES of 0.58 to 0.70 across outcomes. — [core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk/download/524679934.pdf)
- Johnson, Johnson & Stanne (2000) looked at 164 studies covering 8 cooperative methods. Every method had a significant positive effect on achievement. "Learning Together" had the largest effect against competition, followed by "Academic Controversy." — [Typeset PDF](https://typeset.io/pdf/cooperative-learning-methods-a-meta-analysis-1gsgjl6t24.pdf)
- Competition and low achievers:
  - Cheng et al. (Computers & Education, 2009) argue that competition can undercut students with low beliefs about their own ability. They propose an "equal opportunity" mechanism that narrows the gap between strong and weak players. — [Bohrium entry](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/equal-opportunity-tactic-redesigning-and-applying-competition-games-in-classrooms/811848464924147712-9279)
  - An Israeli poster (Holon Institute of Technology, Chais 2015): digital games for low achievers raised achievement-oriented motivation but did not significantly change achievement. — [Open University Chais 2015](https://www.openu.ac.il/innovation/chais2015/b3_4.pdf)
  - Cagiltay et al.: adding competition to a serious game improved motivation and post-test scores (n = 142, post-secondary). — [Atilim](https://ada.atilim.edu.tr/entities/publication/ecadba71-c044-4fbe-a2b0-5368e5070083/full)
  - A study of students by ability found that low-ability learners had less test anxiety and liked social competition more. — [NTNU Scholar](https://scholar.lib.ntnu.edu.tw/en/publications/learning-preferences-and-motivation-of-different-ability-students-2/)

### Inferences
- **Copy the "shared campfire" from 99 Nights.** Give the family one shared object to protect or grow, such as a "family tree / campfire / spaceship." Each child's correct answers feed it, so every contribution adds to the same team goal. The instant fail state ("fire goes out, run ends") is too harsh for ages 2 to 5. Soften it to "the fire dims; tomorrow it needs more wood."
- **Use Daily Class Shop–style asymmetric roles to handicap by age.** For example:
  - 2.5-year-old: a "Lighter" role. Tap or drag any glowing item, with no failure.
  - 5.5-year-old: a "Gatherer" role. Letter and number recognition, simple counting.
  - 8.5-year-old: a "Builder/Defender" role. Reading, arithmetic, light strategy, like Tower Defense.
  The roles are different, not ranked, which follows Hazelight's "each player is needed" principle.
- **Follow Blooket/Gimkit's split.** Separate the learning loop (answer a question, earn a resource) from a swappable meta-game (tower defense, fishing, climbing, café). One question bank can then power many modes and keep things fresh. Café and Tower Defense fit cooperative play. Gold Quest-style stealing doesn't belong in sibling play.
- **Keep competition self-referential.** Ghost races against your own past self, personal bests, and asynchronous "beat your record" challenges, in line with Cheng et al.'s equal-opportunity idea. Avoid sibling-vs-sibling leaderboards. If the siblings do compete, normalize scores to each child's own level, for example points for improvement over their baseline.
- **Team boss battles and family quests.** A weekly "boss" whose HP falls with each child's level-appropriate correct answers. A parent can join asynchronously, for example by recording a voice clue.
- **Runner and party appeal.** Endless runners (Temple Run, Subway Surfers) and Fall Guys/Stumble Guys work through short 1 to 3 minute sessions, one-thumb input, and instant restart. This is design inference, not sourced; see Gaps.

## (2) Economy design: soft vs premium currency, chests, shops, passes, progression, and how to build an all-earned economy for kids

### Takeaway
The core of any economy is balancing sources (faucets) against sinks (drains). Inflation comes from too many sources, especially event rewards, and not enough destructive sinks. The industry is moving away from opaque random boxes toward deterministic or disclosed rewards: Brawl Stars removed Brawl Boxes in December 2022 to be "more fair and predictable." Apple requires odds to be disclosed for paid randomized items. For an all-earned kids' economy, use:
- one soft currency earned through mastery
- an optional rarer "gem" earned only through milestones, never bought
- transparent or guaranteed chests
- a cosmetics-only shop
- savings goals tied to parent-defined real-world rewards

### Cited Findings

**Sources, sinks and inflation**
- Sources are faucets that add currency and sinks are drains that remove it. Keeping them balanced is the central design task. Inflation happens when sources outpace sinks; deflation happens when sinks outpace sources. — [DEV: How to design a game economy](https://dev.to/hiroshi_takamura_c851fe71/how-to-design-a-game-economy-sources-sinks-loops-and-balance-j05)
- There are two kinds of sinks. Transfer sinks move value between players. Destructive sinks, such as repair fees or crafting that consumes materials, destroy value, and only these actually counter inflation. — [Medium: Designing Game Economies](https://medium.com/@msahinn21/designing-game-economies-inflation-resource-management-and-balance-fa1e6c894670)
- A common mistake is too many resources and too few sinks. Limited-time events that pour in currency without enough sinks cause an inflation spike that lasts beyond the event. Another warning is pairing infinite sources with finite sinks. — [300mind.studio](https://300mind.studio/?p=3564); [Medium](https://medium.com/@msahinn21/designing-game-economies-inflation-resource-management-and-balance-fa1e6c894670)
- Soft currency is earned through play and used for routine progress. Hard currency is scarcer and tied to purchases. Splitting them lets a game reward free players generously while keeping spending power in the hard currency. Economies "should never be set and forget": tune them through soft launch, A/B tests and analytics. — [Meliorgames](https://meliorgames.com/game-development/game-economy-design-revenue-and-player-engagement/); [Metavert](https://metavert.io/game-economy-design)

**Brawl Stars as a case study in moving to deterministic rewards**
- Supercell removed all loot boxes (Brawl Boxes) from Brawl Stars in December 2022 in favor of deterministic rewards, framing it as more "fair and predictable." — [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2022/12/13/brawl-stars-loot-boxes-removed-supercell); [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/supercell-pulls-loot-boxes-from-brawl-stars-in-favor-of-deterministic-rewards-)
- Starr Drops were added later as a daily reward for the 1st, 4th and 8th win of each day. They start at Rare and get four chances to upgrade rarity. The number per day differs by source: up to 3 in one guide, and "0–2" in Supercell's August 2026 release notes. — [AFK Gaming](https://afkgaming.com/mobileesports/guide/brawl-stars-starr-drops-rewards-rarities-drop-chance-how-to-get-more); [Supercell release notes Aug 2026](https://supercell.com/en/games/brawlstars/blog/release-notes/release-notes-august-2026)
- Mega Boxes later came back with a mix of items. Third-party odds figures (about 50% Rare, 12% Epic, 2% Fabled) conflict with each other and could not be checked against an official table. — [Mobile Matters](https://mobilematters.gg/news/brawl-stars/brawl-stars-boxes-return)

**Platform rules**
- Apple guideline 3.1.1: "Apps offering 'loot boxes' or other mechanisms that provide randomized virtual items for purchase must disclose the odds of receiving each type of item to customers prior to purchase." — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Apple guideline 1.3: Kids Category apps "must not include links out of the app, purchasing opportunities, or other distractions to kids unless reserved for a designated area behind a parental gate." — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
**Recommended all-earned kids economy**
- **Coins (soft currency).** Earned per *mastery event*, not per minute played. Pay for correct answers on newly introduced items, streak repairs, or a skill moving up a level. Give diminishing coins for repeating already-mastered content, so grinding easy questions pays little. This ties currency to effort and learning, not time.
- **Gems or stars (rare currency).** Never bought. Earned only at milestones: finishing a unit, a weekly family quest, or a co-op boss. Spend them on rare cosmetics or on "family" items like decorations for the shared campfire. This keeps value meaningful without monetization.
- **Chests.** Either show the contents before opening (preview, then a tap-to-open animation for delight), or guarantee rewards with only a cosmetic variation inside a known tier. Show odds to the 8-year-old in kid language ("1 in 10 chests has a golden hat"). Avoid escalating "near-miss" animations. This follows Brawl Stars' turn toward predictability, and applies Apple's disclosure standard even though nothing is sold.
- **Shop.** Cosmetics only: avatar clothing, pets, campfire decorations, pen colors. Never sell learning power or progress. A small rotating "daily shop" (3 to 4 items, Fortnite and Brawl Stars style) creates freshness. For kids, add a **wishlist and "save for it"** feature so missing a rotation never causes FOMO. Items come back, and a wishlisted item can be pre-saved.
- **Sinks that destroy currency but are kind.**
  - Consumable decorations, such as seasonal campfire skins.
  - Pet food and care with no death mechanic.
  - Crafting that combines common items into rarer ones.
  - Co-op "donations" to the family goal. This sink is pro-social.
- **Inflation control.**
  - Cap daily coin income per child.
  - Scale coin rewards to the child's level, so a 2.5-year-old and an 8.5-year-old earn similar amounts for an age-appropriate effort.
  - Avoid large event injections without a matching event sink.
- **XP levels and skill trees.** XP tracks learning progress, for example a Hebrew letters branch, a numbers branch and a reading branch. A skill tree shows mastery visibly. Keep XP separate from coins so spending never lowers progress.
- **Collectible pets.** Unlocked by curriculum milestones, such as one pet per Hebrew letter group. This doubles as a visible learning map.
- **Financial literacy (ages 5.5 and 8.5).**
  - A savings-jar UI with a progress bar toward a wishlisted item.
  - An optional "interest" bonus for saving (for example 10% after 7 days unspent).
  - A "spend / save / share" split, with share going to the family goal.
  - A visible price comparison.
- **Parent-defined real-world rewards.** A parent-gated "reward store" where the parent sets real rewards ("trip to the park = 500 coins"). This must sit behind a parental gate under Apple 1.3. Keep the ratio of real rewards modest so intrinsic motivation isn't crowded out.
- **For the 2.5-year-old.** No currency arithmetic. Use instant stickers or stamps with no scarcity.

### Gaps
- Could not reach Deconstructor of Fun or Machinations primary articles. The sinks and sources material comes from secondary blogs.
- No sourced specifics gathered on Fortnite Item Shop rotation timing, battle pass tier counts, or season length.
- No peer-reviewed evidence found on teaching financial literacy through in-game economies to children, or on the overjustification effect of real-world rewards in learning apps. These need a follow-up search, for example Deci, Koestner & Ryan's 1999 meta-analysis.
- Official Starr Drop odds were not verified.

## (3) iOS/iPadOS interaction: Pencil and handwriting, voice, touch targets, SharePlay and Game Center, Kids Category rules, and native vs cross-platform tech stack

### Takeaway
- Kids miss touch targets far more often than adults: 46% vs 32% in Anthony et al.'s study. They also produce gestures that recognizers handle poorly, so use large targets, generous hit areas and padding at screen edges.
- Kids Category rules (1.3, 5.1.4) ban links out and purchases outside a parental gate. They also effectively ban third-party analytics and ads.
- SharePlay comes almost free with the Game Center multiplayer UI, but players have to be Game Center friends.
- **Hebrew support is the critical unknown.** Hebrew support was not confirmed for Scribble, PencilKit recognition, or the new iOS 26 SpeechAnalyzer and SpeechTranscriber. The team must test it on a device.
- React Native and Expo RTL is a global, restart-requiring flag. That is a real downside for mixed Hebrew/English UI compared with native SwiftUI.

### Cited Findings

**Children's touch accuracy (Lisa Anthony et al.)**
- On 43 touch targets of varying location and size, children's touches landed outside the target **46%** of the time, against **32%** for adults. The authors concluded that kids need more tolerance for where touches land. — [Anthony et al. JPUC 2013 (search snippet)](https://lisa-anthony.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/04/anthony-et-al-jpuc2013.pdf); [Brown & Anthony CHI 2012](https://lisa-anthony.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/03/brown-and-anthony-chi2012eist.pdf)
- Target sizes tested ran from **0.125" (about 3 mm) to 0.5" (about 12.7 mm)**. The smallest targets were hardest. — [Woodward et al. CHI 2016 slides (snippet)](https://init.cise.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/378/2017/05/Woodward-et-al-CHI2016-presentation-slides.pdf)
- Gesture recognition accuracy was **34% for kids vs 64% for adults** in one condition. Kids draw taller gestures, more slowly, and with less pressure per stroke. — [Lisa Anthony research overview talk](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/research-overviewtalkuidigitalfutures20141107/42404625)
- CHI 2016 follow-up: edge padding affected miss rates, and age and target size were significant factors. — [Woodward et al. CHI 2016](https://init.cise.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/378/2017/05/Woodward-et-al-CHI2016_0.pdf)
- A cross-platform comparison covered children's touch on tablets, tabletops and smartphones. — [INIT Lab: Tablets, Tabletops, and Smartphones](https://init.cise.ufl.edu/?p=1612)
- A "Finger-Fitts" law was tested on 54 children aged 5 to 10 to model children's touchscreen movement time. — [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10175145-examining-fitts-ffitts-law-models-childrens-pointing-tasks-touchscreens)
- The MTAGIC project studied children's multi-touch gestures. — [Anthony et al. IJHCS 2019 preprint](https://init.cise.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/378/2019/03/anthony-et-al-IJHCS2019-MTAGIC-final-preprint.pdf)

**Apple Kids Category and privacy (official guidelines)**
- Guideline 1.3: no links out, purchasing opportunities or distractions outside a parental gate. Kids Category apps may not send personally identifiable or device information to third parties, and "should not include third-party analytics or third-party advertising." There are narrow exceptions: analytics that collect no IDFA and nothing identifying, and contextual ads that get human review. Once in the category, the app must keep meeting the rules even if it later leaves the category. — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Guideline 5.1.4: comply with COPPA, GDPR and other laws. Apps may ask for birthdate and parental contact only for compliance. An app that can share personal information, explicitly including "drawings" and "the ability to chat," must have a privacy policy. The parental gate is "not the same as securing parental consent" for data collection. — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

**Handwriting, Pencil and Scribble**
- Scribble is built on an on-device handwriting recognition engine that Apple calls fast and accurate. — [WWDC20 session 10106](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10106?time=555)
- Accuracy varies by language: a review found English cursive handled well and German less so. — [Galaxus Scribble review](https://www.galaxus.ch/en/page/apple-scribble-review-ai-faces-cursive-16861)
- **Hebrew:** the only Hebrew-specific item found was a 2020 Apple Developer Forums thread *asking* for Hebrew support in Scribble. Current support could not be confirmed. — [Apple Dev Forums thread 650609](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/650609?page=2)

**Speech input**
- iOS 26 adds SpeechAnalyzer and SpeechTranscriber, an on-device stack that supersedes SFSpeechRecognizer. Supported locales are checked through `SpeechTranscriber.supportedLocales`. One write-up says about 40 locales; some need an asset download. DictationTranscriber is the fallback and is said to cover the same languages as SFSpeechRecognizer. — [Anton Gubarenko: iOS 26 SpeechAnalyzer Guide](https://antongubarenko.substack.com/p/ios-26-speechanalyzer-guide); [issoh.co.jp](https://www.issoh.co.jp/tech/details/8929/); [Apple Dev Forums 790108](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/790108)
- **he-IL support in SpeechTranscriber was not confirmed** by any source found.

**SharePlay and Game Center**
- Games using the Game Center multiplayer UI get full SharePlay integration with one small update: add the GroupActivities capability in Xcode. Custom UIs call `startGroupActivity` on the shared matchmaker during a FaceTime call. — [Apple Tech Talk 110338](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/110338)
- SharePlay with Game Center games over FaceTime works on iOS/iPadOS 16.2 and later. Players need to be Game Center friends. Many "SharePlay" games only support screen sharing. — [Gadget Hacks](https://ios.gadgethacks.com/how-to/facetimes-latest-trick-lets-you-play-games-with-family-and-friends-your-iphone-during-calls-heres-works-0385365/)
- GroupActivities docs cover defining activities and promoting SharePlay from the app UI (GroupActivitySharingController). — [Apple: Defining your app's SharePlay activities](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/groupactivities/defining-your-apps-shareplay-activities.md); [Apple: Promoting SharePlay from your UI](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/groupactivities/promoting-shareplay-activities-from-your-apps-ui.md)

**Cross-platform vs native (Hebrew RTL)**
- React Native RTL is a global switch: `I18nManager.forceRTL(true)` flips the whole layout. It needs a full app restart and gives no per-component control, which is a problem for mixed Hebrew and English (LTR) content. A third-party `expo-rtl` library tries to fix this but is new and unvetted. — [DEV: React Native app for 20 languages](https://dev.to/pocket_linguist/building-a-react-native-app-for-20-languages-lessons-in-i18n-2bib); [DEV: Why RN makes you restart to switch language](https://dev.to/ibrahimtarhini01/why-does-react-native-make-you-restart-the-app-just-to-switch-language-i-fixed-it-2pn2)
- 2D casual games are feasible in Expo: one example is an arcade runner built with Expo, Reanimated and NativeWind, using sprite sheets and gestures. — [Pagepro: What if Expo was your next game engine](https://pagepro.co/blog/react-tldr/what-if-expo-was-your-next-mobile-game-engine/)
- As of December 2025 there was no definitive library for embedding Unity in Expo or React Native, so combining them is custom work. — [Zenn: expo-uaal prep](https://zenn.dev/livetoon/articles/expo-uaal-prep)

### Inferences
**Touch targets.** Given 46% child miss rates at targets of 3 to 13 mm:
- Use visual targets of at least about 15 to 20 mm (roughly 60 to 80 pt), comfortably above Apple's 44 pt minimum for adults.
- Make the invisible hit area bigger than the visual.
- Snap drag-and-drop to the nearest valid slot.
- Pad interactive items away from screen edges.
- Accept "close enough" taps for the 2.5-year-old.
- Ignore resting palms and multiple fingers in toddler mode.

**Letter tracing (Hebrew).** Because recognizers do poorly on kids' gestures (34% vs 64%) and Hebrew Scribble is unconfirmed:
- Don't use free-form handwriting recognition for grading.
- Grade with template- or path-based tracing: check the stroke against a known letter path with a tolerance corridor, plus stroke order and direction (Hebrew letters are written right to left).
- Use PencilKit or raw touch and Pencil input for capture, with a wide tolerance and pressure-agnostic scoring.
- Treat Apple's handwriting recognition or Vision text recognition for Hebrew as an optional bonus only after testing on a device.

**Device-specific layouts.**
- iPhone: one-handed, portrait, bottom-third controls for the 8-year-old's solo play and "on the go."
- iPad: landscape, two-handed, room for **same-device co-op**, such as split-screen or "both sides of the table" for siblings. iPad plus Pencil gives the tracing and writing experiences.

**Cross-device family co-op.** SharePlay through Game Center is the cheapest path, but it needs Game Center friends and a FaceTime call. That's awkward for kids under 13 in Family Sharing.
- For in-home sibling play, prefer same-device co-op on an iPad.
- Or use a local, family-scoped sync (CloudKit shared zone keyed to the family), with no chat. Under 5.1.4, chat and drawings sharing trigger privacy duties.

**Analytics.** Use only first-party, non-identifying analytics, because of guideline 1.3. Put the parent dashboard, settings and reward store behind a parental gate.

**Tech stack for an iOS-first, Hebrew-first kids game in 2026.**
- Native SwiftUI plus SpriteKit (or RealityKit for any AR) gives first-class RTL (per-view `layoutDirection`), PencilKit, Core Haptics, GroupActivities, Game Center, Speech and Family Controls with no bridging.
- Expo/React Native is viable for a 2D quiz-style game, but the global RTL flag and the extra bridging for Pencil and SharePlay are real costs.
- Unity is best only if rich 2D/3D game feel matters more than native-API depth.
- A web PWA loses Pencil Scribble integration, haptics, Game Center and SharePlay.
