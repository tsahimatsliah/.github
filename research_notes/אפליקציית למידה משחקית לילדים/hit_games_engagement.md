# Engagement, Progression, Reward and Game-Feel Design in Hit Kids' Games (ages 5-12), and What a Family Learning Game Can Borrow Ethically

Research date: 2026-11-02. Audience for the target product: three Israeli siblings aged 2.5, 5.5 and 8.5 who love Roblox and Brawl Stars.

**Method and source note for the report writer.** In this session, direct page fetches to supercell.com and brawlstars.fandom.com were blocked by the sandbox network allowlist. Facts about those sites come from search-engine snippets and secondary guides (timesaver.gg, trophycoach.com, RoyaleAPI, PocketGamer.biz, etc.). Some long-established design knowledge comes from canonical sources I know but did not re-open in this session: Swink's *Game Feel*, the Jonasson/Purho talk video, Bartle's 1996 paper, Hayashida's kishōtenketsu interview, Quantic Foundry, Nir Eyal's *Hooked*, and the Duolingo/Candy Crush sound design. These are marked **[background, not re-verified this session]**. Treat them as well-known industry knowledge whose exact wording should be checked before quoting.

## 1. Supercell: Brawl Stars, Clash Royale, and the "games people play for years" philosophy

### Takeaway
Supercell's kid-favourite games use short sessions (2-3 minute matches), a long visible progression road (Trophy Road), a seasonal pass, and collectible characters. Between 2022 and 2025 Supercell moved away from classic paid loot boxes and wait-timer chests: Brawl Stars replaced Brawl Boxes with free-to-earn Starr Drops, and Clash Royale removed chest timers and chest keys in March 2025. Randomized reward reveals remain central in both games, though; the randomness was reworked, not removed. The healthiest elements to borrow are the short match, the visible road of milestones, the character roster, and the "tap to reveal" celebration moment. The parts to avoid are paid randomness, FOMO pass timers, and pay-to-progress.

### Cited Findings

**Supercell philosophy**
- Supercell was founded in 2010 to be "the best place for the best people and teams to create the best games." It was partly inspired by World of Warcraft, which the co-founders had played "for years and years" — [Supercell, "10 learnings from 10 years"](https://supercell.com/en/news/10-learnings-10-years) (via search snippet).
- The company is organised into small independent teams called "cells" that act like independent companies. Paananen calls teams of "20 to 35 people" relatively small for the industry and says small teams move faster with "less management and bureaucracy and process", which produces "happier developers and better games" — [GamesBeat interview](https://gamesbeat.com/?p=69936); [PocketGamer.biz, why Supercell built a company of startups](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/why-supercell-built-a-company-of-startups-and-how-it-works).
- The "played for years" bar drives cancellations: "we don't only kill games in beta, we kill good games." Smash Land and Rush Wars are the cited examples — [Supercell 10 learnings](https://supercell.com/en/news/10-learnings-10-years/7436/).
- Paananen: "You can't design fun on a spreadsheet." He also admits people can work at Supercell for years without shipping a game — [GamesBeat DeanBeat: 10 takeaways](https://gamesbeat.com/the-deanbeat-supercell-ceos-10-takeaways-from-10-years-of-mobile-games/2/).

**Brawl Stars: current (2026) systems**
- **Starr Drops / Chaos Drops.** Chaos Drops are an upgraded Starr Drop, described in Supercell's preview as worth "an average of 4x more than a standard Starr Drop." They come from Daily Wins, Trophy Road and the Brawl Pass, and the 6th Daily Win of each day always includes a Chaos Drop. On 25 Feb 2026, rare skins were removed from Epic/Mythic Starr Drops and Chaos Drops. Sources disagree on Chaos Drop launch timing (fan guides say December 2025) — [Supercell blog via search](https://supercell.com/en/games/brawlstars/blog/news/new-power-brawl-pass-changes-and-a-new-starr-drop-2/); [timesaver.gg Starr Drops July 2026](https://timesaver.gg/blog/brawl-stars-starr-drops-july-2026).
- **Brawl Pass (January 2026 rework).** 60 tiers instead of 50. Price is $8.99 (Brawl Pass) and $12.99 (Brawl Pass Plus), with payouts in "Vault Keys." The Season 55 free track reportedly includes 18 Starr Drops, 6 Chaos Drops, 1 Legendary Starr Drop and 2 Mega Boxes — [timesaver.gg Brawl Pass guide](https://timesaver.gg/blog/brawl-stars-brawl-pass); [Brawl Pass/2026 wiki](https://brawlstars.fandom.com/wiki/Brawl_Pass/2026).
- **Trophy Road.** Supercell buffed the early Trophy Road "up to 25k trophies for new players so they can catch up better." Milestones include Legendary Starr Drops at 13,000, 18,000 and 23,000 trophies (wiki last updated April 2026) — [Trophy Road wiki](https://brawlstars.fandom.com/wiki/Trophies/Trophy_Road); [Supercell blog via search](https://supercell.com/en/games/brawlstars/blog/news/new-power-brawl-pass-changes-and-a-new-starr-drop-2/).
- **Brawler unlocks (September 2026).** Starr Road was replaced by "Brawler Blast", a shooting-gallery mini-game. Rare and Super Rare brawlers now come from Brawler Blast instead of Trophy Road. The same update buffed 19 brawlers, nerfed 11, and added Mythic brawlers Cosmo and Vince — [timesaver.gg Brawler Blast explained](https://timesaver.gg/blog/brawl-stars-brawler-blast-explained); [trophycoach September 2026 update](https://trophycoach.com/brawl-stars/guides/brawl-stars-september-2026-update).
- Note: "Mega Boxes" appear again as Brawl Pass rewards in 2026, so box-style reveals have partly come back as earned (not directly purchased) rewards — [timesaver.gg Brawl Pass](https://timesaver.gg/blog/brawl-stars-brawl-pass).

**Clash Royale**
- In the March 31, 2025 update, Clash Royale removed chest timers, the Season Shop, Banner Tokens and Chest Keys. Rewards now arrive after each battle instead of through a timed chest queue. Each Chest Key was converted into 5,000 Gold — [EGW News](https://egw.news/gaming/news/26988/clash-royale-march-2025-update-brings-major-change-zXrplBuv1); [RoyaleAPI "RIP chests"](https://royaleapi.com/blog/rip-chests-2025-q1-update); [GamingOnPhone](https://gamingonphone.com/news/clash-royale-bids-goodbye-to-chest-keys-to-be-completely-removed-by-end-of-march-2025).
- The battle reward is a "Lucky Drop" (spin-to-upgrade). In October 2025 it was renamed and reskinned as "Lucky Chests," a "mainly superficial change" aimed at nostalgic players. Rewards are still determined by spins — [MobileMatters](https://mobilematters.gg/news/clash-royale/lucky-chests-replace-lucky-drops); [Fragster October 2025](https://www.fragster.com/clash-royale-october-2025-update-lucky-chests-champion-rework-new-evolutions/).
- Supercell ran a "chest experiment" for King Tower levels 1-9 before the global change — [Supercell CR blog](https://supercell.com/en/games/clashroyale/blog/news/chest-experiment-king-tower-levels-1-9).
- I found no official Supercell rationale text (fetch blocked). Fan sources paraphrase the goal as "rewards instantly, so players don't have to wait on chests" — [EGW News](https://egw.news/gaming/news/26988/clash-royale-march-2025-update-brings-major-change-zXrplBuv1).

**Classic Clash Royale loop [background, not re-verified this session]:** win 3-minute match → earn chest → chest occupies one of 4 slots and unlocks on a real-time timer (3h/8h/12h/24h) → open to get random cards → duplicate cards upgrade card levels → trophies move you up arenas, each unlocking new cards. The 4-slot timer was the famous "appointment" and session-limiting mechanic, and also the monetisation lever (pay gems to skip).

**Brawl Stars core loop [background, not re-verified this session]:** pick brawler → about 2-3 minute 3v3 or solo match (Gem Grab, Showdown, Brawl Ball, etc.) → trophies for that brawler plus Daily Win rewards → Starr Drop reveal (tap repeatedly; rarity can "upgrade" from Rare to Super Rare, Epic, Mythic, Legendary with escalating colour/sound) → coins/power points/gadgets → upgrade brawler → climb Trophy Road. Meta loop: Brawl Pass season (about 1 month), Clubs (social groups with chat and Club League), Ranked, rotating events, collaborations (e.g., licensed seasons), skins, pins (emotes), and sprays.

### Inferences
- Supercell's 2023-2025 shift (no paid boxes, no chest timers) shows that kid-heavy games can stay hugely popular after removing paid gambling-like boxes and wait-to-play timers. That supports a learning game that has none of them.
- The strongest moment-to-moment appeal of a Starr Drop is the **escalating reveal ritual**: tap, shake, colour change, rising pitch, final burst. This can be borrowed without randomness of value. For example, reveal a *guaranteed* new sticker with the same escalating animation, or make the rarity reflect effort ("you got 3 stars → gold reveal").
- The short-match format (2-3 min) maps cleanly onto learning "rounds" of 5-10 questions/tasks for the 5.5- and 8.5-year-olds.
- Trophy Road is a single, linear, always-visible progression track with milestone rewards. It is a very legible structure for children and easy to tie to learning milestones (e.g., "letters learned").
- The Brawl Pass free/paid split, seasonal expiry and daily "6th win" Chaos Drops are retention levers built on FOMO and daily obligation. These are the parts to avoid or soften (see section 7).

### Gaps
- Official Supercell rationale texts (Brawl Stars blog, Clash Royale March 2025 notes) could not be fetched; designer quotes on why boxes and timers were removed are unverified.
- Exact Starr Drop rarity odds for 2026 were not confirmed from a primary source.
- Exact date Brawl Boxes were removed and Starr Drops introduced (my recollection: Brawl Boxes removed around December 2022, Starr Drops introduced 2023) was not verified this session.
- No published data found on Brawl Stars' under-13 player share.

## 2. Roblox: UGC, avatar economy, and why kids love Adopt Me!, Pet Simulator, Grow a Garden, Brookhaven, Steal a Brainrot and obbies

### Takeaway
Roblox's 2025-2026 hits are mostly simple "idle/tycoon + collect + social" loops: plant, wait, harvest, sell, and upgrade in Grow a Garden; collect meme characters, earn passively, and steal or defend in Steal a Brainrot. Live events ("admin abuse" weekends) and meme culture drive them. The pillars kids respond to are avatar self-expression, pets and collections, trading, roleplay with friends, and passive progress that grows while away. Children themselves describe Roblox's paid random items as "literally just child gambling," so the trading/gacha economy is the part to avoid.

### Cited Findings
- **Grow a Garden** reached a Guinness-record 22.3 million concurrent players in August 2025, with typical 2026 daily concurrency around 1.0-1.2M. It is described as a relaxing, progress-driven farming loop — [SteelSeries](https://steelseries.com/en-hk/blog/most-popular-games-in-roblox); [Studiokrew, Top Roblox Games May 2026](https://studiokrew.com/blog/top-roblox-games-may-2026/).
- **Steal a Brainrot** overtook it in September 2025. Peaks are reported between 24.1M and 25.8M depending on the source: [RTC on X](https://x.com/Roblox_RTC/status/1966948186653863995?lang=en) reports 24,136,040, [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/robloxs-steal-a-brainrot-becomes-first-game-to-surpass-25m-concurrent-players/) reports 25M+, and Studiokrew cites 25.8M (October 2025). Its loop is "a simple tycoon loop with low-stakes player-versus-player stealing, layered on a strong meme cycle" (Italian-brainrot meme characters) — [Studiokrew](https://studiokrew.com/blog/top-roblox-games-may-2026/); [Wikipedia: Steal a Brainrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot).
- Roblox as a platform peaked at **47.4 million concurrent users** in August 2025, driven by the two games competing. Weekend "Admin Abuse" live events, where developers trigger rare spawns and weather in real time, drove the spikes — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/08/25/roblox-grow-garden-steal-brainrot-admin-war-record/); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/robloxs-peak-concurrent-user-count-hits-record-474m-as-steal-a-brainrot-and-grow-a-garden-compete/).
- The garden and brainrot games set "new records in 2025 with unprecedented player engagement" — [mi-3](https://www.mi-3.com.au/17-12-2025/roblox-garden-brainrot-games-set-new-records-2025-unprecedented-player-engagement).
- **Children's own view of Roblox monetisation.** A UK study of 22 children aged 7-14 found many described random paid rewards as "literally just child gambling," and reported confusing transactions and refund policies on Roblox. The authors argue monetised random rewards are inappropriate in children's games, but do not call for banning monetisation outright — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/literally-just-child-gambling-what-kids-say-about-roblox-lootboxes-and-money-in-online-games-250387).

**Game-by-game loops [background, not re-verified this session]:**
- **Adopt Me!**: hatch eggs → raise pets through life stages (newborn to full grown) by fulfilling "needs" (hungry, sleepy, dirty), Tamagotchi-style → combine 4 full-grown pets into a "Neon" and 4 Neons into a "Mega Neon" (a deterministic merge/evolution ladder) → decorate a house → roleplay family → trade pets. Rarity tiers: common, uncommon, rare, ultra-rare, legendary. Trading pressure and scams are widely reported.
- **Pet Simulator (X/99)**: click/auto-farm coins → buy eggs → random pet hatches with rarity → stronger pets farm faster → new zones unlock. A clear "number goes up" idle loop with heavy gacha. "Huge" pets are rare tradeable status items.
- **Brookhaven RP**: a pure social roleplay sandbox (houses, cars, jobs, families) with almost no progression. Its appeal is pretend-play with friends.
- **Obbies** (obstacle courses): stage-numbered checkpoints, instant respawn, a visible "Stage 47/100" counter, and difficulty colour bands. Easy to understand and great for the 5-8 age range.
- **Avatar economy**: Robux buys clothing, accessories and "limited" items. Avatar identity is a key self-expression and social-status driver; "default avatar" (noob) shaming exists among kids.
- **Platform safety (2025-2026)**: Roblox rolled out age estimation/age checks for chat features in late 2025 and early 2026 amid lawsuits and regulatory pressure. Details not verified this session.

### Inferences
- The specific appeals for the 5.5- and 8.5-year-olds are: (a) things that **grow while you're away** (garden, pet), (b) **collections with visible tiers**, (c) **silly meme characters** (brainrot humor = absurd, gross, nonsense names), (d) **self-expression through an avatar**, and (e) **doing it together with siblings/friends**.
- Grow a Garden's "plant now, harvest later" loop can be re-skinned ethically: learning effort plants seeds that grow over real time. Harvest must stay **deterministic** and capped, with no paid speed-ups. Plants must never wither as punishment, to avoid guilt-based return pressure.
- Adopt Me's Neon merge ladder (4 → 1) is a deterministic, fair collection-evolution mechanic. It maps well onto "practice the same skill 4 times → evolved badge."
- The "steal" PvP and open trading economies are not suitable for a family learning game. They bring scam/fairness/conflict risks and teach value-by-scarcity.
- Siblings at three ages favour a Brookhaven-style **shared space** (a family home/island all three kids decorate) over competitive ranking.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed data found on the age distribution of Grow a Garden or Steal a Brainrot players.
- Roblox's 2025-2026 safety and age-verification changes and its paid-random-item policy were not verified from Roblox's own docs this session.
- No reliable data found on Israeli children's Roblox usage specifically.

## 3. Other hit games: Minecraft, Pokémon, Animal Crossing, Nintendo/Mario, Fortnite, Subway Surfers, Among Us, Stumble Guys, Candy Crush, Tamagotchi, Webkinz/Club Penguin, and gacha (Genshin) as a warning

### Takeaway
Across these games, the most durable kid-pleasers are creation (Minecraft), collecting plus evolution (Pokémon), a gentle real-time daily rhythm without punishment (Animal Crossing), wordless "teaching through play" level design (Nintendo), expressive emotes and cosmetics (Fortnite), instant-restart micro-sessions (Subway Surfers, Stumble Guys), a visible level map with heavy "juice" (Candy Crush), and caring for a creature (Tamagotchi, Webkinz). Each also has a darker side to avoid: Candy Crush's lives/boosters, Fortnite's FOMO battle-pass and item-shop rotations, Tamagotchi's guilt/death, and Genshin's gacha "pity" systems.

### Cited Findings
- **Animal Crossing.** The series uses the console's real-time clock, and time passes in the game whether or not it is played. It originated on the 64DD; because the N64 lacked a clock, a battery-backed clock was put in the cartridge — [Wikipedia: Animal Crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_Crossing).
- Eguchi (GDC, Wild World case study): the real-time clock lets players share experiences at the same time and visit friends' towns at different times of day. The team "had to provide some sort of daily stimulus," with examples like digging fossils, checking mail and new store items. Store stock changes daily. He described the game as an intentionally "inconvenient" world where satisfaction is not handed over — [Game Developer: GDC "Is That a Franchise in Your Pocket"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-is-that-a-franchise-in-your-pocket-an-i-animal-crossing-wild-world-i-case-study); [Nintendo World Report](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/news/11224/is-that-a-franchise-in-your-pocket).
- New features were designed to allow "instant change" in a real-time game — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/why-nintendo-s-new-i-animal-crossing-i-features-allow-instant-change-in-the-real-time-game).
- **Nintendo World 1-1.** Shigeru Miyamoto designed World 1-1 as a tutorial. Its "learning through play" philosophy carried into all his later games. It teaches jumps, hazards and power-ups without a single line of text. The first rule it teaches is that progress means moving right. Miyamoto wanted players to "gradually and naturally understand what they're doing." Some pits have floors so a failed jump doesn't kill Mario. It influenced later openings such as Super Mario 3D World's first level — [Wikipedia: World 1-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_1-1); [MCV/Develop: Miyamoto video](https://www.mcvuk.com/development-news/video-miyamoto-shares-his-level-design-secrets/); [AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/shigeru-miyamoto-explains-the-making-of-super-mario-bro-1798284041).
- Iwata Asks on New Super Mario Bros. Wii covers adjusting the world map in a daily cycle and the series' approach to beginners and experts playing together — [Iwata Asks NSMB Wii](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/nsmb/1/4/); [Nintendo UK Iwata Asks vol. 2 part 4](https://nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-New-Super-Mario-Bros-Wii/Volume-2/4-Adjusting-the-Map-in-a-Daily-Cycle/4-Adjusting-the-Map-in-a-Daily-Cycle-233027.html).
- **Kishōtenketsu [background, not re-verified this session]:** Koichi Hayashida (director of Super Mario 3D Land/3D World) described a four-act structure for each level, borrowed from Japanese/Chinese narrative form. **Ki** introduces a mechanic in a safe setting, **Shō** develops it with more challenge, **Ten** adds a twist that recontextualises it, and **Ketsu** concludes, then the mechanic is retired. One gimmick per level, taught then discarded. This came from a 2012 Eurogamer/Gamasutra interview; the specific URL was not retrieved.
- **Gacha.** Systematic review evidence shows a consistent positive association between loot box engagement and gambling tendencies in under-18s, with possible "gateway" effects. Representative longitudinal studies are still needed — [Trends in Psychiatry and Psychotherapy 2025 review](https://trends.org.br/journal/trends/article/doi/10.47626/2237-6089-2025-1217). Several longitudinal studies found young people who bought loot boxes were more likely to gamble later; causation is not proven — [ResearchGate chapter](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384678044_Loot_boxes_and_other_gambling-like_products_inside_video_games).
- Children's accounts show loot boxes normalise gambling-style systems and can cause financial and emotional harm even when kids value the items — [Annals of the AAG / Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/24694452.2023.2248293).
- UK loot box self-regulation on the Apple App Store showed poor compliance; the authors call for legal regulation — [PMC 2025 longitudinal study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12115820/).
- Korean mobile games show deliberate interplay of monetisation and retention strategies — [arXiv 2504.10714](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.10714).

**Per-game loops [background, not re-verified this session]:**
- **Minecraft**: open sandbox. Survival loop: gather → craft → build → survive the night. Creative mode has unlimited blocks. Intrinsic goals; no external progression bar is needed. Minecraft Education adds lessons, worlds, a "Code Builder," chemistry, and teacher tools; the key insight is that *the world is the reward* (building is self-expression).
- **Pokémon**: a "Gotta catch 'em all" Pokédex (a visible, completable collection with silhouettes of the unseen), evolution (a creature you raised visibly transforms, a huge emotional payoff), type-matchup rock-paper-scissors, and trading/link play as a designed social mechanic (version exclusives). Pokémon GO adds real-world walking, egg hatching by distance walked, and community days (time-limited live events).
- **Fortnite**: Battle Pass invented the modern season pass (tiers unlocked by XP, free and premium tracks). Emotes and dances are a central kid-culture element: self-expression, humour, celebration, and "taunting." The Item Shop rotates daily (FOMO). It has 5-minute LEGO/creative modes.
- **Subway Surfers**: endless runner. 3 lanes, swipe controls, instant restart, coin trail "breadcrumbs," a score multiplier through missions, characters/hoverboards, a daily challenge (collect letters), a "World Tour" with themed seasonal city reskins (live-ops), and a "Save me" continue (paid/keys, an ethically grey area).
- **Among Us**: social deduction, short rounds, simple tasks, very low skill floor, crewmate cosmetics (hats/pets), and emergency meetings (talking is the game).
- **Stumble Guys / Fall Guys**: 32-player elimination party races of about 1 minute each, ragdoll slapstick humour (failure is funny, not punishing), and emotes.
- **Candy Crush**: a winding saga **level map** (visible path, friends' faces on the map), extreme juice (cascades, combo voice lines such as "Sweet!", "Tasty!", "Delicious!", "Divine!", "Sugar Crush!" escalating with combo size), special-candy combos, 3-star ratings per level. Its **5 lives regenerating over time**, paid boosters, and "near-miss" fail screens offering 5 more moves for gold are the exploitative parts.
- **Tamagotchi**: needs bars (hunger, happiness), real-time care, evolution based on care quality, and *death/neglect* as a guilt mechanic. Modern reissues soften it (the creature "returns to its planet").
- **Webkinz / Club Penguin** (2005-2017 era kids' MMOs): physical plush toy code → virtual pet, igloo/room decoration, mini-games earning coins (KinzCash), moderated or "safe chat" (pre-selected phrases), membership paywall for clothing/items, and live parties/events (Club Penguin). They demonstrate that **safe-chat menus** and **decorating a personal space** work for ages 6-10.

### Inferences
- **Animal Crossing's model** of a daily "small fresh thing" with no punishment for missing a day, rather than streak loss, fits a family learning app well: a new letter-of-the-day fossil, a daily mail from a character.
- **Pokédex silhouettes** are a powerful, healthy collection device. Showing the shapes of uncollected items gives a clear goal without randomness. Collection slots can map to curriculum items (each Hebrew letter, number, or word family).
- **Evolution through effort** (Pokémon level-up, Adopt Me Neon) is a natural fit for mastery learning: a character visibly evolves when a skill is mastered.
- **World 1-1 and kishōtenketsu** map directly to teaching a new concept per level: introduce safely → practice → twist → conclude, with no text instructions. This matters for the 2.5- and 5.5-year-olds who cannot read yet.
- **Fortnite/Stumble Guys emotes** and **Among Us cosmetics** show that self-expression items alone (no power) satisfy kids. Rewards can be purely cosmetic: hats, dances, stickers.
- **Candy Crush's level map** is a great structure for a curriculum. Its lives system is a hard no, since learning should never be gated by an energy meter.

### Gaps
- Hayashida's kishōtenketsu interview, Minecraft Education feature specifics, and Candy Crush's combo voice-line list were not re-verified this session.
- No sourced data found on Stumble Guys or Subway Surfers player age demographics.

## 4. "Juice" and game feel: animation, sound, music, haptics, and celebration moments

### Takeaway
"Juice" is the excess positive feedback a game returns for small player inputs: screen shake, squash and stretch, particles, tweening, sound and haptics. It turns a mechanically identical game from dead to delightful. For kids, juice should celebrate *effort and correct answers* in proportion to the achievement, rise in pitch and intensity with streaks, stay optional and calm for the youngest child, and never be used to dress up spending or random-value reveals.

### Cited Findings
- **"Juice it or Lose it"** is a talk by Martin Jonasson and Petri Purho (associated with the Nordic Game Conference, 2012). They take a plain block-breaker (Breakout clone) and iteratively add particles, screen shake, sound and animation, showing how each layer transforms the feel — [GameJuice, "What 'Juice it or Lose it' teaches us"](https://gamejuice.pages.dev/articles/juice-it-or-lose-it-lessons); [Kenney, must-see videos](https://kenney.nl/learn/must-see-videos-for-indie-developers). Video [background]: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fy0aCDmgnxg
- **"The Art of Screenshake"** is by Jan Willem Nijman (Vlambeer), 2013, given at INDIGO Classes / Control Conference Amsterdam. It is often mislabelled as GDC. Nijman builds a top-down shooter and progressively adds feedback, with specific numbers for camera kick and shake on firing — [Game Developer: Vlambeer co-founder advice](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/vlambeer-co-founder-shares-advice-on-building-better-action-games); [GameJuice screen shake guide](https://gamejuice.pages.dev/articles/screen-shake-guide). Video [background]: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJdEqssNZ-U
- **Apple HIG, "Playing haptics"**: games commonly use custom haptics to enhance gameplay. Long-running haptics can enhance gameplay but blunt meaning in other apps. Make haptics **optional** and keep the experience enjoyable without them. **Match haptic intensity and sharpness to the accompanying animation and synchronise with sound.** Avoid overuse. iPad itself doesn't play haptics (only connected controllers), and Apple TV uses controllers — [Apple HIG Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics.md); [Apple HIG Game controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls.md); [WWDC19 Introducing Core Haptics](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/520); [WWDC19 Audio-haptic design](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/810).

**Background principles [background, not re-verified this session]:**
- **Steve Swink, *Game Feel* (2008)**: game feel combines real-time control, simulated space, and **polish** (the effects that emphasise interactions without changing the simulation). The core claim is that responsiveness must be under about 100 ms to feel "connected."
- **Juice toolkit** (from the two talks): tweening/easing (no linear motion; ease-out-back and elastic), squash and stretch on impact and jump, **anticipation** (wind-up before action), follow-through/overshoot, particles on hit and collect, screen shake (short, decaying, small amplitude), **hit-stop/freeze frames** (30-100 ms pause on impact), camera kick, flashes (white frame on hit), sound variation (random pitch ±5-10% so repeated sounds don't fatigue), permanence (debris and marks stay), and characters with eyes that react (look at the ball, blink).
- **Disney's 12 principles of animation** (squash and stretch, anticipation, staging, follow-through, slow-in/slow-out, arcs, secondary action, timing, exaggeration, appeal) are the foundation most game juice draws from.
- **Pitch-rising streak sounds**: Candy Crush cascades and Duolingo's correct-answer chime/combo use successive notes rising up a scale as the combo or streak grows. The ear predicts the next note, which builds anticipation, and the streak's end lands on a resolving chord. Duolingo's correct "ding" is short, bright, and major-key.
- **Reward-sound design conventions**: success uses major intervals, ascending, bright timbre (bells, marimba, glockenspiel), short duration (150-400 ms). Error uses a soft, low, short "bonk" or descending two notes; never harsh buzzers for small children. "Collect" uses a coin chime with random pitch. Big moments (level complete) get a 1.5-3 s fanfare with layered percussion and a cymbal swell.
- **Music loops**: seamless 30-90 s loops; intensity layering (add a percussion stem when a timer runs or a streak builds); separate volume sliders; and a quiet mode. Nintendo games use adaptive music, for example Mario's tempo increase when time is low.

### Inferences (design principles for the learning game)
**Animation**
- Every tap gets an immediate (<100 ms) visual response: button squash, a little bounce.
- Correct answers trigger a short **squash-stretch + particle burst + rising chime**. A wrong answer is a gentle wobble ("no-no" head shake) with no red X flashes for the 2.5-year-old.
- Use **anticipation** before reveals: a chest/egg wobbles 2-3 times, the wobble grows, then it bursts. This is the Starr Drop/Pokémon-egg ritual, and it works without random value.
- Characters have eyes and react to the child: look at the touch point, cheer, blink, giggle. Personality is juice.

**Sound**
- Build a **streak ladder**: each consecutive correct answer plays the next note of a major pentatonic scale (it can't sound "wrong"), then the streak completion lands a resolving chord plus a voice line. Use Hebrew voice lines (e.g., "!יופי", "!מדהים", "!אלוף") escalating like Candy Crush's "Sweet/Tasty/Delicious/Divine."
- Never punish a broken streak with a sad sound. Just quietly reset the ladder.
- Randomise pitch slightly on repeated sounds to avoid fatigue.
- Keep the music loop gentle and low-tempo for the toddler mode, with a separate music mute. Many parents will mute music while keeping SFX.

**Haptics (per Apple HIG)**
- Use light, sharp "tick" haptics on taps, a medium double-tap on correct answers, and a short rumble crescendo on big celebrations. Always sync with the sound and animation.
- Provide a haptics toggle. Do not rely on haptics on iPad, since only connected controllers play them.

**Celebration moments (proportional, tiered)**
- **Micro** (per answer, <0.5 s): chime + sparkle.
- **Small** (end of round, ~1.5 s): stars fly into a counter one by one with ascending notes. This is Candy Crush's 1-2-3-star tally.
- **Medium** (new item/sticker, 2-3 s): anticipation wobble, burst, item spins into the album slot, slot "clicks" with satisfying haptic.
- **Large** (milestone, mastery, evolution; 4-6 s, rare): full-screen confetti, character dance or emote, fanfare, and a family-shareable moment (show a parent).
- Make celebrations **skippable** after first viewing, and scale screen shake way down or off for the youngest. Big flashing also has photosensitivity implications.

### Gaps
- Exact Apple HIG wording beyond the snippets was not retrieved; the iPad haptic limitation should be double-checked against the live page.
- No primary source retrieved for Duolingo's specific sound-design process. I recall Duolingo has published about its sound and character animation (Rive), but did not verify it.
- No research found on optimal celebration duration for ages 2-8.

## 5. Player motivation frameworks (Bartle, Quantic Foundry, Octalysis, Hook model) and their ethical critique

### Takeaway
These frameworks are useful vocabulary for balancing what a game offers (achievement, exploration, social, creativity, mastery), but several are explicitly built to maximise engagement or habit, not wellbeing. Octalysis's own "Black Hat" drives (scarcity, unpredictability, loss avoidance) and the Hook model's "variable reward" step are exactly what research on apps for young children flags as manipulative. A children's learning game should design almost exclusively from "White Hat" / intrinsic drives.

### Cited Findings
- **Octalysis** (Yu-kai Chou) maps engagement to 8 core drives. The top drives feel empowering ("White Hat"), the bottom feel pressuring ("Black Hat"); left-brain drives are extrinsic, right-brain intrinsic — [Yu-kai Chou, Octalysis framework](https://yukaichou.com/gamification-examples/octalysis-complete-gamification-framework/); [Medium, Chou](https://medium.com/@yukaichou/the-octalysis-framework-for-gamification-behavioral-design-fe381150f0c1).
- Chou frames white/black as tone, "not a moral compass." Secondary sources caution that Black Hat drives can create short-term engagement but lead to manipulative design, burnout and negative feelings — [Octalysis definition, UZH GBL](https://www.gbl.uzh.ch/quartz/definitions/Octalysis-Framework); [Nudge blog](https://nudgenow.com/blogs/gamification-behavioral-design-octalysis-framework).
- **Manipulative design in young children's apps**: Radesky et al. (JAMA Network Open, 2022) analysed 160 apps used by 3-5-year-olds. Only 20% had no manipulative design features, and nearly 99% of children had at least one manipulative design in a top-used app. Children from lower-SES households saw more of it. Tactics included fabricated countdown timers, pop-up roadblock ads, lures like stickers or trophies, and characters nagging idle kids ("Don't just stand there, do something!"). Manipulative design is defined as nudging the user "in a way that meets the technology developer's best interests – at the expense of the user's interests." Young children can't distinguish selling screens from gameplay — [Deceptive.design summary](https://deceptive.design/articles/prevalence-and-characteristics-of-manipulative-design-in-mobile-applications-used-by-children); [Michigan Medicine](https://michiganmedicine.org/health-lab/design-tricks-commonly-used-monetize-young-childrens-app-use); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/956153); [Radesky Vermont Kids Code testimony 2024](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2024/WorkGroups/Senate%20Economic%20Development/Bills/S.289/Witness%20Documents/S.289~Jenny%20Radesky~Kids%20Code%20Testimony~3-1-2024.pdf).

**Framework details [background, not re-verified this session]:**
- **Octalysis 8 drives**: (1) Epic Meaning & Calling, (2) Development & Accomplishment, (3) Empowerment of Creativity & Feedback, (4) Ownership & Possession, (5) Social Influence & Relatedness, (6) Scarcity & Impatience, (7) Unpredictability & Curiosity, (8) Loss & Avoidance. White Hat = 1, 2, 3; Black Hat = 6, 7, 8. Left/extrinsic = 2, 4, 6; right/intrinsic = 3, 5, 7.
- **Bartle (1996, "Hearts, Clubs, Diamonds, Spades")**: Achievers, Explorers, Socializers and Killers, derived from MUD players. It is often criticised as non-empirical and adult/MMO-specific — https://mud.co.uk/richard/hcds.htm
- **Quantic Foundry Gamer Motivation Model** (Nick Yee): 12 motivations in 6 pairs (Action: Destruction/Excitement; Social: Competition/Community; Mastery: Challenge/Strategy; Achievement: Completion/Power; Immersion: Fantasy/Story; Creativity: Design/Discovery). It is empirically derived from survey data of 1.25M+ gamers, mostly adults/teens — https://quanticfoundry.com/
- **Hook model (Nir Eyal, *Hooked*, 2014)**: Trigger → Action → Variable Reward (tribe/hunt/self) → Investment. Eyal's own "Manipulation Matrix" asks whether the maker would use it and whether it materially improves users' lives. The ethical critique is that variable rewards are the slot-machine schedule (variable-ratio reinforcement), and external triggers such as push notifications target children who lack self-regulation. Eyal later wrote *Indistractable* (2019) about resisting such designs.
- **Self-Determination Theory** (Deci & Ryan), widely used in games research (Rigby & Ryan's PENS: competence, autonomy, relatedness), is the most child-ethical frame: intrinsic motivation from competence, autonomy and relatedness. Extrinsic rewards can undermine intrinsic motivation (the "overjustification effect").

### Inferences
- Use **SDT** as the primary frame, with Octalysis drives 1-5 as the toolbox: meaning ("help the dragon learn to read"), accomplishment (mastery map), creativity (build/decorate), ownership (collection, a pet that is *yours*), and relatedness (siblings and parents).
- Use **drive 7 (curiosity)** carefully, only as *surprise of content* (what's behind the door, a funny animation) and never as *variable value* (random rarity tied to spending or to a chance of getting nothing).
- **Avoid drives 6 and 8** entirely: no timers, limited-time offers, streak-loss threats, dying pets, or "you'll lose your progress."
- The Hook model's **"Investment"** step (kid builds a garden or house that accrues meaning) is healthy. **External triggers** (push notifications aimed at kids) and **variable rewards** are the parts to drop. Use parent-facing reminders instead.
- Radesky's tactics list works as a concrete **"never do" checklist**: fake timers, roadblock interstitials, idle-nagging characters, rewards dangled to prolong play, purchase screens disguised as gameplay.

### Gaps
- Primary texts (Bartle paper, Quantic Foundry site, Eyal's book) were not re-opened this session; details are from background knowledge.
- No study found validating Bartle or Quantic Foundry types in children under 10.

## 6. What children aged 5-10 specifically enjoy, and adapting it to three ages (2.5, 5.5, 8.5)

### Takeaway
Across the hit games, the recurring child appeals are pets and companions to care for, customising an avatar or space, surprise reveals, collecting complete sets, building, feeling powerful, slapstick and gross-out humour, and silly characters. Their prominence follows the 2025 Roblox hits (pets in Adopt Me, gardens, brainrot memes) and Brawl Stars (strong, funny characters, emotes). For a mixed-age sibling group, the design needs layered difficulty and a shared world.

### Cited Findings
- Steal a Brainrot's success rests on absurd meme characters ("a strong meme cycle") plus a simple tycoon loop — [Studiokrew](https://studiokrew.com/blog/top-roblox-games-may-2026/).
- Grow a Garden's relaxing grow-and-collect progression drew 22.3M concurrent players — [SteelSeries](https://steelseries.com/en-hk/blog/most-popular-games-in-roblox).
- Children aged 7-14 value the items they win but recognise and resent gambling-style mechanics ("literally just child gambling") — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/literally-just-child-gambling-what-kids-say-about-roblox-lootboxes-and-money-in-online-games-250387).
- Young children (3-5) cannot reliably tell selling screens from gameplay, so lures like stickers and trophies are especially powerful for them, and especially open to abuse — [Michigan Medicine](https://michiganmedicine.org/health-lab/design-tricks-commonly-used-monetize-young-childrens-app-use).
- World 1-1 shows wordless teaching works: mechanics are learned without text — [Wikipedia: World 1-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_1-1).

### Inferences (age-layered design)
- **Age 2.5 (pre-verbal or emerging speech, no reading)**: big touch targets, cause-and-effect toys (tap → animal makes sound and bounces), no fail states, no timers, no text, voice and visual only. Use a single companion pet that reacts lovingly. Rewards are immediate animation and sound, with collections of 3-5 items at most. Sessions are short (a few minutes) and parent co-play is expected. No haptic-heavy or flashing effects.
- **Age 5.5 (pre-reader/early reader, Hebrew letters and numbers)**: World 1-1-style wordless levels, a sticker-album collection (Pokédex silhouettes), a pet that evolves with mastery, avatar dress-up, 3-star ratings, silly humour (burps, funny falls, a goofy villain), and short "rounds" like Brawl Stars matches (2-3 min). Use a visible linear road (Trophy Road / Candy Crush map).
- **Age 8.5 (reader, wants to feel strong and skilful, Brawl Stars and Roblox fan)**: harder challenge modes, power fantasy ("your brawler gets stronger as you master multiplication"), streak combos with rising sounds, emotes, a cosmetic-only "season" track with **no expiry** and no paid tier, building/decorating (Minecraft/Brookhaven-like home), and the ability to **teach or help** younger siblings (a role reward).
- **Sibling/family layer**: a shared island or home all three decorate (Animal Crossing / Club Penguin igloo), co-op "family quests" where each child's age-appropriate task contributes (Nintendo NSMB Wii "beginners and experts together"), and parent celebration moments ("show Abba/Ima"). Avoid sibling leaderboards; they pit the 8.5-year-old against the 2.5-year-old.
- **Humour**: silly characters with eyes, slapstick fails (Stumble Guys ragdoll), funny names, and mild gross-out (burps, farts, a character who sneezes confetti). These cost almost nothing and are very high-value for 5-10s.
- **Being strong**: let the child's character visibly power up through learning (bigger, glowing, new ability). This is the Brawl Stars upgrade fantasy without pay-to-win.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study in this session specifically ranking game-feature preferences of 5-10-year-olds (pets vs. building vs. humour). The list above is inferred from what the hits share. A dedicated child-development search (e.g., Joan Ganz Cooney Center, Common Sense Media) is advised.
- No Hebrew/Israeli-specific research on children's game preferences was found.

## 7. Exploitative (avoid) vs healthy (borrow): a synthesis matrix

### Takeaway
Most "fun" mechanics in hit kids' games are separable from their monetisation layer. The reveal ritual, collection, progression road, pet care, short rounds, cosmetics and celebration juice are healthy and highly effective. The exploitative layer is paid or random value, artificial scarcity and timers, loss and guilt mechanics, social pressure, and spending disguised as play. It can be stripped out completely without losing the core appeal, as Supercell's own removal of paid boxes and chest timers suggests.

### Cited Findings
- Supercell removed chest timers, chest keys and Season Shop from Clash Royale (March 2025), and replaced paid Brawl Boxes with earned Starr Drops. Both games remain top kid titles — [EGW News](https://egw.news/gaming/news/26988/clash-royale-march-2025-update-brings-major-change-zXrplBuv1); [timesaver.gg](https://timesaver.gg/blog/brawl-stars-starr-drops-july-2026).
- Paid random items are linked to gambling tendencies in minors (consistent association, causation not proven) — [Trends in Psychiatry 2025](https://trends.org.br/journal/trends/article/doi/10.47626/2237-6089-2025-1217); [Taylor & Francis 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/24694452.2023.2248293).
- Fake timers, roadblock ads, sticker/trophy lures to prolong play, and idle-nagging characters are common in preschool apps and are classified as manipulative — [Michigan Medicine / Radesky 2022](https://michiganmedicine.org/health-lab/design-tricks-commonly-used-monetize-young-childrens-app-use).
- Octalysis's Black Hat drives (scarcity, unpredictability, loss avoidance) are flagged as producing burnout and negative feelings — [Nudge](https://nudgenow.com/blogs/gamification-behavioral-design-octalysis-framework).
- Animal Crossing's daily stimulus (fossils, mail, store stock) provides a reason to return through novelty, not threat — [Game Developer GDC](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-is-that-a-franchise-in-your-pocket-an-i-animal-crossing-wild-world-i-case-study).

### Inferences: the matrix

| Mechanic (source game) | Borrow (healthy version) | Avoid (exploitative version) |
|---|---|---|
| Starr Drop / egg / chest reveal (Brawl Stars, Adopt Me, Pokémon) | Escalating anticipation animation for a **guaranteed** earned item; rarity reflects effort/mastery | Random value, paid drops, "near-miss" upgrades, odds |
| Trophy Road / level map (Brawl Stars, Candy Crush) | Linear visible curriculum map with milestone gifts; friends' or siblings' avatars on map | Gating levels behind lives/energy; pay to skip |
| Season/Battle Pass (Brawl Stars, Fortnite) | "Adventure album" of themed chapters that **never expire**; all free | Monthly expiry, premium track, FOMO countdowns |
| Chest timers / energy (Clash Royale, Candy Crush) | Optional gentle session-end ("the pet is sleepy, see you tomorrow"), with parent-set limits | Wait-or-pay timers, regenerating lives |
| Daily wins / login rewards (Brawl Stars 6th-win Chaos Drop) | Animal Crossing-style daily surprise (new mail, new fossil) with **no streak loss** | Streak breaks, escalating daily obligations, "don't lose your streak!" |
| Collection (Pokédex, Pet Sim, Adopt Me) | Sticker album with silhouettes, mapped to curriculum items; duplicates merge (4→1 Neon) | Gacha with duplicates as currency sink; tradeable scarcity |
| Pets/companions (Tamagotchi, Adopt Me, Webkinz) | Pet that grows and evolves with learning, reacts lovingly, never dies or gets sick from absence | Guilt, neglect death, sad pet notifications |
| Idle growth (Grow a Garden, Pet Sim) | Garden grows from effort; harvest once you return, capped | Pay to speed growth, offline earnings designed to compel returns |
| Customization/avatar (Roblox, Fortnite) | Earned cosmetics: hats, colours, emotes, house decor | Paid cosmetics, status signalling, "default" shaming |
| Emotes/celebration (Fortnite, Brawl Stars pins) | Unlockable dances or emotes the child performs after success | Paid emotes |
| Short matches (Brawl Stars, Stumble Guys) | 2-3 min learning rounds with clear end | "One more" autoplay chains, no natural stopping points |
| Social (Clubs, Roblox, Among Us) | Sibling co-op, family quests, parent "show-off" screen, safe-chat-like presets | Open chat, trading with strangers, sibling leaderboards, PvP stealing |
| Live-ops/events (Admin Abuse, Pokémon Community Day, Subway Surfers World Tour) | Holiday-themed reskins (Hanukkah, Purim costumes, Pesach) that stay available | Limited-time exclusive items that vanish |
| Juice (all) | Proportional celebrations, rising-pitch streaks, gentle error feedback | Juice on purchase buttons; celebrating spending; overstimulating flashes for toddlers |
| Idle prompts | Quiet idle animation (pet naps) | Nagging characters ("Don't just stand there!") |

**Regulatory note [background, not re-verified this session]:** PEGI announced in 2025 that games with paid random items would receive higher age ratings (reportedly PEGI 16) starting in 2026. Belgium treats paid loot boxes as illegal gambling (2018). Avoiding paid randomness entirely future-proofs the product.

### Gaps
- PEGI 2026 rating change and Israeli regulation on loot boxes/children's apps were not verified this session; worth a targeted check.
- No empirical study found that measures learning outcomes from specific juice/celebration intensities in educational games for this age range.
