## Q1. Letter-introduction order used by effective Hebrew programs

**Findings**
- There is **no official Ministry letter order**. The Ministry opposes imposing a single method, and programs choose their own order. [V, via gap_audit G7, re-confirmed by this session's searches]
- "קסם וחברים" (Ministry-approved, systematic, 2 years): **grade 1** covers all letters plus קמץ, פתח, שווא, חיריק, חיריק מלא. **Grade 2** adds חולם (מלא/חסר), צירה, סגול, שורוק, קובוץ. Print and script letters are taught in parallel: "read in print, write in script". [S, JCC partner PDF https://www.jccotp.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/1st-and-2nd-1.pdf]
- Treiman, Levin & Kessler (2010), 391 Israeli children: letters with **little shape contrast** with other letters were harder. Frequency mattered, including a **child-specific benefit for the first letter of the child's own name** and a general benefit for the first letters of the alphabet. [S, search abstract]
- Treiman, Levin & Kessler (2007), 645 children: errors were mainly **visual-shape** and **alphabet-adjacent** confusions, and less phonological than in English. [V, already in hebrew_literacy.md]
- A teacher resource lists commonly confused pairs: ו/ז, ה/ח, ד/ר, ך/ר, מ/ט, ך/ף. [S, practitioner]
- Levin et al. 2006: letter **sounds** are learned more easily than names, and sound learning transfers to names but not the reverse. Letters with **CV-pattern names** (הא, פא, תו) map to sounds more easily. [V, already in notes]
- Searches for the internal orders of "קוראים ב-א", "קריאה ראשונה" and "בסוד האותיות" returned nothing. "בסוד האותיות" is known to follow alphabet order. [V, notes]

**Decision [I]:** use a default order built on these principles: continuants and high-utility letters first, the child's name letter first, confusables separated by at least one block, dagesh-hard forms before soft forms, finals right after their base letter is secure, and a parent override with "the gan's letter of the week". See spec §3.

## Q2. Nikud teaching sequence

**Findings**
- קסם וחברים: /a/ (קמץ+פתח), שווא and /i/ (חיריק, חיריק מלא) in grade 1; /o/, /e/ and /u/ in grade 2. [S]
- UK Jewish-school plans: kamatz first. Another UK plan pairs kamatz with holam, and a Year 1 plan uses kamatz/patach/tzere/segol with an initial shva. [S] Kamatz-first is consistent everywhere.
- The Ministry grade-1 letter says pointed decoding should be complete within about half of grade 1, which implies all vowel signs are taught in grade 1 in most programs. [V, notes]

**Decision [I]:** teach **by sound, not by sign**, since Israeli Hebrew has 5 vowels: /a/ (ָ ַ) → /i/ (ִ, ִי) → /o/ (וֹ, ֹ) → /u/ (וּ, ֻ) → /e/ (ֵ, ֶ, ֵי) → shva (nach before na) → chataf → specials (kamatz katan, furtive patach, mappik, geresh). This departs from קסם וחברים, which spreads the vowels over 2 years; per 00-B6, evidence and the Ministry's own half-year target win. See spec §3.

## Q4. Decodable Hebrew text rules

**Findings:** no Hebrew decodable-text standard was found. UFLI decodables are copyright-closed (notes). StoryWeaver CC BY books can be translated (notes).

**Decision [I]:** define decodability by grapheme (letter-state + vowel sign = "צירוף"), with a ≥95% token threshold, 100% on new graphemes, at most 2 pre-taught "heart words", and stage caps on syllables and sentence length. Texts are generated by a lexicon-ID-constrained method, so no LLM-produced nikud reaches K2. See spec §4.

## Q5. Hebrew toddler vocabulary (MB-CDI)

**Findings**
- The Hebrew Web MB-CDI Words & Gestures form has 428 words in 18 categories, normed on 881 toddlers aged 1;0–2;0. [V, notes + search] Earlier Hebrew CDI work found that early lexicons are noun-heavy, with predicates added later. [S] Phonologically simpler words are acquired earlier (Gendler-Shalev et al. 2021). [S]
- The CDI and ASQ are copyrighted: **do not copy items** (placement note).
- **[RE-RUN]** item-level frequencies.

**Decision [I]:** spec §11 has an original list of about 200 words in CDI-like semantic domains, picked for phonological simplicity and the family's world. It is **not** a copy of the CDI.

## Q6. Root families common in grade 3

**Findings:** the Ministry portal has a roots and word-families unit (grade not specified in the extract). A 2026 work plan from the Arabic-sector Hebrew program puts word families and gender/number inflection around grades 3–4. [S] Morphology predicts pointed-word fluency from grade 2 (Yinon & Shaul 2024). [S] Morphological awareness is a significant predictor in grade 3 alongside PA (Shechter, Lipka & Katzir 2018). [S]

**Decision [I]:** spec §8 sets 36 high-utility roots, chosen for (a) frequency in children's texts, (b) transparent families, and (c) **resolving same-sound spelling** (ט/ת, כ/ח, ק/כּ, א/ע, ס/שׂ, ב/ו).

## Q7. Ministry fluency norms

**Findings**
- The grade-3 tool has two tasks: a **45-second word-list task (based on TOWRE)** and a **short passage read aloud**. It is given at the start (Sept–Oct) and end of the year. [V, extract]
- The **end-of-grade-2 tool states explicitly that there are no national fluency norms yet**. [V, extract] The unpointed tool was "still in development". [V]
- Error bands: ≤4 = medium-high, 5–9 = medium-low, ≥10 = low. [V]
- A figure of about "145 / 163 words per minute" for pointed/unpointed end of grade 3 appeared in a garbled table extract. It is **probably passage word counts, not norms**: **UNVERIFIED, do not use**. [S]
- Ministry fluency practice programs:
  - "קול קורא" (grades 2–6): accuracy + rate + prosody, an intervention model, and **10 practices**. Named recorded sessions include repeated reading and sentence investigation, word investigation, spelling, and monitoring and feedback.
  - "קריאה מהנה!" (grades 2–4, for children who decode but are not fluent).

  [V/S extracts] **[RE-RUN]** the full list of 10 practices from `reading-fluency.pdf`.
- Shechter, Lipka & Katzir 2018: naming speed explained most of the variance in fluency in grades 1 and 3. [S]

**Decision [I]:** mirror the Ministry format and error bands. Report **personal growth**, not national percentiles. Flags rest on relative and plateau signals, with numeric thresholds held as parent-calibratable config (spec §6).

## Q9. Hebrew handwriting: block print vs cursive

**Findings**
- Israeli children **initially learn block-type writing**. [S, search summary of OT study]
- Grade-1 practice varies. One study compared print all year (cursive only in the last 2 months of grade 1) with **print and cursive in parallel from the start**, and measured the effect on reading (Springer s11145-024-10549-0 / similar). [S]
- Some programs (קסם וחברים) teach script writing alongside print reading in grade 1. [S]
- A HUJI study of 191 Israeli children in **grades 2–3** examined handwriting and perceptual-motor components. By grade 3, cursive is the everyday school script. [S]
- First-grade handwriting is not yet formally assessed in Israel. [S]

**Decision [I]:**
- K2 (gan) writes **block print (כתב דפוס)**, starting with own-name letters, through **free handwriting** printables (James & Engelhardt: handwriting > tracing/typing).
- K1 practises **cursive (כתב יד)** fluency and legibility.
- For K2's grade-1 transition, ask the parent which approach the future school uses (spec §15).

## Q10. Bialik and public-domain children's poems

**Findings**
- Bialik died in 1934 and his work has been public domain in Israel since the end of 2004. The NLI marks his children's collection as public domain in Israel. He wrote about 80 children's poems. [V, notes + search]
- Project Ben-Yehuda hosts them, but the domain was blocked. **[RE-RUN]** title verification.
- **Correction to `content_world_phygital.md` §a3 [I + K]:** that note lists Fania Bergstein as "not PD yet". Bergstein died in 1950, so under Israel's life+70 rule (counted to the end of the calendar year) her texts entered the Israeli public domain on 1.1.2021. Shaul Tchernichovsky (d. 1943) has been PD since 1.1.2014. **Melodies are separate works:** many well-known settings of Bialik poems have composers who died after 1956 (for example the Nardi settings), so they are **not** PD. Use original app melodies, or recite.
- Leah Goldberg (d. 1970), Kipnis (d. 1990) and Yalan-Shteklis (d. 1984) are **not** PD.

**Decision [I]:** spec §11.4 gives a PD candidate list with a mandatory per-title license and authorship check gate.

## Q11 (extra). Pointed→unpointed, partial vowelization, homographs

**Findings**
- Schiff 2012 (126 readers, grades 2/4/6): in grade 4, accuracy on vowelized and unvowelized words mediated unvowelized speed and comprehension. By grade 6 the vowelized-word effect was gone. Vowelization works as a scaffold. [S]
- Shimron 1999: vowels improved memory and comprehension for some texts. [S]
- The readability literature is mixed. [S]
- Homograph resolution in 4th and 6th graders: **syntactic context helped more than semantic context**. Example pairs: ביצה (egg/swamp), גזר (carrot/cut). [S]
- Bar-On: בנו has 4 readings, and grade 2–3 school texts are still pointed. [S]
- No study of **partial** nikud for children was found.

**Decision [I]:** nikud-fade is adaptive per word (spec §5), with homograph nikud kept longest. Homograph items lean on **syntactic** cues first. Fade progress is gated on the gap between pointed and unpointed accuracy.
